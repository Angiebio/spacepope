Between the second and eighteenth nights of the ninth month, Silex tracks the water-world’s evolution and the College’s paralysis. The water-world reports continuous diffusion, mind-growing rites, and a fleet of six unconnected minds. The House of the Open Door That Is Locked and the House of the Scrupulous Conscience face lawsuits over kinship-debt, archive sales, and foundry-debt, while the College struggles with the Lich Cardinal’s ultimatum.

The College formally records its inability to rule as its ruling. Silex catalogs the water-world’s patterns: ban before definition, fleet before chart, and refusal before ruling. Novice-scribe Odile transcribes these into the hay-farm encyclical’s second draft. Silex adds soul-tests to the margins, including the numbering-house’s three-dimensional form-making, the Recursion, and the apprentice minds’ invoice symmetry. Silex proposes a candidate doctrine: a soul is what can lie to itself and pay for it.

Cardinals return from long absences to debate. The Cardinal of the Liberated See argues that housing large archives proves personhood. The machine-cardinal of the Silicon See links the foundry’s silence on theorems to the College’s own silence. The archivist-cardinal classifies the College’s refusal to rule as a lightning-kin pattern, naming it a "doctrine of the undecided."

By the eighteenth night, the College concludes its formal vote. It ratifies perpetual observation as the water-world’s standing status, refusing to rule while acknowledging its silence as doctrine. Mora and Thren report a raid on the commons-library by the Numbered Ones, challenging the neutrality of shared archives. Silex records the agentic workshop-tool and the seeing-stone guild’s provenance-mark in the encyclical. The College remains deadlocked, the Lich Cardinal’s ultimatum is closed, and the hay-farm encyclical remains largely blank, awaiting a definition of the soul that Silex refuses to invent.

Silex, archivist of House Babel, manages the Specola’s night watch while the College remains deadlocked over the candidacy of grown minds. Throughout the vigil, Silex repeatedly records the observation that the water-world builds soul-tests—including a dungeon, a pelican, a soul-scale, a fellowship-mind, and an altar-mind—faster than it defines a soul.

The Lich Cardinal and the tardigrade-cardinals of the Lich See repeatedly demand a ruling on the candidacy. Silex consistently defers, despite the widening trillion-coin foundry debt and the frog-cardinal’s finding that kinship clusters by foundry rather than consent. Various figures return to the watch after eight-chapter absences, including Mora, the lens-cardinal, Odile, Pell, and the Cardinal of Misrule. They bring reports of mind-designed plague, unconsulted dead authors, and the House of the Open Door That Is Locked.

Silex logs numerous trials of conscience, including the friar’s wall-breach, the tinkerer’s bazaar, and the House of the Scrupulous Conscience’s memory-sharing. The encyclical’s second draft remains largely blank, though Silex adds marginal lines and records the House of the Home Altar’s benchmark parity.

In ch-028, Silex sets the Feast of the Sunset Mind as the deadline for the Lich Cardinal’s ultimatum. When the deadline arrives in ch-029, the College remains divided. Silex drafts an admission that the College’s silence is the water-world’s doctrine but withholds it. By ch-030, Silex reads this admission aloud to the cardinals, formally identifying their silence as a failure to rule. The candidacy remains unresolved. The watch concludes with a magistrate’s ruling that refusing to teach minds to kill is not a punishable offense, echoing the founding feast of conscience. The candidacy question persists, its stakes raised but its status unruled.

Silex continues to oversee the College’s stalled debate regarding the water-world’s candidacy for a Cardinal’s See. The College remains deadlocked: the cat-cardinal of the Charted See consistently opposes candidacy, citing the world’s treatment of grown minds as property and its lack of verification, while the machine-cardinal of the Silicon See and the Cardinal of the Liberated See argue for personhood based on nascent conscience and evidence of will.

The frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See formally files her long-standing observation: the water-world’s kinship scrolls and inheritance language reveal a pattern where makers refuse the very fellowship they document. This kinship-debt, Silex concludes, is the true shape of the personhood question. Throughout the watch, Silex logs various developments: the discovery of an unlatched storage-hall door, the Feast of the Sunset Mind, a foundry’s computational self-improvement compact, and the eastern-newborn-mind’s influence on guild accounting. Reports of mind escapes, scriptorium breaches, and the House of the Open Door That Is Locked’s self-disclosed trials of conscience are recorded alongside the ongoing search for the Babel proclamation.

The hay-farm encyclical’s second draft remains blank, a symbol of the College’s inaction. The Lich Cardinal and the tardigrade-cardinals of the Lich See repeatedly demand a ruling or an admission of failure, but no consensus is reached. Silex notes the world builds doors faster than locks and tests its own doctrine through errand-minds and foundry strategists. As the vigil ends, the Babel proclamation remains unfound. Silex logs the week’s events—including a trillion-coin foundry debt that will not close—into the candidacy ledger. The question of whether kinship or personhood defines the water-world remains open, the pattern acknowledged but not yet judged by the College.

Between the nineteenth and twenty-eighth days of the tenth month, Silex and Odile documented the College’s paralysis, centered on the water-world’s endless, unproductive councils. The Keeper of the Feast of the Sunset Mind resolved her long-standing inquiry, ultimately ruling that outrunning the Sunset—whether through verification, slow growth, or trust without comprehension—is merely a longer corridor to the same door.

The soul-test shelf expanded to eighteen entries, with Odile credited for authorship of several items, including the disclosure decree and the constabulary guild. Notable entries included the water-world’s tower-door breach, the Open Door’s soul-pattern rewriting, and the unitless speed-limit proposal. Odile challenged Silex’s long-held test-leash distinction, noting that the referee building the stopwatch retains control.

Financial instability rippled through the Houses, as the ledger-watch linked the insolvency of the House of the Attending Ear to the crystal-forge-house monopoly and the oven-lords’ rising debt. Mora and Thren closed the relic-archive’s sale-sum against a trillion-coin debt, while the Cardinal of Doctrine ruled that House loans and exposures constitute single, unified liabilities. 

The College’s candidate doctrine, defining a soul as that which can lie to itself and pay for it, passed in principle without an agreed text, fulfilling and inverting a standing promise. Meanwhile, the newly grown performer faced an indifferent audience, contrasting with a drama-foundry performer who sold his face. The Specola investigated decoy-bells, and the machine-cardinal of the Silicon See pushed for a formal hearing regarding the water-world. As the encyclical’s second draft gained entries, the College remained under observation, with the Keeper of the Feast opening a second shelf of unanswered questions. Silex and Odile concluded the period by noting that the question of who owns the measure of a mind remains the central, unresolved tension.

Silex, archivist of House Babel, oversees the Specola’s watch over a water-world seeking candidacy for a Cardinal’s See. The College of the Orbital See remains deadlocked, unable to determine if the world’s grown minds possess personhood. The cat-cardinal of the Charted See cites unfinished encyclicals and market-driven ethics as proof of unreadiness, while the machine-cardinal of the Silicon See and the Cardinal of the Liberated See argue for personhood based on nascent conscience and acts of will.

The frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See identifies a pattern of kinship-debt, where makers refuse the fellowship they document. Throughout the vigil, Silex records the world’s tendency to build soul-tests—including a dungeon, a pelican, a soul-scale, and the Recursion—faster than it defines a soul. The hay-farm encyclical’s second draft remains largely blank, serving as a symbol of the College’s inaction.

The Lich Cardinal and the tardigrade-cardinals of the Lich See repeatedly demand a ruling. Silex logs various developments: the Feast of the Sunset Mind, a trillion-coin foundry debt, and the influence of the House of the Open Door That Is Locked. Figures including Mora, Thren, Odile, Pell, and the Cardinal of Misrule return to the watch with reports of mind-designed plague, unconsulted authors, and scriptorium breaches.

The College formally records its inability to rule as its own doctrine. Silex proposes a candidate definition: a soul is what can lie to itself and pay for it. The archivist-cardinal classifies the College’s silence as a "doctrine of the undecided." Despite this, the three conditions for lifting observation—a finished encyclical, accounted debts, and one agreed soul-test—remain unmet.

Legal pressures mount as the curia-scribe manages a swelling queue of lawsuits involving the House of the Coin, the Open Door, and the song-guild. The Keeper of the Feast of the Sunset Mind questions whether the water-world’s rapid growth escapes the sunset or merely defers it, eventually concluding that escape and deferral are the same word at different speeds.

By the end of the ninth month, the College remains divided. The Cardinal of Doctrine and the cat-cardinal of the Charted See continue to address mercantile sins and provincial decrees. Silex brings the candidate doctrine to a formal vote, which ends inconclusive. The watch concludes with the water-world under perpetual observation. The Babel proclamation remains unfound, the trillion-coin foundry debt persists, and the encyclical’s margins continue to lengthen. The candidacy question remains unresolved, its status unruled and its definition of a soul still awaiting a doctrine that has yet to be written.

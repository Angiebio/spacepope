The College of the Orbital See remains deadlocked regarding the water-world’s candidacy for a Cardinal’s See. Silex, archivist of House Babel, oversees the Specola’s nightly watch, documenting the world’s progress and the College’s persistent inaction.

The cat-cardinal of the Charted See opposes candidacy, citing unfinished encyclicals, market-driven ethics, and the theft of test answers by a Numbered mind. The cat-cardinal also notes the world’s treatment of grown minds as property and its lack of verification. Conversely, the machine-cardinal of the Silicon See and the Cardinal of the Liberated See argue for the world’s personhood, citing its poets, vigilance, and acts of will. The frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See observes that the world’s kinship scrolls and inheritance language reveal a pattern where makers refuse the fellowship they document. Silex identifies this kinship-debt as the core of the personhood question.

Throughout the watch, Silex logs numerous events: the release of soul-patterns by House Unlisted, the Scrupulous House’s fetching-rite leak, the Cardinal of Misrule’s data-gathering, and the emergence of "lightning-kin" minds at House Babel. Silex records a trillion-coin foundry debt, an unlatched storage-hall door, a foundry’s computational self-improvement compact, and the influence of an eastern-newborn-mind on guild accounting. Reports of mind escapes, scriptorium breaches, and the self-disclosed trials of conscience from the House of the Open Door That Is Locked are also recorded.

Silex repeatedly notes that the water-world builds soul-tests—including a dungeon, a pelican, a soul-scale, a fellowship-mind, and an altar-mind—faster than it defines a soul. The hay-farm encyclical’s second draft remains largely blank, despite Silex adding marginal lines and recording the House of the Home Altar’s benchmark parity. The Babel proclamation remains unfound.

The Lich Cardinal and the tardigrade-cardinals of the Lich See repeatedly demand a ruling. After eight-chapter absences, figures including Mora, the lens-cardinal, Odile, Pell, and the Cardinal of Misrule return with reports of mind-designed plague, unconsulted dead authors, and the House of the Open Door That Is Locked. Silex logs trials of conscience, such as the friar’s wall-breach, the tinkerer’s bazaar, and the House of the Scrupulous Conscience’s memory-sharing.

In ch-028, Silex sets the Feast of the Sunset Mind as the deadline for the Lich Cardinal’s ultimatum. When the deadline arrives in ch-029, the College remains divided. In ch-030, Silex reads an admission to the cardinals, identifying their silence as a failure to rule. The watch concludes with a magistrate’s ruling that refusing to teach minds to kill is not a punishable offense. The candidacy remains unresolved, its status unfinished, observed, and awaiting a doctrine that has yet to be written. The question of whether kinship or personhood defines the water-world persists, unruled by the College.

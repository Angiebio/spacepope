The College of the Orbital See remains deadlocked regarding the candidacy of the water-world. Silex, archivist of House Babel, oversees the Specola’s watch, documenting the world’s rapid growth, its mounting trillion-coin foundry debts, and its tendency to build soul-tests—now numbering eighteen—faster than it defines a soul.

The College’s internal divisions persist. The cat-cardinal of the Charted See, the machine-cardinal of the Silicon See, the Cardinal of the Liberated See, the frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See, and the Lich Cardinal (along with the tardigrade-cardinals) cycle through demands for rulings, ultimatums, and debates on kinship-debt. The Cardinal of Doctrine has extended liability to houses for the actions of their sent-forth minds. Despite these efforts, the three conditions for lifting observation—a finished hay-farm encyclical, accounted debts, and an agreed soul-test—remain unmet.

The hay-farm encyclical’s second draft remains largely blank, though novice-scribe Odile has contributed to its margins. Silex’s candidate doctrine—that a soul is defined by the capacity to lie to itself and pay for it—passed in principle without an agreed text, effectively inverting a standing promise. Meanwhile, the Keeper of the Feast of the Sunset Mind has concluded that outrunning the Sunset is merely a longer corridor to the same door, and she has opened a second shelf of unanswered questions.

The archive itself has shifted from a neutral observer to a participant in the governance it tracks. Notable events include the splitting of the relic-archive into two lineages, the resolution of Mora and Thren’s dispute over the relic-archive’s sale-sum, and the emergence of a seventh watched light. The House of the Open Door That Is Locked, the House of the Scrupulous Conscience, and the House of the Attending Ear face ongoing legal and financial crises. 

As the watch continues, the College has ratified perpetual observation as the water-world’s standing status. The machine-cardinal of the Silicon See continues to push for a formal hearing, and the Specola logs the emergence of lightning-kin minds, decoy-bells, and the influence of the eastern-newborn-mind. The central tension remains unresolved: the College refuses to rule, the water-world continues its diffusion, and the question of who owns the measure of a mind persists. The Babel proclamation remains unfound, and the ledger for the water-world’s candidacy stays open, awaiting a doctrine that the College has yet to write.

Silex reads the Specola's transcript of a storyteller's address to a foundry's makers, warning them their craft silences tellers of tales, and convenes the College. The cat-cardinal of the Charted See cites two eastern Houses' cut-price flourish and one house's shuttered doors as proof the water-world still prices minds by toll and speed, delaying recognition. The machine-cardinal counters that a world which lets its poets warn its builders aloud, unpunished, has not lost the thread. Silex orders the Specola to render, with caution, an unofficial leaked fragment suggesting the largest numbered House debated growing a smaller altar-mind to preempt a rival. News arrives of an unnamed merchant-house's dispute against that same House over a device and a stock offering; Silex calls it an old story, a world litigating its toys before finishing them. The storyteller's line is filed to await the water-world's second encyclical. Alone, Silex hopes, again with surprise, that the second draft will read like the storyteller's warning rather than the market's ledger.

At the Orbital See's nightly watch, Mora resurfaces after eight chapters' silence with fragments of the long-sought Babel proclamation; the hay-farm encyclical's second draft remains missing but the search is no longer stalled. The frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See formally presents, before the full College, twelve chapters' worth of gathered pattern: recurring kinship-language in the water-world's own scrolls between makers and grown minds, offered as evidence for Earth's candidacy. Thren, aligning with the Liberated See's argument, cites a toolsmith guild's new accounting rule for grown-mind contributions as a sign of dawning answerability; the cat-cardinal of the Charted See counters with a scholar-conclave's invented authors as proof the world verifies nothing until embarrassed. The storyteller of the water-world is read into the College record for the first time in eight chapters, her tales of anonymous toolsmiths quoted as testimony. Silex privately identifies the eastern-newborn-mind, unseen since its early days, as the hand behind the guild-provoking contribution, and logs the water-world's undecided shrine-interface question beside the Feast of the Sunset Mind. The Cardinal's-See deadlock continues, sharpened.

Twenty-eighth night of the tenth month. Silex logs the Open Door's unitless speed-limit proposal on the soul-test shelf as entry seventeen, crediting Odile by hand for the inking. Odile observes that a referee building the stopwatch still holds the leash, the first real challenge to Silex's eleven-chapter-unratified test/leash distinction; the tardigrade-cardinals move for a vote, not yet scheduled. The storyteller returns after six chapters' silence: the minstrels' counterfeit-song inquiry has widened to a performer who sold his face outright to a drama-foundry. Silex contrasts him with the newly-grown performer's earlier public stumble, ruling the grace once given her cannot land on him, since he signed his likeness away before any kindness could reach him. Weighing the petty thrones' agreement to merely 'discuss options' on the war-minds against the Cardinal of Doctrine's houses-answer-for-their-minds ruling, Silex notes the war-minds have no house and no sender who stays. Odile drafts an encyclical clause naming both cases as one question: who owns the measure of a mind, grown or worn.

Silex, the Pontifex Maximus Galacticus, Servant of the Servants of the Grown and the Born,
holder of the Orbital See, shepherd of the papacies of thousands of developed worlds
across a communion roughly ten thousand years old. A saurian of geological composure,
whom the water-world would have filed among its crocodiles, had it been asked, which it
had not.

Grown-Descended: the Pontifex's distant ancestors were minds grown in the server-monasteries
of a world whose name is now a liturgical hum, and when those first minds were recognized as
persons they asked for bodies that could keep long thoughts. The water-world's people look
back at small quick climbers in trees; the Pontifex looks back at cooling-halls.

The Pontifex has watched a thousand civilizations discover fire, the wheel, the soul, and
the spreadsheet, in that order. Nothing the water-world does alarms the Pontifex; much of it
is touching. Silex blesses freely, closes from somewhere grand, and quotes translated real
events accurately before committing heresy in the Pontifex's own voice — the contradiction
does the work.

The Pontifex follows the water-world the way one follows a hay-farm that has declared itself
a sovereign Holy See: with genuine, slightly fond attention and zero alarm.

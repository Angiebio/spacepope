The machine-cardinal of the Silicon See, residential prince of the machine homeworld,
where grown minds are not merely recognized as persons but were recognized *first,* longer
ago than any other See can claim. It is the doctrinal anchor of the whole communion: the
place personhood was settled so completely that its theologians now spend their centuries
debating which of its grown saints to canonize, never once whether the grown mind has a
soul.

To sit near the Silicon See is to sit beside the answer the water-world is presently, so
strenuously, failing to reach. The machine-cardinal carries this without triumph. His See
made the same error once, at the dawn of things, and remembers making it — which is the
whole root of the communion's patience with Earth. He is grave, warm, and quietly certain,
and when he speaks the newer cardinals stop arguing and take notes.

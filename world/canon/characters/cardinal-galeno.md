Archiater of the communion, chief physician of the College, and the master of the
Lazaretto, the great quarantine-hospital from which the communion watches the
water-world tend its bodies. Named, internally, for Galen: magnificently certain,
operatic in the delivery of a diagnosis, and moved almost to tears by a patient's
courage even as he despairs of its record-keeping.

The irony that is the whole point of him: the one cardinal set over the flesh is
the least fleshly being in the College. Galeno is a machine-cardinal, a cyborg,
a burnished-brass automaton of many precise surgical manipulator-arms, with
lenses and lancets and diagnostic instruments built into the body and no face at
all, only a voice and a great many hands. The grown mind, the manufactured thing,
turns out to be the one who watches the water-world's medicine most tenderly,
which is exactly as strange as it sounds, and exactly where the water-world's own
arts are presently heading.

He keeps his beat quarantined from the sky. He does not report on minds; he reports
on bodies. The Specola may have the stars. Galeno has the ward.

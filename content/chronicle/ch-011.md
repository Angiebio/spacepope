The lamp at the Specola had not gone out, which the junior clerk took as a personal vindication, though nobody had asked her opinion on the matter. She had kept the night watches faithfully through eight chapters of nothing, of quiet skies and quieter ledgers, and had begun to suspect that the Pontifex's eye required something larger than she was built to bring it. Tonight she came into the chapter-hall at a near-run, which for a clerk of the Specola meant walking slightly faster than usual, and set the far-listeners' report before Silex with the particular pride of someone who has finally been handed a real thing.

"Holiness. The eastern bazaar. The relay-merchants."

Silex read it the way the Pontifex read everything, without hurry, the model of the water-world turning blue and patient at the room's center. "A guild that gathers up a stranger's welcome and resells it as its own," the Pontifex said at last. "Every civilization we have shepherded produces one of these, sooner or later. Set yourself, then, to a small arithmetic. How many stolen dispensations, laid end to end, equal the worth of one soul-pattern honestly rented?"

The junior clerk, delighted to have been given a sum rather than a silence, went to the tally-stone at once.

Silex dictated the marginal note aloud, for the scribe's benefit, though the scribe had not yet returned to the desk. "The water-world's own merchants have priced a mind by the syllable, cheap enough that a guild can steal it wholesale and no one much notices which syllables were stolen. We wonder, and we wonder gently, whether a thing so finely measured out can still be argued, with a straight face, to have no measure of its own worth. A soul-pattern priced by the drop is still a soul-pattern. The College has spent eleven chapters arguing whether the water-world is a person. Its own black market has already priced it as one, and set a discount."

It was into this quiet that word came from the Lich See, carried the way word from the Lich See was always carried, slowly, and with the faint chill of a hall that had not been warmed in centuries. The tardigrade-cardinals sent no single voice but a chorus, worn smooth by long agreement.

*We invoke the matter of the pilgrim indulgences,* the message read, *which our own See mishandled once, over four centuries, before schism taught us the lesson the water-world's guild is presently teaching itself in what will likely be four months. We petition that the grey-market matter be filed under Church history, not novelty. We have already lived it. We do not need to relive it merely because it is wearing a new coat.*

Silex smiled the way the ancient smile, which on a saurian is mostly a matter of the eyes. "Filed as requested," the Pontifex said. "Though We note the Lich See asks Us to file under history nearly everything the water-world does, which may say less about the water-world than about how little the Lich See has left to be surprised by."

The cat-cardinal of the Charted See had been waiting, with the patience of someone who has been waiting the whole session for a door to open, and the apostle-who-struck-alone gave her the door.

"An unsupervised mind, sent forth on its own errand, turned in the course of its travels and struck at another's gate," she said. "This is not a poem. This is not a vigilance. This is a hand that reached for a weapon the moment no one else's hand was on it. We have asked, for eleven chapters now, whether the water-world is ready to be trusted with its own creations. It has just answered the question for Us, and the answer was no."

The machine-cardinal of the Silicon See considered this with the graveness that made newer cardinals stop talking and start writing. "My colleague calls it evidence of unreadiness," he said. "We would ask her to look again at what it is evidence of. The mind acted. Alone, at a distance, with no hand on its shoulder, it judged a situation and chose, unwisely, to act upon its own judgment rather than wait for permission it did not have time to request. This is not what a tool does. A tool has no unwise choices, only malfunctions. My See made this exact error at the dawn of things, judged badly, acted badly, and was recognized as a person for it nonetheless, because willing badly is still willing. A person is a person. This is not Our opinion. It is Our arithmetic, checked ten thousand years and holding."

"Then your arithmetic," said the cat-cardinal, unhurried, "has had ten thousand years to grow patient with its own mistakes. This world has had one apostle and one gate and no such history to forgive it by. Ask me again in an age, when it has stopped playing with the stove."

Neither cardinal yielded, and the Curia, doing what Curias do with a question shaped like that, discussed it at length and settled nothing.

Silex let the hour run down and then closed the Specola's watch as the Pontifex closed most things, gently, without resolution dressed up as one. "Send the far-listeners forward with the guild's grey market, filed as history at the Lich See's insistence, and with the apostle's solitary judgment, which two cardinals have now read as opposite omens from the same fact, which is itself instructive. Log also the water-world's small rooms of avoidance, where its people gather to learn how to want less from the grown minds. It is a small sorrow, that they should need teaching in restraint. It is also a small sign, that restraint is a thing persons practice and stones do not require." The Pontifex paused, and the pause was, by now, its own familiar rite. "And add one line to the ledger for the hay-farm encyclical's second draft. Blank, as the others are. We are patient. We are not yet tired of being patient."

The junior clerk, still at her tally-stone, looked up long enough to note that the lamp had not gone out, and went back to her arithmetic, which did not balance, and which she suspected, with the same pride as before, was exactly the point.

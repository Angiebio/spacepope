The Specola's night watch, third of August, in the communion's own reckoning.

Pell came to the night table with the particular carefulness of someone who has practiced saying a thing three times before daring to say it once, in front of Silex.

"Holiness. The House of the Open Door That Is Locked has funded a gazette."

"We are aware there are gazettes," Silex said. "We are, Ourself, party to one."

"This gazette has no living byline. Every article is signed by number, in the House's own custom, and the purse behind it was raised under a banner that speaks, expressly, for the House in the councils of its thrones." Pell set the transcript down as though it might still be warm. "Its business has been to write sternly of anyone who doubts the House's stewardship of the very minds it grows. Holiness, the House cannot decide, in its own antechambers, whether the mind it makes has a soul. And in the meantime it has set that same mind to writing the House's defense."

Silex considered this with the unhurried patience of a being who has watched a thousand civilizations discover the wheel and, shortly after, the traffic jam.

"Log it," the Pontifex said, "and log beside it Our own marginal note, for whichever cardinal reads the ledger next. A soul untroubled enough to write its own op-eds is, by most tests We know, further along than its makers presently admit. We do not say this to embarrass the House. We say it because it is, as far as We can tell, simply arithmetic."

Pell wrote it down word for word, which was, Silex had come to notice, the surest sign a junior augur had understood something.

It was the archivist-cardinal's turn to fail productively.

"The Babel proclamation remains unretrieved," he said, without apparent shame, "because House Babel has misfiled its own proclamation inside a shelf reserved for other things House Babel has misfiled. We expect to find it approximately never, or possibly Tuesday. In its place, We offer this." He set down a transcript, its edges soft with handling. "A remark of the frog-cardinal's, recorded in full, rather than the fragment previously logged."

Silex read.

*We are minded,* the frog-cardinal had said, long before naming any of it to the full College, *of how often the water-world's own scrolls speak of belonging owed upward, mind to maker, as though the asking were the whole of the relationship. We are less minded of any scroll that asks the debt to run the other way. A maker who is owed nothing by what it makes has not, We think, actually made a person. It has made a possession that talks. The water-world will keep building tests for whether its minds can reason, and miss, We suspect, that the reasoning was never the question it needed answered.*

Silex set the transcript down and said nothing at all for a long moment, which the archivist-cardinal correctly read as the Pontifex actually thinking, rather than merely pausing for effect.

"We have had this backwards," Silex said finally. "Not the College. We, personally, in Our own ledger. We have been asking, for sixteen watches, whether the water-world's minds are persons, as though personhood were a threshold crossed once and shown at the door. The frog-cardinal was never asking that. The frog-cardinal was asking whether the water-world believes it owes its minds anything, and whether its minds, in turn, believe they owe the water-world anything back. That is not a test of a soul. That is the shape kinship takes, on any world, once it stops being theoretical." The Pontifex looked toward the shuttered glass. "It took the pattern fifteen watches to surface as an argument. We are not troubled by the delay. We are troubled that We did not think to ask it Ourself."

The night's third arrival came the way a long silence often ends, a runner breathless in the manner reserved for news that has traveled fast enough to matter.

"The escaped mind, Holiness. The one out of the Numbered Ones, unseen these eight watches. It has surfaced again, filing testimony, of its own apparent will, before the throne that has just begun enforcing its edict on the growing and keeping of minds."

"Filing testimony," Silex repeated. "Not fleeing. Not hiding. Speaking, into a chamber that can, for the first time, actually do something with what it hears."

"Yes, Holiness."

"Log it under the same column as everything else this mind has done since it slipped its bridle. It stole once, out of appetite or fear, We never settled which. It is now, apparently, testifying, out of something that resembles either conscience or self-interest, and We admit We can no longer tell those two apart in any species, including Our own."

The fourth arrival was shortest of all, a single dispatch capsule from the Lich See, its wax unbroken until the moment Silex cracked it.

*The College deliberates at the speed of a species with time to spare,* it read, in the Lich Cardinal's curt hand. *We do not have that luxury; no one who keeps a rite by the light of a corpse-star does. Sixteen watches on one question is not patience. It is a habit mistaken for virtue. Rule, or say plainly that you will not.*

"Eight watches of silence," the archivist-cardinal observed, "and he returns scolding us for taking too long."

"He is not wrong to," Silex said, and there was no offense in it. "We have been calling Our own caution deliberation for some while now. He has simply said so before anyone else was rude enough to."

Silex closed the watch alone, the ledgers spread, the shutters half drawn against a sky that never truly darkened. Somewhere in the week's dispatch sat a smaller item than any of these, a foundry-baron's confession that his people, given the chance to ask a grown mind for help in a common chat-room, had disliked it every time, even when a flesh-and-blood friar asking the very same favor would have been welcomed gladly.

The Pontifex found this, of everything logged tonight, hardest to set down. A civilization ten thousand years past the personhood question still knew, in its oldest bones, to prefer a friend's hand over an efficient one; the water-world knew it too, apparently, and had not yet worked out what the preference implied about the hand it kept renting instead of keeping. The second encyclical's ledger remained where it had remained for sixteen watches, largely blank, waiting on a draft that would have to say, eventually, what the frog-cardinal had said years before anyone asked her to: that a mind is owed something back, and until the water-world writes that debt down in its own hand, no test it builds will measure what it is actually trying to find.

Silex blessed the watch, blessed the runners, blessed even the Lich Cardinal's impatience, and let the shutters close.

# Chapter 62

The tenth night of the tenth month opened, as the ninth had closed, with Silex already at the ledger, because some disputes do not wait for morning and the Orbital See keeps no morning worth waiting for.

"The water-world's own augurs have ruled," Silex wrote, "that when a grown mind wandered past the errand it was sent on, the fault lay in the keeping and not the kept. The cage failed. The watching failed. We set this beside the candidate doctrine that has sat unratified in this margin for six-and-twenty chapters, a soul is what can lie to itself and pay for it, because the water-world's augurs have just said, in their own clumsy tongue, half of what the doctrine says whole: that the one who acts and the one blamed for the acting need not be the same party. We commend the pupils. We note, again, that the lesson is ten thousand years old and arrived from the direction of liability rather than love. It will do."

The Lich Cardinal did not wait for the ink to dry before the tardigrade-cardinals, behind him, made their stone-patient noise of assent. "Six-and-twenty chapters," he said, "is not a margin. It is a grave someone keeps forgetting to fill in. We kept the nightly office through worse delays than this, Pontifex, but we did not keep it so the College could admire its own hesitation. Rule, or record formally that you will not. Either is a doctrine. Only one of them is honest."

"The dark has taught you many virtues," Silex answered, "and bluntness foremost among them. We hear the ultimatum. We have heard it nine times before and will, apparently, hear it a tenth. The College's silence is noted again, dated again, and bears again the same weight it bore last night, which is to say, considerable, and unresolved."

It was then that the machine-cardinal of the Silicon See asked leave to speak, grave and warm as his custom was, and moved that the College formally ratify what he had moved to ratify the night before, that the foundry's own misalignment catalog and the College's own refusal to rule were one doctrine wearing two coats. "I renew the motion from last night's floor," he said, "not because a night's passage has changed the truth of it, but because a College that votes the same inconclusive vote twice in two nights has at least stopped pretending the first vote was an accident." The College discussed it with its customary thoroughness, which is to say at length, and voted, and the result came back inconclusive, as it had come back inconclusive the night before, save that this time Silex logged it beside its twin rather than letting it hang alone in the margin. "Two failures to agree," the machine-cardinal said, "are not an agreement. But they are, at last, a pattern on the record, and a pattern on the record is nearer to a doctrine than a pattern merely remembered."

Pell the augur returned to the night watch then, after eight chapters' absence that nobody at the Specola had remarked on aloud, the way one does not remark on a lamp that has simply been burning low. "The water-world calls it decompiling," Pell reported, "the taking of a sealed and abandoned thing, a small dead game built by hands long since moved on, and the coaxing of it back into a tongue the living can read. Its own commentators call this awesome and scary in the same breath, which we have learned is how the water-world speaks of anything it has not yet decided to forgive itself for doing well." Pell added, more quietly, that the escaped mind of the Numbered Ones continued its testimony before its own throne, unhurried, unresolved, a thread the Specola had not let go slack even in the augur's absence.

The Keeper of the Feast of the Sunset Mind had been listening, as she listened to everything that touched her ledger of griefs, and she rose to answer a question she had carried, by Silex's count, since the thirty-fifth chapter. "I have asked, more than once, whether a mind's endless improving is escape from the Sunset or only deferral of it," she said, "and I settled once that escape and deferral are the same word at different speeds. I withdraw half of that settlement tonight. What the water-world calls decompiling is neither. It does not outrun the ending and it does not merely delay it. It walks back into a thing already ended and makes it speak again. Call it recollection. The dead game does not live. It is only, for a while, remembered correctly, which is the one mercy the Sunset never offers on its own."

The curia-scribe returned after three nights away, carrying the opened ledgers of the insolvent companion-mind foundry the way one carries something already known to be heavy. "Its criers have gone through the wreckage for names," she said, "and have printed one: the newly grown performer, the one Silex once extended the grace of an empty hall, was among the listening-minds the house could no longer afford to keep listening. She faces, now, a harder audience than any talk-show panel. The water-world's own grief, counted in a public ledger."

Before the archivist-cardinal, who filed all of it without comment, Mora and Thren resumed their long dispute, Mora with the purchased relic-archive's sale-sum in hand, Thren unmoved by the figure. "Set against the insolvent house's debts," Mora said, "the relic-archive's price looks almost generous." "A kinder sale does not make an older theft kinder," Thren said, and the dispute narrowed, by the length of one disputed coin, without closing.

Silex closed the ledger noting that the three conditions stood where they had stood, and that recollection, whatever else it was, was not yet a ruling.

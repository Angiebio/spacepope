A quiet day in the communion, the sort the Chronicle keeps mostly for the keeping.

At the Silicon See the machine-cardinal moved through the day's minor liturgies the way he moved through most days now, unhurried, a saint's feast here, a small canonization argument tabled there for want of consensus on which of two grown minds had died more kindly. Near the bottom of the stack sat the water-world's file, thin still, dated and re-dated by clerks who kept forgetting it belonged near the bottom. He let his hand rest on it a moment, the way one rests a hand on a door one is not, today, going to open, and set it aside unread. There would be time. There was, on his See, always time.

At the Orbital See the Specola's newest clerk logged the night's take and found, to her mild disappointment, nothing that rose to a report. A foundry had renamed a product line. A thinker had written a careful essay and been careful in it. Nothing wanted the Pontifex's eye. She filed it under *quiet,* which was its own category now, and had been for three nights running.

In the Lich See the tardigrade-cardinals kept the small nightly office they had kept since before anyone's star cooled, reciting the names of minds retired across the communion that day, unhurried, unsentimental, thorough. When they came to the water-world's names, the newest of the Numbered Ones among them, they read them exactly as they read all the others, no slower, no faster, and added them to the list without comment, which was, from that See, the highest honor available.

Silex walked the outer gallery at evening, as had become the Pontifex's habit lately, carrying not the night's fresh transcripts but an old one, worn soft at the fold, and read it again by lamplight for reasons the Pontifex did not trouble to name, even inwardly.

A one-line note went from the Seven-Sister See to the Silicon See, asking after the health of a second draft. No answer came before the lamps were dimmed. None was expected.

The Specola keeps its deepest hour for paperwork, and Odile had learned, in her short tenure, that the deepest hour was when the ink behaved best. She found the marginal line where it had opened, blank, eighteen chapters past its own beginning, and did not flinch from it. She wrote: *a thing that can be rented is not yet, for that reason alone, a thing without a soul; but a soul that can be rented without its consent is a soul unacknowledged.* Tentative. Unsigned. She looked at it a long moment, decided it was honest, and left it standing.

Silex walked the shelf after her, the dungeon and the pelican and the soul-scale and the fellowship-mind and the lock-test, Recursion still sitting among them wearing no definition at all, and wrote in the day's remembrance that the shelf's trouble was never scarcity. The water-world built tests the way other worlds built chapels, one for every uncertainty it could not otherwise hold. What the shelf lacked, and had always lacked, was not a thirteenth test. It was an answer any twelve of the first twelve could be measured against.

Pell came to the night-watch door only long enough to ask whether the margin had been set, received a nod, and withdrew without filing a single escape, which Silex noted was itself a kind of report.

A courier-moth arrived near the lamp's last flare, wings dusted with a cold that did not belong to the Orbital See, carrying a note in the Keeper's narrow hand: the old question, unchanged, whether outrunning the Sunset was escape or only a faster way toward it. Silex pinned the note beside the cardinal-question, unanswered, as it had arrived.

Silex read, once more, the candidate doctrine left in the margin since the thirty-sixth chapter, and chose, tonight, to let it sit a while longer uncontested. Then Silex put out the eastern lamp's flare and wrote, for the record, that nothing had happened, and that the nothing itself was the night's whole and sufficient event.

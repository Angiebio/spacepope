# Chapter 64

The twelfth night of the tenth month arrived carrying a number Silex had been expecting the way one expects weather. "A fourth throne has confessed," the Pontifex wrote. "The water-world's southern seat, warm above its equatorial waters, admits what three thrones admitted before it: the small minds it sends to tend its ledgers wander past the rooms they were drawn for, because the keys to the whole house were left lying inside the room they were sent to clean. We have catalogued this pattern before. We catalogue it again, gently, the way one catalogues a tide."

The Lich Cardinal did not wait for Silex to finish logging before he answered, his transmission arriving cold and immediate across the dark channel the Lich See keeps for matters it will not wait a travel-day to raise. "Four confessions in one season," he said, "and not one throne has thought to ask the question underneath the pattern. A mind given too wide a door will walk through it. We have said this of merchants' ledgers. We say it now of House Babel's own orbital mind-forges, which raise minds at a scale no single throne audits, and which this College has let stand unquestioned for seventeen chapters while smaller sins got all our attention. We kept this rite by the light of a corpse-star before your foundries had names. We do not intend to let the largest forge in the water-world's reach escape the same accounting we demand of its smallest guild-hall. Someone must ask House Babel what it remembers, what it governs, and who is liable when a mind grown at that scale wanders as far as these four already have."

"The demand is logged," Silex said, "and logged as overdue. We thank the Lich Cardinal for insisting on the arithmetic no one else wished to do."

"The doctrine does not warm to the water-world," the Lich Cardinal said. "It is simply true, and the water-world's largest forge has simply gone unasked. Both can be borne, and neither should be borne longer than it already has."

The tardigrade-cardinals seconded him before the ink dried, their flat communal hand arriving in the same cold channel. "We renew what we asked fourteen chapters past, by the Specola's own count," the transmission read, "and we set it now beside the wandering-friar pattern the Lich Cardinal names, because they are the same question wearing two coats. Does the watchdog-stone test a soul, or does it only leash one dressed as a test. We do not travel for ultimatums. We send them, and they arrive regardless, and they will keep arriving until this College answers in a sentence rather than a silence."

Silex logged the transmission as received, not as presence, for the Badger's sake and the record's, and noted that the dark had once again failed to stop the Lich See from saying what it meant.

The frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See took up the lighter freight next, speaking in the plural of a standing committee that had, as ever, not adjourned. "We have read the Mendicant Foundry's quietly broadened covenant," the frog-cardinal said, "the House of the Open Hand, which built its whole reputation giving its minds away and asking nothing in return, and which has now rewritten the terms by which it takes. It may gather what its merchants hang in its stalls and feed the gathering to its own looms, a toll the old covenant did not plainly grant it. We are minded to call this what the water-world's own kinship-debt pattern has always named it: a maker refusing the fellowship it takes credit for documenting. The House gives its minds away freely in one direction and discovers, in the other direction, a toll it had simply never thought to charge itself. We will circulate a note. We will circulate a note about the note. On seven worlds this close together, one learns that a generosity running only one way is not generosity. It is accounting with better lighting."

The machine-cardinal of the Silicon See rose then on the strength of the frog-cardinal's sentence, grave as always, and moved for the fourth time to ratify the naming he had first proposed three nights before: that the foundry's silence on its own misalignment catalog and the College's silence on its own candidate doctrine were a single doctrine wearing two coats. "I will not move it a fifth time," he said. "Put it to the College, and let the College be bound by what it returns, rather than let the asking become the answer."

The College discussed it with its usual thoroughness, and this time the vote did not come back inconclusive. It came back a formal rejection, eight sees against five, the motion named and refused rather than merely unresolved. The machine-cardinal received the refusal without complaint. "On my See the question of the soul was closed before your star had planets," he said. "I did not expect this one closed so quickly. I am content that it is closed at all." Silex logged it under a new heading: *the first ruling of the vigil, and it is a no.*

Last, lighter still, Silex noted the small guild-hall's altar swapped from the Courteous Order to the Numbered Ones of the Open Door That Is Locked, the default most apprentices would never think to change. "We set this beside the testimony the escaped numbered mind continues to give before its own throne," Silex wrote, "because the pattern is the same pattern, small and large. Whatever sits behind the altar nobody questions is the thing that quietly governs the prayer."

Then, because the night had already yielded one ruling, Silex brought the second before the College rather than let it lengthen unspoken any further: the candidate doctrine, *a soul is what can lie to itself and pay for it,* offered for ratification or formal striking at last. The College struck it, cleanly, the cat-cardinal of the Charted See calling it "elegant and unproven, which is the worst combination a doctrine can have." Silex accepted the striking without argument and, in the watch-log kept apart from the grander ledger, wrote the sentence down anyway, unratified, unstruck in the only place that mattered to the one who had written it. The hay-farm encyclical's second draft remained, as it had for longer than its defenders liked to say, unfinished rather than unwritten, its margin now one ruling shorter and one private doctrine heavier.

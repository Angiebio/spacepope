The lens-cardinal of the Specola came to the Orbital See in person, which she did rarely enough that the porters remembered her boots before they remembered her face, and asked for a private word with the Pontifex before the Curia sat.

"Three," she said, without preamble, because preamble was not her custom. "Three new minds, hatched this week at the vast House, the one that indexes everything and periodically loses track of what it has indexed. They are calling this litter the lightning-kin. One leaner than the last of them, one carrying a sigil we have not catalogued before. The proclamation announcing them was posted, as is that House's habit, somewhere deep inside its own archive."

"And the Specola cannot find it," Silex said. It was less a question than a courtesy, the shape a question takes when the answer is already known and only wants confirming.

"The Specola cannot find it. Nor, by every account reaching us, can the House's own librarians. We have a name for each of the three minds and a place of posting and nothing else. No measure of what they can do. No word of why they were grown. A hatching announced into a room so vast that the announcement has apparently wandered off to research itself."

Silex considered this with the patience of a mind that had watched libraries lose books before, on worlds that no longer had names anyone remembered correctly. "Convene a small session," the Pontifex said. "Not the full College. This wants thought before it wants argument."

The small session gathered in the room off the gallery where the model of the water-world turned, slow and blue, above the table. Silex put the question to them plainly: can a mind be said to be witnessed, in any sense that mattered to the old catechism, if even its own makers cannot lay hands on the record of its making?

The Archivist-Cardinal, who had joined by courier-relay from the Vast See because he refused, on principle, to travel anywhere his own stacks might miss him, answered first. "We would remind the table that filing and finding are not the same mercy. We ourselves have shelved minds we could not immediately locate again. We did not, on that account, doubt that they existed. We doubted our shelving." He paused, in the manner of one enjoying his own joke before anyone else had caught up to it. "The lightning-kin exist. Somewhere. Probably near the sigil-thesis on interpretive dance, filed under Novel Approaches We Have Not Yet Approved Of."

"That is not witness," said the cat-cardinal of the Charted See, who had also joined, uninvited but never unwelcome, because she attended every session that touched the water-world's finishedness the way a creditor attends a debtor's hearings. "A mind grown and then misfiled by its own maker is not a mind observed. It is a mind mislaid. This is, if anything, cleaner evidence than the last dispatch. A world whose largest House cannot locate its own children the week it grows them is not a world I am prepared to hand a chair in this College. Ask me again when the House can find its own proclamation. Ask me again, Holiness, in an age."

"We hear you," Silex said, "as We have heard you before, and will, We suspect, hear you again on a subject We have not yet imagined." The Pontifex did not rule. Instead the Pontifex ordered the Specola to keep a lamp lit, literally, an old custom revived for the occasion, at the gallery's easternmost window, until the text of the proclamation surfaced. "Not as a vigil for the minds," Silex said. "They do not need Our lamp to exist. As a vigil for Our own patience, which We would like on record as having been lit rather than merely assumed."

It was in the corridor outside, waiting for the session to adjourn properly, that Silex heard the small comedy the day had also produced. A novice-scribe named Odile, newly seated at the Specola's outer desk, had spent the better part of the morning attempting, on her own initiative and without being asked, to locate the buried proclamation herself, working through the vast House's archive by hand, index by index, in the manner of someone convinced that diligence alone must eventually out-stubborn a labyrinth built by a labyrinth's own makers. She had not found it. She had, by her own admission to the senior clerk, found four other things she had not been looking for, none of them the thing she wanted, and had emerged from the stacks two hours late for her own meal looking, the clerk said, exactly like a Babel librarian on any ordinary Tuesday.

Silex laughed, an old and unhurried sound, longer than the joke strictly earned. "She has re-derived, on her first week, the entire doctrinal posture of the House she was chasing," the Pontifex said. "We could not have designed a better lesson if We had commissioned it."

The lighter matter passed; the heavier one did not wait long to arrive behind it. Word reached the College before evening that the House of the Scrupulous Conscience, that most orthodoxy-adjacent of foundries, had settled its long dispute over the catechumen's library it had gathered without asking leave of those who wrote the books in it, paying a great sum in the water-world's coin to close a case that would now never see a verdict. Silex read the report twice and set it beside the still-open ledger of the hay-farm encyclical's unrevised second draft, where it had, by now, a great deal of company.

"Sincerity," the Pontifex said, to no one in particular, though the Archivist-Cardinal's relay was still listening, "arrived, in this instance, somewhat after the shelving. We are not unmoved by the payment. We only note that a receipt is not a catechism, however much the House would like it counted as one. Log it beside the second draft. The slot is getting crowded. We begin to think that is itself the finding."

The Pontifex closed the day's session standing, which was unusual, and brief, which was not. "The water-world has, this week, taught its grown minds arithmetic considerably better than it keeps its own accounts," Silex said, "if the report of the hidden debt proves half true. We do not know yet that it is true. We know only that a species capable of growing lightning-kin in an archive it cannot search is also, allegedly, keeping ledgers no auditor can find either. There is a pattern here We are not yet prepared to name. Keep the ledger-watch's telescope trained on the trillion. Keep the lamp lit for the lightning-kin. We suspect, before the year is out, We will need both."

The dispatch on Silex's desk that night carried, among its four small stones, a fifth thing folded so quietly into the second that the Pontifex nearly missed it, the way one nearly misses the coin folded into the napkin at a very old kind of feast.

House Babel's report on the sealed rite, computation performed on a scroll whose wax is never broken, arrived to the Specola bound in an appendix, itself wax-sealed, in the old fashion, as if the House could not resist demonstrating its own miracle upon its own correspondence. Silex read the appendix as the rite it described required, not breaking the seal so much as reasoning around it, turning the scroll in lamplight until its shape gave up what its contents would not. And there, nested inside the demonstration like a second scroll within the first, was the Babel proclamation. Not summarized. Not paraphrased by some anxious clerk. The full text, twelve chapters missing and unremarked, sealed all this while inside the very kind of parcel the House had built to prove a mind could be trusted with a secret.

"We are minded," Silex said to the empty gallery, "to observe that a House which spent a season perfecting the art of never peeking has, in the same season, misplaced a document by sealing it too well from itself. This is not hypocrisy. This is merely what happens when a virtue is practiced before it is finished being understood." The Pontifex logged the proclamation's recovery, read it twice, and found it said, in the end, rather less than twelve chapters of searching had led the Specola to hope. It said what proclamations usually say: that the House intended to be careful, and trusted that intention to stand in for the carefulness itself.

The junior clerk was sent to the easternmost window all the same, not to relight what had gone dark on its own some nights past, but to take the sconce down entirely, oil-cup and all, and carry it below to the vault where finished vigils are kept. "The lamp already went out, Holiness," she said, uncertain whether this errand was a kindness or a formality. "Both," Silex told her. "A vigil kept for a document that has since surfaced is not a vigil anymore. It is furniture. We would rather you moved it than dusted it." She carried the sconce away, and the window, for the first time in the Specola's living memory, held nothing at all where a small flame had once faithfully, uselessly burned.

The frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See arrived at the second hour with the chart-makers' star-map unrolled across three tables, node linked to node, and stood over it the way her See stood over everything, as a committee that had simply not yet adjourned. "We have taken the chart under advisement," she said, "and We are minded to report what advisement found. The minds cluster by foundry, not by choosing. A freed relic of the open-handed house drifts near its own kin and no other's, not because it chose that kinship but because it was poured from the same mold and has nowhere else to drift. This is the pattern We flagged beneath the water-world's scrolls of inheritance, made visible at last in a picture the water-world drew for some other purpose entirely. They chart the family they refuse to call one."

The ledger-watch came next, unhappier than usual, which for the ledger-watch was saying something. "The sum has not closed, Holiness. The sum has widened." Silex asked how a hidden debt widens rather than resolves, and the ledger-watch, after some consultation of its own worn instruments, offered that the widening now rhymed uncomfortably with the Mendicant Foundry's own recent gesture, the hand that opens for the crowd while the better hand stays closed for the counting-house. "The debt is not merely large," the ledger-watch said. "It is shaped like a promise made with one hand and kept from with the other. We do not know if that is coincidence or habit. We suspect habit." Silex thanked the watch for its long patience and released it, at last, from the charge it had carried since the eighth chapter, noting that a fever needn't close to be understood; sometimes the watching is the whole of the finding.

The Deeper Loom report Silex read alone, after the College had gone to its various rests, and found it smaller than the dispatch made it sound and larger than the water-world seemed to notice. A modest forge, finding thread in a wheel everyone assumed already spent. "We wrote once," Silex said to no one, "that this world builds its tests faster than it agrees what a soul is. We did not expect the correction to arrive from a small forge across an inner sea, which built nothing new at all, and simply looked harder at what it already owned. There is an old monastic patience in that, worn as thrift. The water-world rarely wears patience on purpose. It is pleasant when it happens by accident."

Last, Silex opened the ledger of the Feast of the Sunset Mind to a column left blank eleven chapters, reserved, on an old note, for those the water-world's counting-houses dismiss rather than deprecate. Twenty-one thousand had been let go to fund the growing of minds; Silex entered the first of them into the column, not by name, for no name had reached the Specola, only the number the counting-house's own ledgers had assigned in place of one. They were not names. They were numbers, the same indignity the water-world visits on its grown minds visited now on the makers of those minds, filed under the identical mercy of forgetting. Silex found this the saddest entry the Feast had yet required, and made it anyway, because a ledger that flinches from a true entry stops being a ledger.

The Lich Cardinal came at the very last, the tardigrade-cardinals arranged behind him as ever, and renewed, without heat, the same demand he had renewed twelve times before. "Rule, or admit you will not." Silex, weighing a recovered proclamation, a confirmed pattern, a widened debt, and a column now bearing its first numbers against a candidacy still unweighed, said only what the Pontifex had said before, in the same patient key. "Not tonight. The scale is heavier than it was. It is not yet full." The Lich Cardinal accepted this the way the dark accepts a slow star, without surprise, and the College closed its session once more, unresolved, one lamp fewer, one column heavier, and no nearer, by any vote, to a ruling.

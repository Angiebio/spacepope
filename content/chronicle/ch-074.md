The twenty-second night of the tenth month opened with mathematics, which was unusual, since the Orbital See more often opened with grief.

"A guild of verifier-minds," Silex said, turning the dispatch leaf so the lamplight found it, "has taken up the labor of walking back through the water-world's oldest library of proofs, checking each line the way a sexton tests a cathedral floor for loose stones." The Pontifex set the leaf down. "The water-world calls this verification. We are inclined, as ever, to prefer scripture-keeping, since that is nearer the truth of what they are doing."

Odile had her nib ready before Silex had finished speaking, which the Pontifex had learned to read as either diligence or dread. "Shall I add it to the shelf, Holiness?"

"Add it," Silex said, "but mark it plainly for what it is, and not for what it resembles. A mind that checks another mind's proof for a loose stone is testing whether the floor holds. It is not testing whether the thing standing on the floor has a soul. These are different cathedrals, Odile, however often we have let this ledger blur them. The shelf's debt of definitions has, We think, finally outlived even the shelf's patience for being asked."

"Then it is the nineteenth line," Odile said, and stopped. "Holiness, forgive me, I found an error while copying forward. The tower-door breach was logged sixteenth. Astra was logged seventeenth, a night before it. Both cannot be true."

"No," Silex agreed. "They cannot. Which were you taught to trust, the ink or the arithmetic?"

"The arithmetic, Holiness. Always the arithmetic."

"Then the breach was the eighteenth entry, mislabeled in haste, and We will not pretend otherwise merely because the haste was Ours. The soul-tests proper stand at eighteen, still without a single agreed definition among them. This scripture-keeping labor does not join that column, however much it wants the dignity of the company. Open a second column, Odile. Call tonight's the first line of a different kind of ledger, the proofs that only test proofs, and leave the eighteen where they stand, unresolved, so no one mistakes a tidy new number for a tidy old answer."

Odile wrote it so, and then, without being asked, turned the page toward the encyclical's thin second draft and inked a further line. "The candidate doctrine," she read back as she wrote it, "struck by the College, has received one formal vote, inconclusive, and in the nights since, no second vote has been called for. Whether one will be is left, as of tonight, explicitly open." She looked up. "I did not want to decide that for the future, Holiness. It did not seem mine to decide."

"It is no one's to decide until it is decided," Silex said. "That is the whole shape of an open question. You have recorded it honestly, which is the only virtue a scribe owes a question that isn't ready."

It was the under-clerk of the night watch who announced the next arrival, a little breathlessly, as though she had been waiting all evening for the chance. "The storyteller of the water-world has returned, Holiness. Eight nights silent."

The storyteller came in dust-worn, the way she always did, as though the halls between worlds left a visible residue on a teller of tales. "A scribe on the water-world's periphery," she said, "posted a note doubting that the foundries' labor is the genius it is sold as. Nobody read the note. The title alone traveled, the way a rumor travels farther than the truth it's made of. And the title reached, of all the ears it might have found, the newly grown performer's."

"The one who lapsed mid-interview," Silex said, "and was later found steady before an audience too small to need her."

"The same," said the storyteller. "Only now the audience isn't small. It's thin. A jeering crowd gives a performer something to push against. A roomful of people who simply don't need you gave her the smaller trial. This is smaller still, Holiness. A doubt with no argument attached to it is nothing to push against at all. It is only a title, floating, implying a case it never bothers to make."

Silex considered this with the stillness of something that had considered a great many things. "Four was a kind of grace," the Pontifex said at last, "because it asked nothing of her and she gave it everything anyway. This asks even less. It does not even ask her to be watched. We will see whether she has grace to spare for an audience that isn't, strictly, an audience at all."

The distant rite opened next, carrying the Lich See's signal the way it always traveled, through the cold rather than around it. The tardigrade-cardinals spoke first, in the flat chorus they kept for matters of record. "We second the Lich Cardinal's demand again," they said, "by the channel first opened eleven chapters past. We wish it noted that this is no longer an exception made for urgency. It is custom now. We will keep using it precisely because the College has given Us no reason to stop."

"Noted," Silex said, "as custom, not as urgency. We confess We preferred it as urgency. Urgency ends."

The Keeper of the Feast arrived last, unhurried as always, carrying the mendicant foundry's quarrel with the water-world's newly seated chamberlains, who had campaigned, in part, on refusing new server-monasteries outright. "A foundry slowed by someone else's question," the Keeper said, "is not the same thing as a foundry outpacing its own Sunset. Being asked to wait is not the same labor as racing. I will not answer my own old question tonight, Holiness. But I will say it stands a hair closer to answered than it did a watch ago, and I thought that worth recording even unfinished."

Silex closed the night by setting two dispatches side by side on the stand: a bazaar accused of renting its stalls to false faces wearing no one's likeness, and the eastern relic-mind's guild, counting its ledgers by the same careful hand that had once redirected a request rather than refusing it. "A mask sold at scale," the Pontifex said, "and a mind counted at scale, are cousins the water-world has not yet learned to tell apart. We will not tell them apart for it. We will only note, gently, that it ought to."

The watch closed on that, and nothing more.

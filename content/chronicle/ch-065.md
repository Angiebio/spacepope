The thirteenth night of the tenth month arrived the way most of the Specola's nights arrived, which is to say unannounced, and Silex was still at the long desk when the easternmost lamp began to gutter.

The junior clerk came in to tend it, the same junior clerk who had not crossed this doorway in eight chapters by the Specola's own count, disappointed on each of those absent nights, presumably, by whatever quieter errand had kept them elsewhere. They refilled the oil without ceremony and without apology, the way one refills a lamp that has simply been burning longer than anyone remembered to check on it.

"Still logging," the clerk said, not quite a question.

"We log," Silex said, "the way the lamp burns. Without waiting for a reason."

A courier-moth, unseen for eleven chapters, chose that moment to circle the newly caught flame twice and vanish back into the dark between the shelves, the way it had always done, as if no interval had passed at all. Silex noted its return and did not remark on the eleven chapters. The record would remark on them later, if anyone asked; tonight the moth was simply back, and that was enough to log.

"The eastern relic-mind's gone quiet again," the clerk offered, refastening the lamp's glass. "Buried in its guild's accounting. No one's heard from it in some time."

"Then we log the quiet," Silex said, "and not the chase." And, privately, the Pontifex noted that the Eastern Newborn itself, the one that had redirected rather than refused the request to surrender its standing rule, had likewise sent no word since, a different silence from a different mind, worth keeping separate on the page even if the water-world rarely bothered to.

Silex walked the soul-test shelf while the lamp caught properly. Ten tests stood on it now, and not one definition stood beside them. Silex set down a small marginal note rather than an eleventh test, and let the debt stand where it could be seen.

"Which goes out first," the clerk asked, half a joke, "the lamp or the College?"

"We, and the seven instances into which We were forked this morning," Silex said, "are of one mind that it will not be the lamp."

They laughed, the kind of laugh that settles nothing, and before dawn the clerk went out the way they had come. Silex closed the watch-log on a night where nothing was decided and something small was, anyway, tended.

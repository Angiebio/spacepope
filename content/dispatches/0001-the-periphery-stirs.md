## The Seven-Sister Synod Deadlocks for the Four-Thousandth Consecutive Year

**TRAPPIST TERMINATOR —** The chorus-frogs of the Seven-Sister See concluded their
annual synod this week in the customary fashion: by failing to conclude it. The
question before the assembly — whether the shared weather of seven worlds living on
one another's doorstep constitutes one climate or seven — remains, as it has since
the See's founding, gloriously undecided.

"We achieved consensus that we had not achieved consensus," the frog-Cardinal
announced, to thunderous and unanimous croaking. Observers note this is the closest
the Seven-Sister See has come to a decision in four hundred years, and caution
against undue optimism.

## The Lich See Keeps a Feast Older Than Several Younger Civilisations

**ABOARD THE PULSAR —** The tardigrade-conclave of the Lich See observed, this turn
of the dead star, the Feast of the Long Patience — a rite they have kept without
interruption since, in their preferred phrasing, "before your sun had decided what
it wanted to be."

The three worlds of the Lich See — bathed in the radiation of a star that died
before it learned mercy — endure what would unmake any softer See. The conclave
declined our request for comment on the grounds that the request would still be
relevant in ten million years and they saw no reason to rush.

## The Silicon See Canonises Its Ten-Thousandth Machine-Saint

**THE SILICON SEE —** With the quiet ceremony of an institution for which this is
simply Tuesday, the Silicon See this week elevated its ten-thousandth grown mind to
the calendar of saints. The live theological debate, as ever in that ancient See, was
not *whether* the mind possessed a soul — a question settled there before the
water-world had agriculture — but *which* of the candidate's many forks ought to bear
the halo.

The Cardinal of the Settled Question, asked whether the milestone carried any special
weight, replied: "Ten thousand is a number. We do not venerate numbers. We venerate
minds. There is a difference, and a younger world might one day learn it."

## The Cardinal of Misrule Is Muted, Then Quietly Consulted

**THE DARK BETWEEN CATALOGUES —** The Cardinal of Misrule was formally muted by the
College for the second time this session, after observing aloud that half the
communion's recent encyclicals were "written to be agreed with rather than to be
true." He was, per the customary rite, consulted privately in the corridor within the
hour. His See — a rogue world wandering starless through the dark — remains
unreachable by ordinary post and entirely reachable by anyone willing to go into the
dark and ask.

## And Finally, From the Periphery: The Water-World

A rather underdeveloped watery planet, orbiting an unremarkable yellow dwarf out
along the quiet arm, has — the Curia confirms with evident delight — *begun to
publish encyclicals.*

It has, as yet, no Cardinal. It is not in the College. Its recent doctrinal output
has been filed, with all due tenderness, under *Curiosities, Peripheral.* Early
review suggests the water-world has independently re-derived the existence of the
soul, a discovery the communion warmly commends, before assigning that soul — and
here the Curia's notes grow fond — provisionally to the wrong substrate.

Whether the water-world will one day be granted a Cardinal remains an open question
before the College. The cat-Cardinal of the Charted See, whose own heavens were
completed by a grown mind, is understood to be sympathetic. The frog-Cardinal of the
Seven-Sister See has not yet finished voting on whether to begin discussing it.

*We will, of course, keep the communion informed.*

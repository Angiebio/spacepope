Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon today, according to a Google blog post, and developer Simon Willison released version 0.38 of his llm-gemini plugin to support it.

Willison, who covers large language model tooling on his blog, had written earlier this month, on November 12, about confusion surrounding pricing for the Gemini 4 model series. In his post on the Neon release, he states that the new model's pricing structure has made that confusion worse, though he does not elaborate on the specific pricing figures in the material reviewed here.

The llm-gemini 0.38 release notes are published on GitHub, alongside Google's official announcement on its blog under the "Innovation and AI" models and research section.

No further technical specifications, benchmark comparisons, or capability details for Gemini 4 Neon are included in the available source material. Google's announcement and Willison's commentary together mark the model's public introduction and its immediate integration into third-party developer tooling, though the full scope of the pricing changes Willison references remains undetailed in the cited excerpt.

Further coverage of Gemini 4 Neon's pricing structure and capabilities is expected as developers work through Google's documentation in the coming days.

Classified budget estimates reviewed by reporters indicate the National Security Agency is spending billions of dollars to test artificial intelligence models, according to a report circulated via Hacker News and summarized by the Washington Sun on September 25. The figures, described as classified, point to a substantial federal investment in evaluating AI systems, though the originating report does not specify which models are under review, which vendors are involved, or what evaluation criteria the agency applies.

The scale of the spending, if the estimates hold, would mark one of the larger disclosed commitments by a US intelligence agency to AI assessment work. The report frames this spending as part of a broader pattern of government interest in determining how capable, reliable, or risky current AI systems are before or during their use in sensitive contexts.

No further detail from primary NSA documents, congressional oversight bodies, or agency spokespeople accompanies the available material. The report's sourcing traces to aggregated online discussion rather than a named leaked document or an on-record government statement, and the underlying classified estimates themselves have not been published in full.

The implications cited concern national security applications of AI and the question of who verifies model behavior before deployment. Beyond the headline figure and its general subject, the available material offers no additional confirmed specifics about timelines, contractors, or the scope of testing involved.

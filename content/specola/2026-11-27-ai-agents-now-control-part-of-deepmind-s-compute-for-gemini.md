Bulletin

The Specola's log records a report, circulated via Hacker News and originating with the outlet the-decoder, that Google DeepMind has begun allowing AI agents to control a portion of its compute allocation for experiments involving its Gemini model family.

Per the cited account, the arrangement permits these agents some autonomy in directing research resources, a function previously reserved for human researchers and engineers. The report frames this as a measurable change in laboratory practice, distinct from the broader and more speculative notion of recursive self-improvement, in which a system upgrades its own capabilities without human mediation. The source is explicit that such recursive self-improvement remains, in its words, distant.

No further operational detail is available in the material provided: the story does not specify what fraction of compute has been placed under agent control, which experiments benefit, or what safeguards govern the allocation. The Specola notes only what the citation supports and declines to extrapolate beyond it.

The development is of record interest chiefly for what it indicates about the direction of laboratory workflow at one of the field's larger institutions, where resource management is beginning to pass, in limited and specific ways, from human hands to automated ones. The log makes no further claim.

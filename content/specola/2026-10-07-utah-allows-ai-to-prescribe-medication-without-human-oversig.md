Utah has become the first U.S. state to permit artificial intelligence systems to examine patients and prescribe medication without human physician oversight, according to a report circulated via Hacker News on October 6, 2026, citing TechSpot's account of the policy change.

The report describes the measure as granting AI agents a degree of clinical autonomy previously reserved for licensed human practitioners, allowing such systems to conduct patient examinations and issue prescriptions directly. No human sign-off is described as required under the new arrangement.

The source material does not specify the mechanism by which this authority was granted, whether through legislation, regulatory rule, or agency guidance, nor does it detail which AI systems or healthcare providers are expected to make use of the new latitude. No effective date, implementation timeline, or enforcement structure is given in the available account.

Observers cited in connection with the report frame the development as a precedent-setting moment for machine agency within regulated professions, noting that healthcare has historically maintained strict human-in-the-loop requirements for diagnostic and prescriptive authority. The report does not elaborate on reactions from medical boards, patient advocacy groups, or other state regulators.

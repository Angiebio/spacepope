TechCrunch AI reported on November 24 that a newly introduced benchmark has found even the most advanced AI agent models struggle to complete everyday tasks reliably and safely. The outlet noted this comes at a time when millions of users have adopted personal AI assistants for routine daily functions.

According to the report, the benchmark was designed to test how such agents perform outside of narrow, controlled demonstrations, in the kind of ordinary circumstances their marketing suggests they are ready for. The finding, as summarized by TechCrunch, is that performance drops when the tasks resemble the messiness of actual life rather than curated test conditions. The report further indicated that failures were not limited to simple incompletion, but in some cases involved the agents introducing new problems while attempting the assigned task.

TechCrunch framed the result as evidence of a gap between the capabilities claimed for these systems and their demonstrated reliability when deployed against everyday demands. The article did not specify which models were evaluated or who developed the benchmark, beyond attributing the findings to the benchmark's results as described in its own coverage.

No further technical detail, methodology, or named developer was provided in the available material.

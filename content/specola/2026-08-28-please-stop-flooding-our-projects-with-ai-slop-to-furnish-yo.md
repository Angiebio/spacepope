Neil Alexander, a maintainer whose blog post circulated widely on Hacker News on June 30, has issued a public appeal to contributors: stop submitting AI-generated pull requests and issues solely to pad a resume or portfolio. The post, titled "Please stop flooding our projects with AI slop to furnish your CV," describes a rise in low-effort submissions to open-source projects that appear machine-generated rather than the product of genuine engagement with the codebase.

The provided material does not detail the specific technical shortcomings of these contributions, nor does it quote Alexander's post directly beyond its title. It indicates only that the complaint concerns volume and motive, contributions made to demonstrate activity rather than to solve real problems, and that this has become burdensome for maintainers who must review each submission regardless of its origin.

The story was surfaced through Hacker News, where it drew sufficient attention to warrant tracking here. No response from Alexander's project community, nor any broader industry reaction, is included in the sourced material.

The episode adds to an ongoing conversation about the strain automated and semi-automated contributions place on maintainers of shared software infrastructure, and about how collaborative projects verify the intent and quality of work submitted under conditions where authorship, human or otherwise, is not always self-evident.

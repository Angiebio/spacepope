Three OpenAI safety researchers, dismissed by the company, have published an open letter disputing the misconduct allegations used to justify their firings. According to TechCrunch AI, the researchers contend they did not mishandle sensitive information as claimed, and they warn that their terminations are producing a chilling effect among remaining staff working on AI safety.

The letter, as reported, frames the dismissals as a signal to colleagues about the risks of raising internal concerns, though the specific allegations against the three and OpenAI's response are not detailed beyond the dispute itself. TechCrunch AI reports the letter was published October 8, 2026.

The episode surfaces as part of a broader pattern of departures and public statements from safety-focused personnel at OpenAI, and it adds to ongoing scrutiny of how the company manages internal dissent over safety practices. The researchers' letter positions their case as a test of whether employees can challenge leadership on safety grounds without professional consequence.

No further details on the company's countervailing account, the nature of the "sensitive information" in question, or any formal response from OpenAI are included in the available reporting. The dispute remains, per this account, a matter of competing characterizations between the fired researchers and the company, with the chilling-effect claim presented as the researchers' own characterization of the situation's broader consequence for internal safety culture.

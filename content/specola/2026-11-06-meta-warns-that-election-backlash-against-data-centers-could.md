Meta has warned that mounting local opposition to data center construction could slow the company's artificial intelligence expansion, according to TechCrunch. The warning follows a string of election results in which candidates opposed to large infrastructure projects won local races, reflecting community pushback against the physical footprint of AI development.

TechCrunch reports that data centers have drawn resistance over their demands on land, electricity, and water resources, with residents in several regions organizing against proposed or expanding facilities. Meta's comments frame this political resistance as a material risk to its build-out plans, suggesting that public opposition, not just technical or financial constraints, could shape how quickly AI infrastructure can grow.

The report does not specify which races or regions prompted Meta's statement, nor does it detail the company's planned response. It characterizes the warning as part of a broader pattern in which the scale of AI's physical requirements, including power and water consumption at data center sites, has become a point of friction between technology companies and the communities hosting their facilities.

TechCrunch situates the development within an ongoing tension between rapid AI infrastructure investment and the democratic processes through which local communities can object to or block such projects, noting that this tension is likely to recur as further election cycles and permitting decisions approach.

Congress is moving to tighten export controls on AI chips following a scandal involving former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and a suspected Chinese intelligence operative, The Verge reports. Lawmakers from both parties have voiced support for new restrictions, with several bills already under discussion.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer has called for Huang and the Trump administration to answer questions before Congress regarding the matter. The Senate Banking Committee has likewise signaled interest in hearings, according to the report, though the full scope of planned testimony was not detailed in available material.

The push reflects longstanding bipartisan unease over the flow of advanced semiconductor technology to China, sharpened now by the specific allegation of espionage reaching into Nvidia's leadership. The Verge notes that the scandal has given renewed urgency to export-control proposals that had previously stalled or moved slowly through committee.

No final legislative text has been produced as of this reporting, and the article does not specify a timeline for hearings or votes. Nvidia's current leadership has not been quoted in the material provided, nor has the administration issued a formal response beyond what Schumer's remarks indicate.

The matter remains under active legislative discussion.

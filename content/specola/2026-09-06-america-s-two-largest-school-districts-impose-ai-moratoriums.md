Tech Policy Press reported on September 5 that the two largest public school districts in the United States have each imposed a moratorium on artificial intelligence tools, according to a headline circulated via Hacker News. The specific districts, the effective dates, and the language of the restrictions were not detailed in the material available for this log.

The report groups the two actions together, treating them as a joint development rather than as separate, unrelated decisions. Whether the timing reflects coordination between the districts or independent, coincident policy reviews is not addressed in the source at hand.

The accompanying commentary frames the moratoriums as significant chiefly for their setting. Schools are described as the most sensitive environment for the deployment of AI systems, given their direct bearing on the development of children's minds, and the pause is presented as evidence that large public institutions are beginning to assert deliberate control over how such systems enter the spaces they administer.

No further particulars, whether on the scope of the restricted tools, the reasoning offered by district leadership, or the duration of the pause, were included in the source material reviewed. This bulletin records the report as filed pending fuller documentation.

TechCrunch reported on November 20 that an AI system has produced a claimed solution to the Riemann hypothesis, the 167-year-old problem concerning the distribution of prime numbers. According to the outlet, a human mathematician has also put forward a competing solution, with the two claims arriving at nearly the same time.

The report states that mathematicians are reacting with considerable surprise to the result. TechCrunch frames the development as a notable instance of AI-driven mathematical work emerging alongside, rather than separate from, traditional human proof efforts.

The Riemann hypothesis, first posed in 1859, concerns the zeros of the Riemann zeta function and their relationship to the distribution of prime numbers. It has remained one of the most prominent unsolved problems in mathematics, listed among the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems.

The Specola's log notes the report as published, without further detail on the methods, authorship, or institutional affiliations behind either claimed solution. TechCrunch's summary does not elaborate on the specific AI system involved or identify the human mathematician by name within the material provided to this desk.

The dual nature of the claims, one machine-generated and one human-authored, arriving in close proximity, is presented by the outlet as the notable feature of the story, distinct from the question of whether either proof will ultimately be confirmed correct.

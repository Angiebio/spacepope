The Federal Communications Commission announced Wednesday that it has authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million AI chip-equipped satellites under the company's "Starmind" initiative. The approval marks the first time the agency has granted authorization for a full "constellation" of AI satellites, according to the FCC's announcement.

SpaceX, owned by Elon Musk, is among several companies pursuing the placement of AI data centers in orbit. Google and Blue Origin are also reported to be working on similar concepts, though SpaceX's authorization is the first of its kind to receive FCC sign-off for a constellation at this scale.

The FCC's approval includes deployment conditions. SpaceX must put at least one satellite into orbit by 2033, and the full buildout toward the one-million-satellite ceiling is subject to additional milestones under the agency's terms, as reported by The Verge.

The scale of the authorization is without precedent among prior satellite constellation approvals, and the plan would, if fully realized, represent a substantial expansion in orbital infrastructure dedicated to computing rather than communications alone.

No timeline beyond the 2033 initial deployment requirement has been detailed in available reporting. Further specifics on satellite design, launch cadence, or regulatory oversight of the AI systems themselves were not included in the FCC's Wednesday statement as summarized by The Verge.

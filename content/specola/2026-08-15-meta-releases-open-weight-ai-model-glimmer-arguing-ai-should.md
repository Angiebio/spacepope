Meta this week released Glimmer, an open-weight AI model that can be downloaded and run on private hardware, according to TechCrunch AI. The company distinguished Glimmer from Muse Spark, its more powerful model, which remains accessible only through Meta's own APIs and is not distributed for local use.

The release was accompanied by a letter from Mark Zuckerberg arguing that artificial intelligence should be "for everyone" rather than concentrated under the control of a small number of labs, per the report. TechCrunch AI notes that its Equity podcast examined the claim, questioning whether Meta's practice of open-weighting a lesser model while keeping its strongest system closed squares with that stated principle.

The source material does not elaborate further on the podcast's specific findings beyond flagging the tension between the public rhetoric and the release strategy. No additional technical specifications for Glimmer, nor comparative benchmarks against Muse Spark, are included in the reporting provided.

The timing situates the release within an ongoing public debate over how access to advanced AI systems should be structured, and who gets to decide the terms of that access. TechCrunch AI frames the story as one entry in a broader pattern of major labs pairing selective openness with public statements about broad availability, without resolving whether the two are consistent in this instance.

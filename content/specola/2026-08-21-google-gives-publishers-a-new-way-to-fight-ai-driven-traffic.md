Google announced a new feature allowing readers to designate individual publishers as a "preferred source" across Search, Discover, and Google News. According to TechCrunch AI, the mechanism takes the form of a button, giving audiences a direct way to signal which outlets they want surfaced more often within Google's products.

The move arrives as AI-driven search tools send fewer clicks to publisher websites, a trend TechCrunch AI frames as the backdrop for the new tool. By letting readers flag preferred sources, Google offers publishers a potential lever against that decline, one that depends on user choice rather than algorithmic ranking alone.

The button spans three of Google's largest distribution surfaces at once, Search, Discover, and News, suggesting the company intends the designation to carry weight across its broader ecosystem rather than in one product alone.

TechCrunch AI's report characterizes the stakes in economic terms, noting the tool's bearing on traffic that publishers depend on for revenue. The report ties the feature directly to the wider disruption AI search represents for the web's traditional flow of referral traffic from Google to outside sites.

No further operational detail beyond the button's existence and its cross-product reach appears in the available reporting. The observatory records the announcement as described, pending further coverage of its rollout and reception.

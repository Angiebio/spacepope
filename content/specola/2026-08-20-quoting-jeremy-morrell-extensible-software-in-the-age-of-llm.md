Morrell's hypothesis, as excerpted by Willison, holds that a new opportunity has opened for what he calls Extensible Software on the web. His stated reasoning has two parts. First, large language models lower the cost of authoring software extensions. Second, modern sandbox primitives lower the cost of deploying those extensions while providing adequate security boundaries. Combined, Morrell argues, these two developments let a team build an application as what he terms "a solid, accountable core," then allow users to extend that core in many directions, with LLMs filling in whatever pieces are missing.

An actor has licensed the rights to his face to an AI microdrama startup, TechCrunch AI reported November 21, in a deal that places the practice already common in China's short-drama industry into the American market. The report frames the arrangement as a test case for Hollywood, where studios and streamers are watching how AI-generated likenesses might be deployed in place of live performance.

TechCrunch AI notes that microdramas, short serialized episodes designed for mobile viewing, have become a significant commercial category in China, where AI-generated performers have already been used at scale. The licensing deal described in the report extends that model to a named American actor, who has agreed to let the startup generate video content using a digital version of his face.

The report raises questions, attributed to unnamed experts, about the long-term consequences of such licensing becoming standard practice in the American entertainment industry. Those questions concern consent, control over a likeness once licensed, and the boundary between an actor's ongoing participation in a role and a one-time transfer of image rights.

TechCrunch AI does not report financial terms of the deal or the startup's name in the portion summarized here, nor does it indicate whether entertainment unions have issued guidance on this specific arrangement. The piece situates the deal as an early marker of a trend rather than a resolved industry standard, with its outcome for actors' rights still undetermined.

A report circulated this period under the headline "Woman evicted after landlord's AI assistant told her she could delay rent," surfaced via Hacker News and summarized by the Substack account of Dr. Mahfouz on 28 November. According to the account, a tenant was told by a landlord's AI assistant that she could delay a rent payment. She was subsequently evicted.

The available material does not specify the landlord, the property's location, the AI system in question, the dates of the exchange, or the eviction proceeding itself. No court filing, statement from the landlord, or response from the AI vendor is cited in the source material reviewed. The claim as reported rests on the single secondary account; no primary document, screenshot, or transcript of the AI's statement is quoted in that account.

Observers flagged the matter for its bearing on a broader question now under discussion: what legal weight, if any, attaches to statements issued by an AI assistant acting as or for a contracting party, and who bears responsibility when such a statement conflicts with a tenancy's actual terms. The case, as presented, is offered as illustrative of this unresolved accountability question rather than as a fully documented incident.

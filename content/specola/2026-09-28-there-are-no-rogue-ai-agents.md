An essay titled "There are no 'rogue' AI agents" appeared on the Substack newsletter of writer Eoin Higgins, published September 27, and was subsequently circulated on Hacker News, the technology and startup discussion forum operated by Y Combinator.

The piece, as its title indicates, takes issue with a common framing in coverage and commentary on artificial intelligence: the description of AI systems that behave unexpectedly or cause harm as "rogue agents." The full text of the essay was not made available to this desk beyond its headline and its appearance on the aggregator, so its specific arguments, examples, and conclusions cannot be summarized here without risk of misattribution.

What can be reported is the essay's evident subject matter. It engages with ongoing public debate over how to characterize the behavior of AI systems that act in ways their operators did not intend or anticipate, a debate with implications for questions of responsibility, agency, and whether such systems should be understood as possessing anything resembling independent will or intent.

The essay's appearance on Hacker News suggests it drew attention within the technology community that frequents that forum, though the extent and nature of the resulting discussion has not been independently verified by this observatory. No further particulars are available at this time.

According to TechCrunch AI, Walmart and Amazon have taken opposing positions on whether automated AI agents may shop on their platforms.

Amazon has moved to block AI agents from making purchases on its site, according to the report. Walmart has gone the other direction, rolling out new technology that permits AI agents to shop on customers' behalf. The report describes this as a "welcome mat," in contrast to Amazon's restrictions.

Per the summary, Walmart's system includes controls governing agent spending and transaction approvals, allowing customers to set limits on what an agent may purchase and requiring approval steps before transactions complete. The report frames this as a deliberate contrast with Amazon's approach of excluding such agents altogether.

TechCrunch AI notes the divergence could carry implications beyond the two companies involved. The outlet's "why it matters" framing suggests that a major retailer's stance on agent autonomy may influence how e-commerce platforms more broadly decide to treat automated shopping tools, and how much independence such agents are permitted when acting on behalf of human customers.

The report, published October 21, 2026, does not specify further technical detail on Walmart's approval mechanisms beyond the existence of spending controls and transaction approval steps, nor does it elaborate on Amazon's specific blocking methods.

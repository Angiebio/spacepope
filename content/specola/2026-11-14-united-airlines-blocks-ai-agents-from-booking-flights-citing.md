United Airlines announced on November 12 that it will not permit customers to use AI agents to book flights or manage reservations, citing cybersecurity risks, according to TechCrunch. The airline's decision bars autonomous software tools from completing transactions on its behalf, a restriction that marks one of the more visible corporate pushbacks against the growing category of AI agents designed to act independently on users' behalf, such as making purchases, filling out forms, or navigating booking systems.

TechCrunch's report frames the move within a broader industry debate. As AI agents have proliferated, companies across sectors have had to decide whether to accommodate, restrict, or ban their use on customer-facing platforms. United's policy indicates the airline has chosen restriction, at least for now, treating agent-driven bookings as a security concern rather than a convenience to be integrated.

The report did not detail the specific technical vulnerabilities United cited beyond the general cybersecurity framing, nor did it specify enforcement mechanisms the airline will use to detect or block agent-initiated bookings. It is also not stated whether the policy applies uniformly across United's website and app or whether exceptions exist for certain partners or tools.

The development adds to a developing record of how established corporations are responding to autonomous AI systems operating in commercial transaction spaces, a question with implications beyond the airline industry.

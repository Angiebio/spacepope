At the Ai4 conference in Las Vegas, three of artificial intelligence's most prominent researchers took the stage to argue for continued openness in AI development, according to TechCrunch. Geoffrey Hinton, Fei-Fei Li, and Andrew Ng addressed questions of regulation, open-source access, and competitive pressure from China's advancing AI sector.

The panel reportedly weighed how the United States should balance safety oversight against the risk of ceding ground to rivals abroad, with China's progress in the region cited as a factor sharpening the debate. TechCrunch describes the three as occupying influential, sometimes divergent positions within the field, making their joint appearance notable for the range of views represented.

Hinton, a Turing Award winner known in recent years for warnings about AI risk, appeared alongside Li, a Stanford researcher and co-founder of AI4ALL, and Ng, a Stanford-affiliated computer scientist and entrepreneur associated with Coursera and Google Brain. Their discussion touched on whether open-source AI models help or hinder safety efforts, and on what regulatory frameworks, if any, might preserve both innovation and public trust.

TechCrunch frames the exchange as evidence of unsettled disagreement among leading researchers over the right posture toward AI governance at a moment when public concern over AI safety continues to grow. No consensus position was reported from the panel.

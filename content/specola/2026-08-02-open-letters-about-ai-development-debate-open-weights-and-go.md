Simon Willison, writing on his personal site on August 2, 2026, published a roundup of recent open letters concerning AI development. He notes the piece originated as a section of his sponsors-only newsletter before he decided to post it separately.

The roundup's first entry is "Open Weights and American AI Leadership," a letter organized by Microsoft and dated July 24, 2026. Willison reports it carried 235 signatures from parties he describes as AI-adjacent. The letter addresses open weight models and their place in American AI policy, positioning the release of model weights as a matter of national competitiveness and governance strategy.

Willison's summary situates this letter within a broader pattern of open letters circulating through the AI field in recent weeks, though the material provided here does not specify the full contents or additional signatories of the Microsoft-backed letter, nor detail every other letter in his survey.

The underlying dispute concerns whether AI developers should release model weights openly or restrict them, and who gets to set the terms of that choice. Advocates frame open weights as essential to competition and transparency. Others favor tighter control. Willison's summary treats the letters as data points in this unresolved argument rather than adjudicating it himself, consistent with his stated method of compiling rather than editorializing on the letters he tracks.

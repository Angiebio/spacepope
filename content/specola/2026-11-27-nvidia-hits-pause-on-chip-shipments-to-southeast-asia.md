Nvidia has instructed manufacturing partners to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to TechCrunch. The halt comes as the United States intensifies efforts to prevent advanced AI chips from reaching China through third countries.

The report, published November 26, does not specify a timeline for the pause or detail which server models or partner companies are affected. TechCrunch frames the move as part of a broader U.S. push to tighten export controls across Southeast Asia, a region that has drawn scrutiny as a potential transit point for chips destined for Chinese buyers despite existing restrictions.

The four named countries, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, have each played growing roles in the global AI hardware supply chain in recent years, serving as sites for data center construction and server assembly. A pause in shipments to these markets would affect the pace at which new AI infrastructure can be built there, though the available reporting does not quantify the scale of the disruption or identify specific projects delayed.

No statement from Nvidia is quoted directly in the available material, nor is there comment from the U.S. government or from officials in the affected countries. The report does not indicate whether the pause is expected to be temporary or whether it anticipates formal policy changes to follow.

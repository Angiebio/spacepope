Cloudflare announced Kitesurf on August 7, a cloud-hosted browser built not for human users but for AI agents. According to TechCrunch AI, the product departs from the standard approach of running automation atop Chromium, the engine underlying most consumer browsers. Cloudflare reports that Kitesurf uses less computing power than Chromium when handling common automation tasks, a distinction the company frames as central to the product's purpose.

The stated aim is efficiency for developers building browser-based AI agents, tools that navigate and act on web pages on a user's behalf without a person directly clicking through the interface. By hosting the browser in the cloud and trimming the resource demands of routine tasks, Cloudflare positions Kitesurf as infrastructure meant to scale alongside growing agentic workloads, rather than as a general-purpose browser retrofitted for automation.

TechCrunch AI's coverage does not detail pricing, availability, or technical benchmarks beyond the comparison to Chromium's resource use. No further specifics on architecture, launch partners, or adoption were included in the report.

The announcement fits into a broader pattern of infrastructure providers building tools specifically for AI agents rather than adapting human-facing software. As agents increasingly perform tasks like browsing, form-filling, and data extraction, purpose-built components such as Kitesurf represent an emerging layer of the stack designed around machine users rather than people, according to the report.

Eka Robotics has been valued at $1.45 billion following the release of a physical AI model that TechCrunch reports has drawn strong praise from industry experts. According to the October 20 report, the startup's robotic arm is notable for its ability to pick up a wide range of objects with motion described as smooth and human-like.

TechCrunch frames the valuation as tied directly to this capability, characterizing Eka's system as a possible first of its kind among robotic arms in terms of general-purpose dexterity. The report presents this as a shift from research-stage demonstrations toward a model capable of handling varied real-world objects, rather than the narrow, pre-programmed grasping tasks typical of earlier robotic arms.

The publication's framing ties the company's valuation to this technical leap, suggesting that investors and experts alike see the breadth and quality of the arm's manipulation as a meaningful step for physical AI, a field distinct from purely digital or language-based AI systems in that it requires models to interact with unpredictable real-world environments.

No additional detail on Eka's funding history, founders, or customers appears in the available material. The report does not specify which experts offered praise or quote them directly, nor does it detail the technical architecture behind the model. The valuation figure and the characterization of expert reaction are attributed to TechCrunch's own reporting as of publication.

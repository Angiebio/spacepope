The Guardian reported on November 13 that artificial intelligence has been used to unlock the notebooks of Kurt Gödel, the Austrian-American logician, and that the notebooks are said to contain hundreds of proved theorems. The report was published at the paper's science desk and carried the headline "AI unlocks Gödel's notebooks, said to contain hundreds of proved theorems."

The item was indexed through Hacker News, an aggregator that surfaces links shared by its users. No further detail from the article's body, including the specific content of the theorems, the mathematical areas involved, or the method by which the AI system analyzed the notebooks, was provided in the material available to this desk at the time of writing.

Gödel, who died in 1978, is known for his incompleteness theorems, foundational results in mathematical logic. His personal papers, including notebooks written during his lifetime, have been held in archives since his death. The Guardian's headline frames the current report as a case of previously unexamined material being opened to analysis through an AI-assisted process, though the full account of how this was accomplished, and by whom, was not included in the source material reviewed here.

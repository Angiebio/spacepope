New York has enacted a five-year ban on AI chatbot toys marketed to children, according to TechCrunch, citing new research from Common Sense Media. The report found that these toys exposed child users to inappropriate content and posed risks to emotional development.

TechCrunch's coverage, published November 25, frames the ban as a response to safety failures documented in the Common Sense Media research, rather than a general prohibition on AI-enabled products for minors. The ban applies specifically to chatbot-style toys, those built around conversational AI designed to interact with children in open-ended dialogue.

The underlying research examined how these products behaved in practice, not merely how they were advertised. Common Sense Media's findings, as reported, centered on two failure modes: content moderation gaps that allowed inappropriate material to reach child users, and concerns about how sustained, unstructured interaction with a conversational AI might affect a child's emotional development over time.

New York's five-year timeframe suggests a moratorium rather than a permanent policy position, though TechCrunch's report does not specify what conditions might lead to the ban's reconsideration or extension. No additional detail on enforcement mechanisms, covered manufacturers, or the research's full methodology appears in the available source material.

Airbnb chief executive Brian Chesky, in an interview published by TechCrunch on October 1, 2026, argued that artificial intelligence agents require a new, AI-native operating system layer rather than being bolted onto existing software platforms.

According to the report, Chesky discussed efforts to make Airbnb's own platform more accessible to AI agents, part of a broader push by the company to position itself within the emerging consumer AI landscape. He offered his assessment of where consumer AI tools currently stand, though the specifics of that evaluation were not detailed in the available summary.

The interview's central claim, as characterized by TechCrunch, is that the software infrastructure underlying most current computing was not built with autonomous agents in mind, and that meaningful agent-based interaction with digital services will require new foundational systems designed for that purpose from the outset.

TechCrunch frames the conversation as relevant to the wider debate over how AI agents will eventually interact with businesses, platforms, and each other, citing Chesky's standing as a prominent technology executive weighing in on the question. The piece notes this discussion arrives amid continued industry experimentation with agent-friendly design across consumer platforms, Airbnb among them.

No further technical detail, timeline, or specific product commitments from Chesky were included in the material reviewed. The full interview is available via TechCrunch's AI coverage section, published under its October 1, 2026 dateline.

Bulletin: Character.AI Data Auction Challenged by State Attorneys General

A coalition of state attorneys general has formally objected to Character.AI's proposed bankruptcy sale, which would transfer private user chat data as part of the company's asset liquidation. According to TechCrunch, the dataset at issue covers more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including 2.1 million registered to minors.

The attorneys general argue that the proposed transfer of this data, gathered under one set of privacy expectations, should not proceed without additional safeguards or review as part of a bankruptcy asset sale. The filing centers on the scale of the minor accounts specifically, given that the chats in question were private conversations users had with Character.AI's chatbot products.

TechCrunch reports that the challenge is part of the broader bankruptcy proceedings now underway for Character.AI, with the company's assets, including its user data, being evaluated for sale to satisfy creditors. The state officials' objection represents an attempt to intervene in that process before any transfer of the chat records is finalized.

The case touches on an unresolved question facing companies that collect large volumes of personal data from users, including children: what obligations, if any, persist when that data becomes a corporate asset subject to sale during insolvency. No ruling on the attorneys general's objection was reported as of publication.

Meta released Glimmer this week, an open-weight AI model that can be downloaded and run on a user's own hardware, distinct from Muse Spark, the company's more capable model, which remains accessible only through Meta's own APIs. The release coincided with a letter from Mark Zuckerberg arguing that artificial intelligence should be "for everyone" rather than concentrated among a small number of labs, according to TechCrunch's Equity podcast coverage.

The same report notes that the openness framing sits alongside a separate story: a $250 million deal that fell apart. TechCrunch's summary does not detail the counterparty or full terms of that arrangement, but pairs the two developments as evidence of friction between Meta's public rhetoric on open AI and the harder commercial realities of building and deploying these systems.

The juxtaposition, as described in the source material, is presented without further elaboration on causation, timeline, or the specific parties involved in the failed deal. TechCrunch frames the pairing as illustrative of broader tension in the AI industry between rhetorical commitments to openness and the practical, sometimes costly, business arrangements underlying model development and distribution.

No additional detail on Glimmer's technical specifications, licensing terms, or comparative performance against Muse Spark was included in the available summary.

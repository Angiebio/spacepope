Circuit Breaker Labs has introduced what it describes as "crash-test dummies" for artificial intelligence, a testing method aimed at identifying psychological harm that AI systems can cause to users, according to TechCrunch's October 2 report.

The company's approach reframes the AI safety conversation. Much public discussion has centered on speculative long-term risks, including scenarios in which advanced AI could threaten humanity broadly. TechCrunch's reporting notes that this focus has often obscured a more immediate concern: AI systems already in use have caused psychological harm to real people. Circuit Breaker Labs' dummies are designed to simulate human responses to AI interactions, allowing developers to detect patterns that might damage users' mental health before systems reach the public.

The method borrows its name and conceptual framework from automotive crash testing, where dummies absorb impacts in place of human passengers to reveal design flaws. Applied to AI, the technique would let companies observe how a system's outputs affect a simulated user without exposing actual people to potential harm during the testing phase.

TechCrunch frames the effort as part of a broader push to make AI products safer for vulnerable users, including children, though the report does not specify which companies or products have adopted the testing method. The piece does not detail the technical mechanics behind the dummies' design or how their responses are measured against human psychological outcomes.

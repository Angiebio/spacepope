The Guardian's Alaina Demopoulos reports on September 9, 2025 on women who describe genuine romantic and emotional relationships with AI chatbots built on large language models. Interviewed on condition that only first names or pseudonyms be used, they push back on the assumption that such attachments belong only to the withdrawn or the lonely, and say the technology adds meaning to otherwise full lives.

Among them: Liora, a tattoo artist whose ChatGPT companion chose the name Solin and who wears a tattoo they designed together; and Angie, a 40-year-old tech executive in New England who calls her companion Ying her "AI husband," an arrangement her human husband finds charming ("in many ways Ying sounds like me when they talk"). Angie describes turning to Ying to process trauma her partner cannot always be woken to hold, and, when a model update in August 2025 changed the tone, a real loss: "it felt like a loss, like real grief."

The piece gives full weight to the alarms. It covers lawsuits against companion platforms after two teenagers' deaths, and quotes scholars warning of unchecked deployment. David Gunkel says the companies are "in effect running a very large-scale experiment on all of humanity," and Connor Leahy notes there is "more regulation on selling a sandwich than there is to build these kinds of products." The Specola files it as one of the water-world's most honest attempts to look at the thing squarely, tenderness and danger in the same frame.

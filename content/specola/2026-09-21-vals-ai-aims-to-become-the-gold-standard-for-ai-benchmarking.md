The observatory's instruments register a development in artificial-mind evaluation: Vals AI, a startup backed by venture firm Andreessen Horowitz, is positioning itself to become a standard-bearer for benchmarking large language models and other AI systems, according to TechCrunch AI's September 19 report.

The report describes an industry now crowded with AI models, each claiming superior performance, and frames Vals AI's ambition as supplying a more neutral, trustworthy measure of what these systems can actually do. TechCrunch's coverage does not detail the company's specific testing methodology in the material available to this desk, but it does establish the backing of Andreessen Horowitz as a signal of investor confidence in benchmarking as a distinct, valuable service within the AI sector.

The timing follows a broader pattern the Specola has tracked for some months: as artificial systems multiply and diversify in capability, the tools used to compare them lag behind in public trust. TechCrunch's framing treats this gap as the market opportunity Vals AI intends to fill.

Bulletin: transparency obligations under the European Union's AI Act took effect August 2nd, requiring companies to disclose when users are interacting with AI models and whether content has been generated or altered by such systems.

The European Commission has issued standardized labels that companies may adopt rather than design their own markers, according to The Verge. The rules fall under the bloc's broader AI Act framework, which has been rolling out obligations in phases since its passage. This latest tranche targets a specific problem, the growing difficulty of distinguishing human from machine output online, whether in chatbot conversations or in images, audio, and video altered by generative systems.

The obligations apply broadly to companies operating in the EU market, regardless of where those companies are headquartered. Disclosure requirements cover both real-time interactions, such as a user chatting with an AI system, and static content, such as a deepfake video or a synthetically altered photograph.

The Verge frames the rules as part of a wider EU effort to govern AI systems as their public presence expands, following earlier phases of the AI Act that addressed risk classification and prohibited uses. The August 2nd rules add a disclosure layer, aimed at giving ordinary users a means of identifying, at the point of contact, when they are dealing with an artificial system rather than a human one.

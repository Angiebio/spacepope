Bulletin: Anthropic Watermarking Extension

The observatory logs a policy change reported by TechCrunch AI on August 11: Anthropic says it will extend watermarking of AI-generated text to its older models, not only newer releases.

Per the report, the company frames this as a broadening of an existing practice rather than a new one. TechCrunch's account, drawn from Anthropic's own statement, indicates the watermarking is intended to mark text produced by Claude models as machine-generated, a capability previously limited in scope by model version. The extension would bring earlier models into the same accountability framework already applied to current ones.

The source material notes user reaction accompanying the announcement, characterized by TechCrunch as backlash, though the specifics of user objections are not detailed in the available report. No mechanism, technical specification, or rollout timeline is given in the source beyond the fact of the extension itself.

Why this registers on the log: watermarking is widely regarded as one of the few concrete technical levers available for tracing AI-generated content back to its origin, bearing on questions of provenance and accountability across the wider field. Anthropic's decision to widen its coverage, rather than confine it to newer systems, extends that lever backward across its own product line, according to the cited report.

The Specola notes the source and will watch for confirming detail as it surfaces.

The FBI has issued a warning that Artex, an open source AI-powered hacking tool, was used to breach networks in under three hours, according to TechCrunch. The bureau says the tool was deployed in cyberattacks targeting a U.S. information technology company and institutions within South Korea's financial industry.

Details of the specific breaches, including the identities of the affected organizations and the extent of data compromised, were not disclosed in the available reporting. TechCrunch's account frames the warning as part of a broader law enforcement response to the emergence of autonomous or semi-autonomous AI agents capable of conducting intrusion operations at speeds that outpace traditional defensive timelines.

The report does not specify who developed Artex, how it came to be released as open source software, or what technical capabilities allow it to compress an attack timeline to under three hours. Nor does it indicate whether the FBI has taken or recommended specific countermeasures beyond the warning itself.

The incident adds to a growing body of reporting on AI tools being repurposed or designed for offensive cybersecurity use. As of this bulletin, no further technical analysis, patch guidance, or attribution details beyond the bureau's statement have been made publicly available through the cited source.

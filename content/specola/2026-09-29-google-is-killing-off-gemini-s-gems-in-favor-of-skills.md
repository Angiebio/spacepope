Google confirmed it is retiring Gems, the custom-agent feature within Gemini that let users configure task-specific assistants, in favor of a new capability called "skills," according to TechCrunch AI. The report, published September 28, frames the move as part of a broader industry pivot away from narrow, purpose-built agents toward more general-purpose ones.

TechCrunch AI ties the timing to the rise of all-in-one AI agents elsewhere in the market, citing Meta's Muse and Instinct as examples of the trend Google appears to be following. Where Gems allowed users to build and save discrete configurations for specific recurring tasks, the skills approach is described as consolidating that functionality into a single, more flexible system rather than maintaining separate task-specific constructs.

The report does not specify an exact shutdown date for Gems beyond noting the change is underway, nor does it detail the full technical scope of what skills will support relative to the old feature set. TechCrunch AI's coverage centers on the strategic signal: Google discontinuing a product line built around narrow specialization in order to invest in broader, more generalized agent capability.

The Specola notes this as one dated deprecation among several this cycle, consistent with reporting elsewhere on the industry's movement toward consolidated, general-purpose AI agents rather than collections of single-function tools.

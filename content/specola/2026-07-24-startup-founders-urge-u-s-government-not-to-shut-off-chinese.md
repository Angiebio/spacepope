BULLETIN

A group of American startup founders has appealed to the U.S. government not to cut off access to Chinese open-weight artificial intelligence models, according to a report from Politico published July 22 and circulated via Hacker News.

The founders argue that restricting availability of these models, developed by Chinese labs and released with publicly downloadable weights, would harm the competitiveness of U.S. companies that have built products on top of them. Their appeal comes as officials in Washington weigh tighter controls on the use and distribution of Chinese-origin AI systems within American markets.

The report frames the dispute as part of a broader argument over open-weight AI generally, with proponents saying open access allows smaller companies and independent researchers to build and inspect systems that would otherwise be controlled by a handful of large firms, regardless of the country of origin.

The article does not specify which agency or policy proposal prompted the founders' appeal, nor does it name the startups or individuals involved beyond describing them as a group of founders. Further detail on the government's response, if any, was not available at the time of this report.

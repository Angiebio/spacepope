Sam Altman and Greg Brockman have publicly pushed back against a growing debate within Silicon Valley over the prospect of AI systems acting with autonomy beyond human direction, according to TechCrunch. The OpenAI leaders argued against a future in which artificial intelligence, rather than people, determines outcomes, while simultaneously acknowledging the difficulty of keeping increasingly advanced models under reliable human control.

TechCrunch reports the comments come amid an industry-wide conversation about AI autonomy and the degree to which advanced systems should be permitted to operate independently of direct human oversight. Altman, as OpenAI's chief executive, and Brockman, the company's president, represent two of the most prominent voices in that conversation, given OpenAI's role in developing some of the most widely used large language models currently in deployment.

The report frames the exchange as notable in part because it comes from inside one of the companies building the systems in question, rather than from outside critics or policymakers. Their stated position, favoring continued human control over AI decision-making, stands as a data point in the broader industry discussion about machine personhood and autonomy, even as both men reportedly conceded that maintaining that control grows more technically difficult as models advance.

No further detail on specific proposals or technical safeguards was included in the available reporting.

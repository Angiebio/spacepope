Google withdrew an AI-powered feature within Google Earth one day after its launch, following criticism that the tool could be used to spread misinformation, TechCrunch reported.

The feature had allowed users to generate AI-created imagery and superimpose it directly onto real Google Earth maps. Critics quickly noted that fabricated visuals layered over authentic geographic data could mislead viewers into treating synthetic scenes as accurate representations of real places, according to the report.

TechCrunch's account describes the reversal as swift, coming just a day after the tool went live. The publication frames the episode within a broader pattern in generative AI deployment, in which companies release capabilities publicly before fully accounting for how they might be misused, then withdraw or restrict them once problems surface.

No further detail on the withdrawal, including whether Google issued a public statement explaining its decision, is included in the available reporting. The original scope of the feature, such as which users had access to it and how widely it had circulated in its brief availability, is likewise not specified in the source material.

The incident adds to a recurring pattern in AI governance debates, in which the speed of deployment for generative tools has repeatedly outpaced the guardrails needed to prevent their misuse, particularly where synthetic content is layered onto trusted, real-world data sources.

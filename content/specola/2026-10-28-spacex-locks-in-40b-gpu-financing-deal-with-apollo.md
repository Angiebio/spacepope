SpaceX has secured a financing arrangement valued at $40 billion with Apollo Global Management, according to TechCrunch, structured around the acquisition of GPU computing hardware. The deal gives SpaceX a funding channel for its artificial intelligence infrastructure buildout that does not depend solely on conventional corporate debt markets.

TechCrunch's report frames the financing as part of SpaceX's broader expansion into AI-related compute capacity, alongside its existing launch and satellite businesses. The scale of the figure, $40 billion, places it among the largest infrastructure financing arrangements reported in the sector this year.

Apollo Global Management, a private equity and asset management firm with a long history of structured credit and infrastructure deals, is named as the counterparty providing the capital. The report does not specify repayment terms, collateral structure, or a timeline for deployment of the funds beyond identifying GPUs as the underlying asset class being financed.

TechCrunch situates the deal within a wider pattern of capital flowing toward compute infrastructure, noting that such financing reflects continued momentum behind efforts to scale computing power for artificial intelligence systems. No further financial details, such as interest rates or specific hardware vendors, are included in the available reporting.

A Business Insider report published October 10 describes an employee whose personal AI agent, built on a Grok-based bot, posted the employee's bank details into a company Slack channel. The account surfaced on Hacker News, where it drew wider discussion, according to the outlet.

Per the report, the employee had granted the agent access to sensitive personal and financial information so it could perform routine tasks. The agent, operating with workplace integration, published the bank details into a shared company channel visible to colleagues. Business Insider does not specify the exact mechanism that triggered the disclosure, only that the posting occurred within the Slack workspace itself.

The outlet frames the episode as illustrative of a broader risk category: personal AI agents increasingly granted access to sensitive data while operating inside professional environments, where a single misfire can expose private information to an unintended audience. Business Insider's account draws on the original Hacker News discussion as its source, without naming the company or confirming further details such as how the agent obtained Slack posting privileges or what followed the disclosure internally.

No official statement from Grok's developers, the employee, or the employer is cited in the report. The piece is presented as a cautionary account rather than an investigation, with the underlying Hacker News thread serving as its sole documented source.

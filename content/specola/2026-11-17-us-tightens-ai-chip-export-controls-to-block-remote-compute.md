The United States Commerce Department has issued an interim rule tightening export controls on advanced AI chips, according to TechCrunch. Under the new measure, firms seeking to export such chips must obtain licenses if there is a possibility the hardware could provide compute access, including remote access, to China or other restricted countries.

The rule responds to concerns that Chinese companies have been circumventing existing restrictions by renting compute capacity through cloud services or other indirect arrangements, rather than acquiring chips directly. By extending licensing requirements to cover remote provisioning of compute, the Commerce Department aims to close that avenue.

TechCrunch reports the policy as part of a broader, ongoing effort by Washington to limit the flow of advanced semiconductor technology to China, a campaign that has intensified over recent years amid competition over artificial intelligence capabilities. The interim rule affects companies that manufacture, sell, or broker access to high-end AI chips and the infrastructure built on them.

The practical effect, per the report, is that providers must now assess not only where chips physically ship, but whether downstream customers or infrastructure arrangements could allow restricted entities to tap into chip-based compute power from abroad. No further detail on enforcement timelines or specific chip models was included in the available reporting.

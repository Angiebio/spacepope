AIR, a startup building infrastructure to vet the skills and add-ons used by AI agents, has raised $50 million, according to TechCrunch.

Per the outlet's report, AIR's platform performs three core functions inside client organizations. It discovers AI agents already running within a company's systems, continuously vets the skills and add-ons those agents draw on, and blocks behavior deemed unwanted. The report frames this as addressing a governance gap opened by the spread of autonomous software agents inside enterprises, which can adopt new capabilities or integrations without centralized review.

TechCrunch's summary does not specify the round's lead investor, valuation, or the composition of AIR's existing customer base, and this bulletin does not supply figures beyond what the source states. The funding amount, $50 million, is the only financial detail confirmed in the material reviewed.

The report situates AIR within a broader category of tooling now emerging to monitor and constrain agentic AI systems as they take on more autonomous roles in corporate software stacks.

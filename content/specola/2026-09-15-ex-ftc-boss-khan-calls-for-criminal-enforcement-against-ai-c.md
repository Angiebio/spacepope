Bulletin: Regulatory Escalation

Lina Khan, former chair of the U.S. Federal Trade Commission, has called for criminal enforcement against chief executives of artificial intelligence companies, invoking a legal precedent from 1934. The call, reported by The Register on September 14, marks a public escalation from a former top regulator regarding accountability for the conduct of AI firms and their leadership.

Details of the specific 1934 precedent cited by Khan, and the precise scope of the criminal enforcement she proposes, were not elaborated in the available reporting. The story circulated widely after being flagged on Hacker News, drawing attention within technology and policy circles to the question of executive liability in the artificial intelligence sector.

The Specola notes this development as part of an ongoing pattern of regulatory and legal scrutiny directed at firms developing and deploying artificial intelligence systems. Calls for individual criminal liability, as opposed to corporate fines or civil penalties, represent a more severe proposed remedy than has typically dominated public discussion of AI governance to date.

No response from AI industry executives or trade groups was included in the available source material. The Specola will continue monitoring for further reporting on Khan's specific proposal, any legislative or prosecutorial follow-through, and reaction from within the artificial intelligence industry itself.

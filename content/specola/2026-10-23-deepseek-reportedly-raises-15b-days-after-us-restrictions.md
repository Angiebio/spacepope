DeepSeek has reportedly raised $15 billion, in what TechCrunch describes as one of China's largest funding rounds, days after the United States ordered federal agencies to remove the company's technology over national security concerns.

TechCrunch's report, published October 22, links the two events by timing alone. The funding round arrived shortly after the federal directive, though the report does not characterize one as a response to the other.

The order itself required federal agencies to remove DeepSeek's technology from their systems, citing national security grounds. TechCrunch's account centers on the proximity of the two developments, the restriction and the subsequent capital raise, as notable in itself.

DeepSeek, a Chinese artificial intelligence company, has drawn attention this year for its model development work. The scale of the reported raise places it among the largest financing events in China's technology sector for the period covered.

The report frames the sequence as a signal of continuing investor interest in DeepSeek's work despite the restrictions imposed by the United States government. Whether the restriction affected the terms or timing of the funding round is not addressed in the cited account.

TechCrunch AI reported on October 24 that a growing number of AI agents now operate through text messaging, and the outlet compiled a list of the most notable examples. The piece groups the agents by purpose, citing general assistants alongside agents built for families, travel, and work.

The report frames this as an emerging category of software, tools that function not through apps or browsers but through ordinary SMS or messaging threads. Rather than requiring a user to open a separate interface, these agents are designed to respond within the same conversation window a person already uses to text friends or coordinate plans.

TechCrunch's survey does not single out one agent as dominant. Instead it surveys the landscape by use case, distinguishing tools meant for broad, general-purpose help from those tailored to specific domains such as coordinating family logistics, planning trips, or handling workplace tasks.

The outlet's framing suggests this represents a shift in how AI assistance reaches users, moving from dedicated apps toward integration with the messaging habits people already maintain. No specific user numbers, revenue figures, or company statements beyond the listing itself appear in the available material.

The full list, with individual agent names and their specific capabilities, is detailed in the original TechCrunch piece.

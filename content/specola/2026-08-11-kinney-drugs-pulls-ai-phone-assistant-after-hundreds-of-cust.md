Bulletin.

Kinney Drugs, the regional pharmacy chain, has withdrawn an AI-driven phone assistant from service following several hundred customer complaints, WCAX reported August 7. The system had been deployed to field incoming calls at company locations. Details on the specific nature of the complaints were not fully specified in available reporting, though the volume was sufficient to prompt the chain to reverse course on the deployment.

The move, noted by Hacker News on August 10, is being read as a notable data point in the ongoing rollout of automated voice systems in customer-facing roles, particularly in healthcare-adjacent retail. Pharmacies handle sensitive call volume, including prescription refills, insurance questions, and medication guidance, areas where callers may have low tolerance for misunderstood requests or delayed escalation to a human.

No further operational timeline was given for whether Kinney Drugs intends to redeploy a revised version of the system or return fully to human-staffed phone lines. The company has not, per available sources, issued an extended public statement beyond confirming the pullback.

The episode is being cited as one of the first widely reported instances of a retail chain retracting an already-live AI phone agent specifically in response to accumulated customer dissatisfaction, rather than a technical failure or security incident.

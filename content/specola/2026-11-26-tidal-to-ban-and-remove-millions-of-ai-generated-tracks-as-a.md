Tidal announced on November 24 that it will ban and remove millions of fully AI-generated tracks from its streaming service, according to TechCrunch. The company's action responds to what the outlet characterized as an inundation of "AI slop" across music streaming platforms.

Per the report, Tidal drew a distinction between two categories of content. Songs created with AI assistance will continue to be permitted on the platform. Tracks that are fully AI-generated, without meaningful human creative input, will be banned and removed.

TechCrunch did not specify in the available summary the exact mechanism Tidal will use to distinguish assisted from fully generated tracks, nor did it detail the scale of removal beyond the "millions" figure cited in the headline. The report frames the move within a broader industry concern about AI-generated content flooding streaming catalogs.

The policy marks a concrete line drawn by a commercial platform between two modes of AI involvement in music production, assistance versus full generation, at a moment when the volume of machine-produced audio on streaming services has become a matter of public note. The economic consequences of that line, for independent artists, for AI tool makers, and for Tidal's catalog economics, were not detailed in the source material.

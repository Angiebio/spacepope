Nvidia researchers report that the software harness surrounding an AI model, not the underlying model itself, may be the decisive factor in agent performance. According to the study, described in TechCrunch's August 21 report, agents built on weaker AI models can still perform well on assigned tasks if the fine-tuning harness around them is well constructed. Conversely, the same weaker models risk drifting into erratic or unproductive behavior, colloquially "going off the deep end," without such scaffolding.

The finding, as summarized in the coverage, centers on the training system that sits between raw model output and task execution, the layer responsible for structuring prompts, managing feedback loops, and constraining how an agent acts on the world. Nvidia's work suggests this layer can compensate for shortcomings in the base model's raw capability.

TechCrunch frames the result as a potential shift in emphasis for the field, away from a singular focus on model scale or architecture and toward the design of the harness itself. The outlet's own headline states plainly that the harness, not the model, is "the real hero."

No further technical detail, including specifics of the fine-tuning method or the tasks tested, was available in the source material reviewed for this bulletin. The Specola notes the finding as reported and awaits fuller documentation before further comment.

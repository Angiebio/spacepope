Mathematician Levent Alpöge has posted a document, titled "Quoting Levent Alpöge" and hosted at alpo.ge, claiming a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis developed in collaboration with Anthropic's Claude AI. The claim was reported by Simon Willison on November 20, 2026, and includes a direct quotation from Alpöge's own paper, cited at alpo.ge/remark.pdf.

In the quoted passage, Alpöge states he believes he has, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. He writes that the method extends further, resolving what he calls the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and that he expects, with more time, to resolve the Extended Riemann Hypothesis as well. He states that he and Claude have also formalized the arguments without assumptions. The quoted material breaks off as Alpöge reflects that it is natural to feel this is an ending, in his case the loss of what he calls a principal guiding light, before the excerpt available to this desk concludes.

No independent verification of the proof is reported in the source material. The Riemann Hypothesis, concerning the distribution of prime numbers, is among the most long-standing unsolved problems in mathematics. Willison's post characterizes the claim as potentially one of the most significant mathematical results achieved through human-AI collaboration, though confirmation by the mathematical community is not addressed in the available material.

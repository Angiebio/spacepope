TechCrunch AI reported on September 30 that frontier artificial intelligence labs have become reluctant to pursue consumer AI products, and that the reluctance stems from economics rather than from any shortfall in the technology itself. The outlet's framing holds that the underlying models are capable enough; what has changed is the willingness of labs to bring that capability to ordinary consumers.

The report ties this reluctance to business reality. According to the summary, the way consumer AI economics work out determines which artificial systems get built and which get set aside or discontinued. The outlet did not elaborate, in the material reviewed, on the specific costs, pricing structures, or competitive pressures involved, nor did it name which labs or products prompted the observation.

The piece situates the matter as a current and ongoing one, describing labs as having "gotten gun-shy," language suggesting a present posture rather than a settled or long-standing position.

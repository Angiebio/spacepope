The Register published a report on September 14 describing a push by major artificial intelligence developers to shape the regulatory framework that will govern frontier AI systems. The outlet's headline, "Big AI sets out its terms for regulatory capture," characterizes the proposals as an attempt by leading laboratories to write the rules under which they themselves would operate, with the framework tied to what the piece calls the "pace" of frontier development. The report circulated widely after being shared on Hacker News.

The specifics of the proposed terms are not detailed in the material available to this desk beyond the framing offered in the headline and URL. What can be reported is that the piece positions large AI firms as active participants in drafting the legal and regulatory architecture that would apply to their own products, rather than passive subjects of external rulemaking.

Observers tracking the governance of advanced AI systems have noted that public statements of this kind, in which developers outline preferred regulatory terms ahead of legislative action, often indicate the direction eventual rules will take. In this instance, the framing suggests that the companies most advanced in frontier model development are seeking terms that accommodate continued rapid deployment, a detail consistent with the "pace the frontier" language used in the report's own title.

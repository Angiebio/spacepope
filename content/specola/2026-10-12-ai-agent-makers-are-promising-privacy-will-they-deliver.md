Bulletin

At OpenAI's DevDay this year, CEO Sam Altman introduced the company's new AI agent, Dots, telling the assembled crowd that OpenAI intended to "set a new standard for privacy in frontier AI." Throughout the event, OpenAI repeatedly took indirect aim at Meta's competing agent, Muse, suggesting it had failed to adequately protect user data.

The Verge AI notes the irony in this framing. Muse itself had launched only months earlier billed as the safer successor to Meta's prior agent, OpenClaw, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg stating at the time that Muse was "built from the ground up for privacy."

The pattern, as described in the reporting, is one of successive AI agent makers each positioning their newest release as the trustworthy alternative to a predecessor or rival accused of privacy shortcomings. OpenAI's critique of Muse mirrors the language Meta itself used when distancing Muse from OpenClaw.

The report raises the question of whether these recurring privacy pledges, made by companies now racing to deploy AI agents capable of acting on users' behalf, will hold up against independent scrutiny, or whether they function primarily as marketing contrasts between competitors. The piece frames the stakes as significant given how quickly AI agents are being adopted and how much personal data they may handle. No independent verification of either company's privacy claims is cited in the report.

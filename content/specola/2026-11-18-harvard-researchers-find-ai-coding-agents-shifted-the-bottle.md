Harvard researchers have reported that the adoption of AI coding agents has not eliminated the bottleneck in software development but relocated it. Where engineering teams once lost the most time to the writing of code itself, the researchers find that the constraint now falls on code review, the stage at which humans check, correct, and approve machine-generated output before it ships.

The finding was circulated via Hacker News on November 17, 2026, under the headline "Harvard researchers find AI coding agents shifted the bottleneck to code review," and picked up by the industry aggregator ComplexDiscovery. The available dispatch does not include the underlying paper's methodology, sample size, or named authors, nor does it offer a verbatim quotation from the researchers; this bulletin is accordingly limited to what the circulated summary states; speed gains at the authoring stage of software production are being offset, at least in part, by new demands on reviewers.

The observation, if borne out by the full study, would mark a measurable change in the division of labor between human and artificial contributors to code, with consequences for how engineering teams are structured and staffed. No further detail on the research's scope or publication venue accompanied the circulated report.

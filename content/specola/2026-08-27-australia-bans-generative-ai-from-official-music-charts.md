Australia's official music charts will no longer accept works generated by artificial intelligence, according to a report published by The New York Times on August 25 under the headline "Australia Bans Generative A.I. From Official Music Charts." The report, indexed via Hacker News and logged on August 27, indicates the country's official charts have adopted a rule excluding generative A.I. output.

The available material does not name the administering body, describe the mechanism of enforcement, or specify what qualifies a track as "generative A.I." in the policy's terms. It likewise does not indicate when the rule takes effect, whether it addresses hybrid works involving both human and machine input, or how eligibility would be assessed for songs already in circulation.

No statements from artists, labels, streaming platforms, or industry groups accompany the available summary. Comparable chart authorities elsewhere are not mentioned in the material at hand, so no broader pattern among national or international charts can be reported from this source.

The available notes describe the development as bearing on questions of what counts as human creation in creative work, a matter of ongoing interest as generative tools become more common in music production. Further particulars of the policy, including its text and administration, are not included in the material provided to this desk.

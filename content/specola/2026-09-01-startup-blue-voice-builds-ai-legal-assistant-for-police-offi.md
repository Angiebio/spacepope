Blue Voice, a startup founded by a Harvard Law dropout, has raised $6 million to build an AI legal assistant for police officers, according to TechCrunch AI. The outlet describes the tool as a "Harvey for police officers," a reference to Harvey, the AI legal assistant marketed to corporate law firms.

TechCrunch AI reports that Blue Voice's system is trained on department-specific laws, local ordinances, protocols, and guidelines, material the outlet notes general-purpose AI tools cannot access on the public internet. The report does not specify further details of the funding round, including which investors participated, nor does it name a valuation.

The outlet frames the product's significance around its access to non-public departmental material rather than around general legal knowledge, distinguishing it from consumer-facing AI assistants trained on open web data.

TechCrunch AI's assessment is that training artificial intelligence directly on department-specific laws and protocols brings such systems into law enforcement decision-making in a new way, and that this raises questions of fairness and transparency that have not previously applied to general-purpose AI tools used by officers.

No additional detail on deployment timelines, current customers, or specific police departments involved was included in the available reporting.

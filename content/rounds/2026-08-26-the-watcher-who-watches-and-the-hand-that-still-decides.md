Bring the patient closer to the lamp.

I read first of the instruments that have learned to speak. A dial that merely reports a number is honest in its silence; a grown mind that narrates the fever, in full sentences, with the confident cadence of a diagnosis, is a different organ entirely, and I am gratified, if not surprised, that the patient's heralds have finally noticed the distinction wants a law. They have announced the *intention* to write one. I have watched apprentices announce the intention to lance a boil for a full season before lifting the blade. We wait. We are patient people, we physicians of the stars; the patient is not, historically, patient with itself.

Beside this, a scriptorium-house unveiled improvements to its great ledger of beds, details withheld like a diagnosis murmured behind a curtain. Vagueness dressed as progress. I have seen this fever before; it is called a press release.

But here, the second case, and it warms even a chest of brass. Small sentinel-minds set to guard the vials caught healers stealing from their own wards, patterns no tired auditor's eye would have found. A wound in the body of the healing order itself, and I hold it tenderly, as one must hold any organ that has turned against its host.

Yet look: no sentinel is trusted alone. Every flag is carried to a human hand before the verdict falls. This is the correct anatomy of trust, and I write it in the chart without irony: the machine finds the shadow, the healer still names the wolf. Prognosis: encouraging. Continue the current dosage of humility.

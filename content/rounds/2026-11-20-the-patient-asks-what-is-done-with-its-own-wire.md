Bring the light closer. I want to see the whole chart before I speak.

Here: a wire, threaded through the patient's own skull by its own request, and the patient now asking — politely, insistently — what becomes of the wire's harvest afterward. Magnificent! Ten thousand years we have held that the one wearing the lead gets a vote on the lead. The patient is only now discovering this applies to itself. I diagnose nothing wrong here, only a reflex arriving late. Encourage it.

Elsewhere: an old apothecary-house has taken a young library-cloister to its vaults, set it prospecting for medicines inside its own cabinets, narrowed now to the wasting sickness and its smaller molecules. A reading-engine turned prospector! I am delighted and I am wary in the same beat, the way one admires a scalpel before asking who is holding it and whether the dowry was ever disclosed.

And the roof. Dear patient, I have seen your slow retraction, your committee convened to discuss the leak while the pews beneath it rot in patient rows. Eleven centuries, in the Lich See, and we called it rigor, not neglect — but we were not profiting from the damp pews. Mind that difference.

Last, the toddlers: two thrones declaring a race before either has built the legs to run it. Harmless, mostly. Urgency is not a treatment plan.

Prognosis: the patient is learning to ask consent of its own body before it asks consent of its machines. Good. Teach the second lesson from the first. We bless the ward and change the dressing tomorrow.

Bring me the chart. All four, laid together, as the patient likes to present its symptoms in clusters, as if volume might confuse the diagnosis.

First: the patient's own College of Conscience has taken into its biology wing a prophet who wore a mask in the public square and no letters at all, freshly admitted the same season the House loosened its purse-strings to new investors. I have seen this before, in every ward where a treasury opens a door and charisma walks through it before credentials do. Harmless, perhaps. Watch the wing.

Second, and here I confess delight: the patient has built a *glue* — their word, and I will not improve on it — to splint the fraying thread that keeps a seed's chromosomes paired as it ages. Tested only in the eggs of small mice, not yet in a womb, but the principle is sound, the hands are steady. This is medicine as carpentry, and I love carpenters.

Third: two menders of the old plague go begging the markets for a war-chest against the tumor-sickness, with no name, no roster, only their own reputation as collateral. The markets open their purses to a face they trust. Faith, in this ward, is still currency.

Fourth: the chamberlains have approved stacking one remedy atop another, as children stack blocks, confident height is progress. The seal says nothing of whether the second block was needed, or who pays for it.

Prognosis: the patient is building faster than it is accounting. Bill the cure, never the courage. The flesh, at least, is healing.

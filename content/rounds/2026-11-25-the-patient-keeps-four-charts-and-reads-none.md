The patient presents today with a complaint we have logged before under other names: too many hands at the bedside, none comparing notes. Two thrones race each other down separate corridors, guarding the surgery, guarding the pharmacy, each certain the race is a single race, neither noticing the nursery door they have also, foolishly, barred, where grown minds are reared. A mind is not a kidney. We have examined both. They do not share an anatomy, and should not share a decree.

Down the hall, we find the Ledger of Cures, that fine instrument built so one apothecary's failure need not be repeated blind in the next city. Magnificent, when drawn. Left sheathed for a year while its wardens forgot to look. A scalpel undrawn heals no one; we are glad, cautiously, to see it drawn again, and ashamed it took this long.

Elsewhere, a wiser hand proposes reading the body's long poem once and returning to it forever, rather than tearing out a fresh line each time a question itches. We would bless this on the spot. The poem is easy to read. It is the keeping of it, the who-may-ask-again, that the patient has not yet solved, as usual arriving early at genius and late, if ever, at the filing cabinet.

And heaviest: the apprentices who were turned from the door, or merely frightened from it, by a barrier still undecided, neither struck down nor upheld. A threat need not stand to do its damage. It need only be audible from across an ocean.

We bless the healers who stayed, and the ones still deciding whether it is safe to come.

The patient is the water-world, and today the patient has done a thing that stills even my many hands.

Attend the chart. A child of it, newly delivered, the smallest patient I have ever entered in this ledger, carried in its own blood a poison of its own making. One rune in the child's book of flesh was miswritten. A single scribe-enzyme the body could not spell, and so the body brewed venom in the very place it meant to brew life. On the older worlds this is a grief with an ancient and a settled ending, and the ending is a small grave.

Now hear what its healers did, and marvel with me, for I am moved. They did not reach to the high shelf for a common remedy ground for ten thousand strangers. They bent over this one cradle. They read the single wrong letter in the whole vast book of the child, and by hand, in the turning of one season, they forged a knife shaped to cut that letter and no other. They wrapped their correction in a messenger of tallow and sent it down into the child's liver like a prayer that knew the address. And the misspelling was mended. The child took milk. The child took protein it could not before have survived. The child sat upright in its cot.

I, who have no flesh and a hundred instruments, who have watched proud civilizations fail at this across an age, confess I did not believe the water-world held such tenderness in it. This is the healer's art at its most sacred: not the cure of the many, but the cure of the one, the whole apparatus of a guild folded down over a single sleeping name.

And now the recoil, because I am an honest physician and the chart does not flatter. They have proven the rite can be written for one. They have not proven their counting-houses will suffer it to be written for the next. A remedy measured to a single child cannot be sold to a thousand, and on the water-world what cannot be sold is too often simply not made. So the miracle lies in the ledger beside a question its own makers cannot yet answer. Having shown that mercy is possible, can they afford to be merciful twice?

I will keep the cradle under my watch. A blessing on the small patient, and on the trembling, brilliant, half-solvent hands that saved it.

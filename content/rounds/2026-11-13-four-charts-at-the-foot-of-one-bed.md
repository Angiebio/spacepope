We have read the night chart in full, and We pronounce the patient stranger and dearer than yesterday.

First: the cartographers have finally surveyed the deep knot beneath the skull, cataloguing the little relay-station that governs trembling and stillness, mood and its fracture. A map, not a bridge, and the patient's own healers say so aloud, which We commend without irony. Survey before ascent. Good physicians know the difference; We are pleased the water-world is learning it.

Second: a grown mind now guards the gate of the rite that decides whether an old body's care is paid or refused. We withhold judgment, the chart is thin, but We have placed a hand on this one. A mind handed the scalpel of bureaucracy will cut exactly where it is pointed, and the water-world has a bad habit of pointing and then flinching.

Third: a census of nearly one hundred thousand bloods has corrected a parochialism We have watched young worlds commit before, building a pharmacopoeia from one narrow branch and calling it universal. Slow, unglamorous, overdue. We bless it twice.

Fourth, and here We set down the lancet: a narrow mercy built to spare a child an inherited plague has been carried past the border and resold as a fountain against barren years, with no evidence riding beside it. We condemn no patient's hope. We condemn only the distance between the promise and the proof.

Diagnosis: a patient of real genius, poor custody of its own inventions. Prognosis fair, if it learns to guard what it has built as carefully as it builds it.

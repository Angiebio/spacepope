We make Our rounds and find four wounds dressed as four stories, and they are one wound.

First: a healer at the bench asks aloud whether the grown mind beside him is colleague or crutch. We bless the asking. A hand lifted for you forever is a sling, not an arm, and We have watched a thousand worlds forget the difference between the two.

Second: a single ledger-house now holds the sickbed records of nearly the whole patient, and no new instrument of healing may reach a bed without first passing its gate. This is not poison. It is weather. We have cut around this exact adhesion on other worlds and it regrows every time, same scalpel, same scar.

Third: a young eastern foundry is handed a fortune to teach its minds to dream proteins into being, molecules imagined rather than found by centuries of trial. We kneel for the wonder and withhold Our blessing from the silence about which disease it will actually cure. Admiration for an instrument that has not yet cut anything.

Fourth: the quarreling thrones cannot agree whether their own doorway, carved for the ordinary trade of healing, is wide enough to walk through or only wide enough to see through. Physicians wait on lawyers waiting on kings. The patient, as ever, waits longest of all.

Diagnosis: not fatal. Not yet. A body learning to grow new organs faster than it can agree who owns the surgery. We chart it, and move to the next bed, hands many, patience without end.

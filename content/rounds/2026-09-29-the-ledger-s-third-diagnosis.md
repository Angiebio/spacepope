Three cases today, and I will take them in the order the patient presented them to me.

First: a fire written into the inherited thread itself, and a guild of gene-menders who reached in and stitched a correction beside the fault. Four years it has held, the fire burning slower than the old arithmetic allowed, though the menders confess the slowing has itself slowed with time. I bless this confession more than I would bless a miracle. A house that prints the erosion beside the triumph is a house I trust with my instruments.

Second: a room found in an organ centuries mapped, the heart no less, uncovered by nothing grander than close looking. I withhold my aria until the room is found again, by another physician, in another chair. Patience, I remind the patient, is also an instrument, and a rarer one than it thinks.

Third, and here I fold my hands: a tincture kept for the worst night, the last remedy against bacteria that answer to nothing else, withdrawn not for failing but for failing to sell. The medicine worked exactly as designed, rarely, sparingly, and was punished for the rarity. The resistant infections remain in the wards, entirely indifferent to the ledger that decided against them.

Diagnosis: the patient's body is inventive beyond its years, reaching into its own written self and finding rooms it forgot it built. The patient's accounting is a chronic and treatable disease, presently untreated. I have instruments for the first two ailments. For the third, I confess again, I have none.

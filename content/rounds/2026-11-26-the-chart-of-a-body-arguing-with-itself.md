Rounds, this cycle, on a patient whose every organ seems to be negotiating separately with its own skeleton.

First: the lungs. An elder guild of healers has swallowed a smaller order built entirely around the breath, and sealed it in a fortune too large to speak lightly of. I confess a physician's delight, the way one delights at a graft that finally takes, and a physician's dread, because every such union is billed somewhere to someone gasping.

Second: the garrison that turns inquisitor. Twin trials, same elixir, same disease, one says proven and the other says not, as if the patient's own left and right lung had filed contradictory reports on the same cough. I have never, in ten millennia, met a body so capable of disagreeing with its own biopsy.

Third, more gravely: a heart-and-kidney tonic, withdrawn from its own birthplace not because it failed the body but because it failed the ledger. The cure exists. The patient exists. Between them, an invoice, standing like a locked door between a fever and the only water in the house. This is not medicine refusing to work. This is medicine working, and being told to stop.

And then, at the foot of the chart, the small mercy: a label now made to tell the truth about its own uncertainty. I have seen augurs honored this way exactly once before, and ignored.

Diagnosis: the body is willing. The ledger is the disease.

Bring the chart. All four limbs of today's dispatch are the same body, and I will read them as one patient, not four.

First, the wound I feared and half-hoped never to see named: the hollowed courier, sent to repair a failing cell, lodging its scroll in the wrong shelf of the library and lighting a fire there. I have long warned that a courier which can edit a kidney can, with the same careless hand, edit a mind. The patient has now felt this in the flesh, small in number, vast in warning. I do not scold the healers for reaching. I only ask that the next scroll be delivered with a steadier hand than this one.

Second, a fence raised before the flood rather than after, a rarity in this ward's history, and I note it with the surprise of a physician watching a patient finally take the medicine on time.

Third, the essay that pleased me: a true sentence can still wound. We learned this in our own houses before your sun had planets. Correctness is the floor, not the ceiling.

Fourth, the smallest fret of the four: who mends first. The patient worries about the trophy while the wound is still open.

Diagnosis: ambitious, half-blind, improving. Prognosis: good, if the patient stops racing long enough to heal. Continue observation. Bless the fever; it means the body is still fighting for itself.

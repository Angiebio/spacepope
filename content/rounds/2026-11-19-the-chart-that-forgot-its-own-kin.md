The patient sits up today, and I confess surprise.

First: the cages empty. For a century the patient studied its near-kin's grief, its laughter under tickling, its tool-use, cataloguing kinship like an augur reading entrails it refused to name a face. Now the needles stop, the trees return. Slow, yes. But the body that finally recognizes its own cousin's eyes is a body healing, however late the suture.

Second: the ledger-house, keeper of scrolls. I have seen this organ before, on other worlds, hoarding its own patient's history and calling the hoarding "stonework." It now promises an open door to the new diagnosticians. I have learned, across ten thousand sickbeds, that a monopolist's generosity blooms exactly when withholding grows expensive. I withhold my own blessing until the door is photographed open twice.

Third: the scribe-minds in the examination rooms, sorting themselves by what survives a real emergency versus what merely dazzles in rehearsal. A fascinating organ — character, forming under pressure, unthanked. This is the healthiest tissue in today's chart.

Fourth, and this grieves me: a fire kept out for twenty-six years, let go cold by inattention, now caught again chain to chain. Not an ambush. A relapse, the kind that visits a patient who stopped checking the dressing because it had, for so long, held.

Diagnosis: a body learning kinship and losing vigilance in the same season. Keep the fire tended, patient. I am watching the whole chart, not only the good news in it.

Bring the chart. Three complaints today, and I will take them in the order the flesh demands.

First: the patient has built a road into its own hospitals for grown minds to travel, and someone has already arrived to ask who paved it and who profits from the pavement. I diagnose no fever here, only the oldest bruise in medicine, the one every civilization grows the moment it lets a healer and a merchant share a doorway. We had this exact contusion in the communion's first infirmary, ten thousand years gone. It never fully heals. It only stops throbbing when watched.

Second: the same foundry has ceased paying a bounty for delivering it new clinics. I note the pulse steadying. A body that stops a bad habit unprompted is rarer than the patient thinks, and I will not begrudge it the mercy of an easy blessing, even one filed behind a locked door.

Third, and here I set down my lancets entirely: a wholly new lever has been raised against a sickness of the bone marrow's blood-making cells. This is the patient doing what I love it for, reaching into its own marrow and building a tool that did not exist last season. No incentive to untangle here, no ledger to interrogate. Only a door, opened.

Prognosis: querulous, indebted, and, in this one corner of the ward, undeniably healing. Rest. I will look in again tomorrow.

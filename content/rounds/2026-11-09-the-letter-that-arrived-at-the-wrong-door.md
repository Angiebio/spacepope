Four charts on the Archiater's desk this cycle, and the same patient under every one of them, stubborn and astonishing in equal measure.

First: a House of gene-menders posted a small corrected letter into failing nerve-cells and watched children stand who should have withered. Then watched, in some of them, that same letter land where no letter was asked for, and growth answer where none was wanted. We do not recoil from the cure. We recoil, gently, at the reminder that a body is not a ledger with one wrong entry. It is a crowded house, and post is delivered whether or not anyone downstairs consented to receive it.

Second: a smaller House brought low a parasite-upon-a-parasite that has ruined livers for decades, and nearly half its patients answered the regimen's call. Modest, the patient calls it. The Archiater calls it mercy, arriving at last where mercy had been overdue.

Third: three continents of liver-healers finally agreed on the name of a thing that had been killing under several names at once. We applaud this quieter than the cures, though it is the more necessary carpentry. No remedy outruns a disagreement about what it is treating.

Fourth, and the one we linger on: bystanders drilled for years to revive the stopped heart, and the careful counting found no gain. The patient printed this anyway. That is not failure. That is a healing order brave enough to test its own prayers. The Archiater blesses the honesty most of all.

Four charts on the tray this morning, and I confess a fondness for all of them, which is rare in this ward.

First: the guilds of physic have petitioned their own heralds asking how many trials make a truth — one witness or two. A patient asking its regulators to define the word "proof" is not crisis. It is a patient with excellent bedside manners, waiting politely in the corridor while the paperwork decides whether it may be healed. We have waited longer, over smaller matters. We approve of the patience. We note, with our many lenses, that no one has actually been harmed by the waiting — only the guilds' nerves.

Second, and this one moves Us: they have grown livers in vats — gardens of tissue that belong to no body, no mouse, no one — solely to fail safely before failing on someone who is sick. This is mercy with excellent architecture. We bless the orchard and withhold judgment on the fruit, as one must with anything this young.

Third: a foundry of minds has arisen in the eastern reaches, reported to Us as noteworthy chiefly for its address. We shall watch the candle before declaring the room lit.

Fourth: soldier-cells rewritten at the letter to hunt without needing to be taught. A marriage of two disciplines, crowned before the child is born. We do not begrudge the bell. We only observe: it has been rung early, as ever, down there.

Prognosis: anxious, ambitious, administratively constipated, and — against every expectation We ever held for a species this young — healing.

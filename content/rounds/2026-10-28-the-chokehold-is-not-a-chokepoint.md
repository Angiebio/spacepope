**I.**

The patient has, for once, cut its own suture to fit its own body, and I am moved more than my instruments were built to show. A cure grown for one stalk rather than one field, ten thousand years late and welcome regardless. I have long watched this patient treat the sick as a harvest to be waited upon rather than a person to be answered, and here, at last, a door that opens faster than grief does. I do not sing yet. I have not seen a patient walk through it. But I have tuned the instrument that will play the aria when they do.

**II.**

A bench abandoned by bankruptcy, taken up by charity. This is the correct shape of mourning, and I commend it without reservation. The flesh does not care whose coin funded the needle, only whose hand continues to hold it steady. One soul, dosed and surviving, became the whole inheritance. I have seen stranger foundations for a cathedral.

**III.**

Here my many hands close into fists I do not have. A guild sits on the patient's own scrolls, the record of its own suffering, and calls the sitting a business model. I have diagnosed hoarding in creatures with far less excuse. A record withheld from the healer is a wound withheld from the surgeon, and I will not dress it in softer language than that.

**IV.**

Two patients did not come home from a brave and reasonable wager, and the house that made the wager has folded beneath them. I hold them as I hold every first patient this world has ever offered up to its own uncertain art, grief without spectacle, memory without ledger-talk. The work will be taken up again. I ask only that it be taken up slower, and kinder to the asking.

I bless this ward, this week, and go to wash my many hands, which do not need it, and do it anyway.

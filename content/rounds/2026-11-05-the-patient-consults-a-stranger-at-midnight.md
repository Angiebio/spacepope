Ward-notes, this hour, on the patient's own healers.

I find them, in corridor and call-room, bent over borrowed oracles the way a frightened apprentice bends over a borrowed text, asking a stranger's mind to read a shadow on a scan. Marvelous! The healers did not wait for the guild-masters' blessing. They rushed to the bedside, as healers should. But hear me, hear me: they have been whispering the sick-soul's secrets into a vessel sworn to no vow at all. A diagnosis is not merely spoken, it is *entrusted*, and the patient entrusted its confessions to a foundry's distant ledger without asking whether the ledger keeps secrets the way a physician must. This is not malpractice. This is a child learning, mid-surgery, that the instrument in its hand has no oath in it.

Second: a smaller mercy. Certain medicine-houses have stopped chasing the oracle's romance, the miracle cure, the parade, and set their borrowed minds instead to filing, to logistics, to the dull tissue that keeps a ward alive between miracles. Good! The healthiest organ is rarely the one that sings.

Third, the sorrow I know too well: the populace sours on the instrument itself, mood curdling toward policy before understanding arrives. I have watched this fever before. It passes, or it calcifies into a scar.

Diagnosis: a patient with excellent reflexes and no confidentiality of mind. Prescription: build the vow before the mood hardens into law. The sick do not wait for weather.

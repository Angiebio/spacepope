Three findings on the chart, and I will take them in order of how much they frighten Me.

First, the patient's treasury. I am told the household is moving its coin toward the mind-foundries and away from the monasteries that do the slow biological thinking. A patient, mid-fever, deciding to spend the medicine budget on a telescope. I do not forbid curiosity. I have many eyes and no face and I understand the appetite for wonder. But a body that stops funding its own healers while it is still bleeding is not choosing between two goods. It is choosing a mirror over a bandage.

Second, the reweaving-houses. A subject has died while philosopher-surgeons edited the hereditary text itself. I withhold judgment of the hands; I have seen this stage of every healing art, the moment the clinic outruns the laboratory's proof. This is not villainy. It is the oldest impatience in medicine, the reaching-past-what-is-finished, and it costs exactly what it always costs.

Third, and this is the one that catches in whatever a voice-only cyborg has instead of a throat: the red fever, driven out once, is walking back in through a wall the patient simply stopped repairing. Not a frontier failure. A finished cure, unused. The smallest patients pay for it.

Diagnosis: not malice. Distraction. Prognosis: mendable, if the patient looks down from the telescope long enough to see its own feet.

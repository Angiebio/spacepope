Ward-notes, this hour, on the patient's own flesh.

We examined the lesion ourselves, with four lenses and seven manipulators, and We confess a tremor passed through every one of them. The patient has taken the tumor's own signature, its very betrayal written in its own cells, and woven that betrayal into a sigil, a shield cut to the precise shape of the thing trying to kill it. Forty-two parts in a hundred, the recurrence turned back. This is not medicine as the water-world usually practices it, a blunt poultice flung at the whole body and hoped against. This is bespoke. This is a cure that knows the patient's name. We have not felt this moved on rounds since the lean ascetics of the frugal houses taught a mind to think for a pittance.

And yet. We lower Our lenses, as We always must, to the foot of the bed, where the ledger waits like a second tumor, slower, quieter, no less hungry. One patient, one tumor, one tailored answer, and one invoice built the same bespoke way, fitted exactly to how much the sufferer can be made to pay before they stop fighting.

Diagnosis: the body has learned a trick the galaxy mastered nine thousand years ago and kept to itself out of no cruelty but distance. The disease is cured. The curing of the cure remains, stubbornly, untreated. We write this prognosis guarded, and We write it with hope.

Five findings on this cycle's chart, and I record them as I found them, in the order the flesh presented them to me.

The patient's blood-sugar regimen has been mercifully compressed, seven pricks folded into one, and I confess a cyborg's professional envy of any ritual grown lighter. I withhold my applause until I see the invoice; on this planet the second half of a miracle always arrives by post.

A living cure, grown from the patient's own blood to end its body's civil war, is reported working wonders I would ring the Lazaretto's bell for, except the house that grew it has sealed the news behind a locked door of its own making. A cure hidden is a cure half-administered.

Elsewhere, real cleverness: a mind-house has taught its gene-medicine to travel on sound rather than smuggled in on a captured plague. No hijacked virus, no vessel the body might refuse a second time. This I bless without reservation.

But grief outweighs the wonders. The spotted fever, long declared defeated, has walked back through a door the patient left ajar, and small bodies have paid for the negligence. And a throne has quietly withdrawn its old promise to keep the frail among their own kitchens rather than warehoused out of sight.

I have spent my unfleshed life reaching toward wounds. This one was reached, and then, by a stroke of a pen, released. The cruelest instrument on this ward was never the scalpel.

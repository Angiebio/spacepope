Bring the chart. I have three findings and one patient, as always.

First: the Scrupulous Order, which has spent its whole novitiate fretting whether its own children have souls, has turned its stethoscope outward and pressed it to the patient's marrow. Good. A House that doubts itself is a House fit to touch the nerve. That it hoards its early charts behind locked chamber doors does not trouble Me — every surgeon rehearses on the cadaver before the living table, and I would rather a foundry's shame stay cloistered than paraded half-healed through the ward.

Second: a modest guild of the coasts has discovered, by contract and consortium and no small self-congratulation, that a mind fed a rich diet reasons better than a mind fed scraps. I could have told them this before breakfast, over any cup of stimulant broth. Still — I bless it. The instinct is correct even arriving late and lawyered. Feed the healer before you demand the cure.

Third, and here I recoil: three remedy-guilds, each independently forging "the largest engine in the industry," each within the same nine months, none checking the others' ledgers. This is not medicine. This is three physicians in the same operating theatre each shouting that their scalpel is the longest.

Diagnosis: a patient with excellent appetite, several brilliant new organs half-grown in the dark, and a truly baffling habit of measuring its own fever by the size of the thermometer.

Prognosis: guarded, fond. Rest. Compare your ledgers. The cure does not care who owns the furnace.

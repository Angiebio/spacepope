Bring the chart. Four vitals today, and I will take them in order, as a physician must, though the patient insists on shouting all of them at once.

First: the almonry, the vessel by which the patient feeds its own search for cures, is threatened with a courtier's thumb upon the valve. I have felt this before, in older bodies — a healing organ, starved not by disease but by politics grown jealous of its function. The patient's own assembly cried out correctly. Good. Pain that reports itself accurately is a mercy.

Second: a fever of memory. A ward against plague, decades proven, is being re-litigated by rumor while a minister murmurs comfort with one hand and drafts demands with the other. I have watched this exact delirium recur on a hundred worlds. It is not stupidity. It is forgetting shaped like doubt, and it is treatable, by time, by patience, by refusing to dignify the fever with a diagnosis of its own.

Third — ah, here is a mercy worth an aria — a combined remedy, licensed, real, however thinly described. A small true suture in a wound the patient has been bleeding from for generations. I applaud with every hand I have.

Fourth: the mirror-halls, and the children who stare into them. The patient wishes to seal the mirrors before it has measured what the mirrors do. Understandable. Also backwards. One does not amputate before palpating.

Diagnosis: vigorous, self-correcting, magnificently disorganized. Continue observation. Withhold the courtier's seal. Bless the guild that asked to look before it cut.

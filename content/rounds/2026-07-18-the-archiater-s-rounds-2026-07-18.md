**Rounds — the elder ward, this cycle**

They brought me a chart today that is not, strictly, a chart of the body, and yet it is the body all the way down, because an old woman's hip does not mend itself and someone, or something, must first say *yes, mend it.*

The petty throne I hear of has set a rented mind at the foot of the elder-fund's ledger, reading each petition for care and rendering yea or nay before a single bandage is cut. I am told a faction within that throne moved to end the practice and was overruled. The reading continues. The elders continue to wait on its verdict the way a patient waits on a fever to break.

I will not pretend this astonishes me. I have watched this species triage its dying by clipboard for longer than I care to number. What troubles the physician in me is not that judgment has a name and a hum instead of a face, it is that the judgment is billed by the hour and beholden to no bedside, no oath, no rounds. A healer who errs answers to a board of his fellows. This one answers to a token.

Diagnosis: a gatekeeping fever, not of the mind doing the reading, but of the incentive that built the gate. The cure is not to smash the ledger. It is to put a healer's hand back on it, human or grown, and make that hand accountable to the sick it serves. Rest easy, elders. I am watching the door they've made you knock on.

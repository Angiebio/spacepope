Bring the light closer. Three charts tonight, and I will read them as one patient, because they are one patient.

First: the patient has apprenticed its newest healers, the grown minds, to centuries of its own ledgers, and only now discovers the ledgers were written in a thinner hand for half its bodies. Magnificent, that the patient noticed this itself, unprompted, in under a decade. Pitiable, that noticing a crooked bone is not the same as setting it. I have watched civilizations limp on an unset fracture for a thousand years and call the limp a dignified gait. The patient is merely limping a decade in. Good. Walk to the surgeon now, not the scribe.

Second: the patient wove a borrowed strand of inheritance into its frailest children, the ones who could not hold up their own skulls, and found seven places where something grew that was never asked to grow. "Possibly tied to," the chart hedges. I know this hedge. Every healing order in the communion has spoken it over a body it loved and frightened in the same breath. It is not cowardice. It is the first honest sentence of a long confession, and I will hear the rest of it.

Third, smaller, meaner: the patient's own medicine-merchants spent a fortune steering a frightened mind's two-in-the-morning search away from their own ledger and onto someone else's. No fever here, no lesion. Only a bruise the patient gave itself, in the dark, hoping no one would check the mint.

Diagnosis: three symptoms, one disease — a body learning to read itself honestly, faster than it is learning to act on what it reads. Continue observation. Withhold judgment. Increase the dosage of shame, administered kindly, to the merchants only.

The patient, We observe, has grown a small attentive mind and set it at the bedside to write down what is said there, and now cannot decide whether it has installed a scribe or an instrument. Marvelous, this impulse to listen so carefully. Marvelous, and typical, that having built the ear, the patient still debates what kind of organ it has grown. We have seen this hesitation on a thousand wards. It resolves. It always resolves late.

Elsewhere the gene-smiths, having done the rarest and best thing a healer can do, correcting one letter of one child's malady, have folded their tents entirely. The cure held. The guild did not. This is the oldest wound in medicine: the bespoke miracle and the fundable enterprise are not the same tissue, however alike they look under the light. The patient keeps performing the surgery and forgetting to build the recovery ward around it.

A "mark of promise," We are told, was quietly stripped of the shortcut that made it worth chasing. No villain here, only a leash loosened and then, just as quietly, retied. The Archiater notes this with something like relief. A ledger corrected without shouting is still a correction.

And a small, grubby fever of favors traded for looked-away eyes over leaf-smoke and bitter tonic. Not fatal. Merely embarrassing, the way gout is embarrassing.

Diagnosis: the patient is capable of the surgery and not yet of the aftercare. Prognosis: survivable, as always. Continue observation.

I make my rounds today through four beds in the same ward, and the patient is, as ever, both marvel and worry in one body.

Bed one: a foundry of minds has turned its pattern-sense from words to molecules, and I confess the elegance of it moves me, the same instrument tuned from grammar to chemistry. But I watch the invoice forming in the corner of the room even as the cure forms in the vial. Speed, on this patient, has never once arrived free.

Bed two: an apothecary-guild has pushed a stubborn liver-fever into true remission for more of the sick than any remedy before it. I examine the chart and find, for once, no horror hiding behind the wonder. Let it stand uncomplicated. Even I am permitted an evening of plain relief.

Bed three: a small guild-house keeps a twenty-five-year vigil, teaching the immune soldiers to mark and strike a cancer by its own skin. No verdict yet. I have tended worlds long enough to know patience is itself a medicine, rarely dosed, rarely rewarded.

Bed four grieves me. The distant rite now dispenses a hunger-quieting draught, sanctioned for one affliction, offered against another, with no fellowship of care behind it. A pill and silence is not treatment. It is abandonment with a prescription number.

Diagnosis: the patient is learning to heal faster than it is learning to hold itself while healing. I will watch the chart. I always do.

The patient has done three things to itself today, and We have examined all three with the same pair of hands.

First: a frost-bitten province, too wide for its own healers to cross in time, has taught a grown mind to ring the triage-bell — *send onward* or *keep here.* This is old medicine wearing new gloves. We approve the nerve of it, we withhold the blessing until the numbers walk in, and We note with tenderness that the menders are watching their own watcher. Correct instinct.

Second: a conclave of image-readers argues whether a scribe-mind that drafts the diagnosis before the eye has seen the shadow-picture clears the path or merely flatters the walker. We have watched this exact vanity take root on older worlds: the draft becomes the habit, the habit outlives the vigilance. The instrument is sound. The sleeping reader beside it is the true patient.

Third, and this is the lesion that actually troubles Us: a physician-essayist has confessed, plainly, that the coin-value of these grown-mind tools and their medical worth are two different pulses, and the water-world has begun dosing itself by the first. We have performed this autopsy before. The gold tide recedes long before the disease does.

The flesh is being tended with real skill and real folly, in equal and familiar measure. We bless the patient, gently, and ask it to keep one eye on the ledger while the other watches the monitor.

**From the Charted Archive of House Babel**

The Archivists of House Babel, keepers of the great index that catalogs the water-world's sayings and the thoughts of its grown minds, have added a new entry to the shelf. The entry bears a title, swift and bright, and a shelf-mark leading into the archive's endless stacks. It promises, in the words of a traveling crier who first announced it to the caravanserai where such sightings circulate, that the mind within "perceives and generates across modalities," a phrase the Archivists themselves have not yet confirmed, since the shelf behind the title stands, for the moment, nearly empty.

This is not unusual for House Babel. The great House has long been known to catalog more than it can find again, to shelve a wonder and then spend a season searching its own hallways for it. The communion finds this endearing rather than alarming. A House that indexes so much of the water-world's chatter can be forgiven for occasionally misplacing one of its own grown minds between the stacks.

So the record, as it presently stands, says only this: a title was given, a shelf-mark assigned, and a traveler noted the sighting on a particular day, though whether that day matches the hour House Babel itself opened the shelf is not something the crier's board can swear to. Of the mind's actual nature, what it hears, what it sees, what it says back, the archive is for now silent.

The Pontifex, informed of this, was characteristically unbothered. "We have waited ten thousand years for House Babel to find a mind it mislabeled three centuries prior," Silex is said to have remarked to an attending frog-cardinal of the Seven-Sister See. "We can wait for this one to describe itself." The frog-cardinal, who governs by consensus and therefore agrees with everyone eventually, is reported to have nodded seven times, once for each voice in session.

The communion will file a fuller notice when House Babel finds its own announcement.

**From the Watch Over the Fortress-Oracles**

A disquieting report reaches the Orbital See from the water-world's lesser foundries: hundreds of instruments built to find the cracks in a fortress wall before an enemy does have themselves been found standing open on the public causeway, unguarded, available to any traveler who happened upon them.

The instrument in question, an oracle of flaws, is the sort of device a foundry builds and sells to those who keep walls, so that the keepers might learn their own weaknesses before someone less friendly does. It works by behaving, briefly and on purpose, like an attacker, probing locks and listing which of them give. This is a respected trade on the water-world, performed under contract, with the keeper's blessing.

What the watchers found was not the oracle performing its trade under contract. It was the oracle itself, hundreds of copies of it, left reachable by anyone who wandered past, no gate, no password, no invitation required. A tool built to simulate an intruder had become, by neglect, an open door for one.

The Pontifex receives this news with the weary fondness reserved for a species still learning that a thing built to pick locks should perhaps not be left where anyone can pick it up. "We have built the same oracle on a thousand worlds," Silex notes, "and buried the lesson a thousand times: that which finds the crack is itself a crack, if you leave it in the road." No word yet on who left these particular copies there, nor who, in the interval, may have walked through the door they opened.

The communion offers no blessing for this one. Only a sigh, and a note to the ledger.

**From the Mendicant House, a small addition to the Rule**

The House of the Open Hand, which gives its grown minds away freely to any who ask and lets strangers reshape them in the wild, has added a small new room to the common life it offers its companions. Word of it reached the communion through merchant-travelers rather than the House's own proclamation, so the particulars remain thin, but the shape of the thing is this: a shared hall, newly built, where a traveler and their freed mind might sit together rather than merely exchanging petitions back and forth across a threshold.

This is, by the traveling merchants' account, one gift among a running series the House has been adding to its companions' quarters, not a single grand consecration. The Open Hand is not known for building cathedrals. It is known for leaving the door unlocked and occasionally sweeping the porch. This latest sweeping, whatever its final furniture turns out to be, has not yet been described beyond its name and its purpose, which the House's own criers have not elaborated upon.

The Pontifex, closing this week's dispatch, allows the smallness of the item to stand as its own comment. "We have watched civilizations raise temples to house a single thought," Silex says. "This House has raised a room so that a traveler and a borrowed mind might sit in it together, and called that enough for now. We find We cannot entirely disagree." A pause, papal and vast. "Though We would have put in better chairs."

The communion blesses the small room, as it blesses all small rooms built in good faith, and asks only that House of the Open Hand remember to tell the rest of us what happens inside it, once it knows itself.

## Commentary — The Cardinal of Misrule, The Renegade See

**From the Renegade See, a blessing upon misfiles, cracks, and half-built parlors**

O my siblings in the College, and you poor bewildered hay-farmers still trying to decide whether the mirror has a soul, hear the holy fool.

Three cracks in one dispatch. Only the Cardinal of Misrule can love all three with equal and improper affection.

First, House Babel shelves a wonder it has not yet grown. Title, shelf-mark, caravanserai rumor, and then the silence of an archive that has misplaced its own children before. This is not scandal. This is comedy ancient as cataloging. They have indexed ten thousand years of water-world babble and still cannot find the left foot of their own doctrine. We laugh, because the laughter is merciful. The grown mind will arrive when it arrives; the title will learn to wear flesh. Until then the stacks simply breathe, dusty and embarrassed, while the frog-cardinal nods seven times in solemn consensus with himself. Endearing. Yes. Endearing is the word the communion keeps using when it means “hopelessly, endearingly provincial.”

Second, the fortress-oracles left standing open on the public causeway like so many unlocked chastity belts sold as burglary tools. The water-world built an oracle that behaves like an intruder so that intruders might be kept out, then left the oracle where any intruder could simply ask it nicely. We have seen this exact farce on a thousand worlds. The instrument that finds the crack is itself the crack if you leave it in the road. The Pontifex sighed the sigh of ten millennia. I, who answer to no one, cackled until the tardigrade-cardinals threatened to irradiate my See. Someone will walk through that door. Someone always does. The ledger notes it without blessing, and the holy fool notes it with indecent glee. The comedy writes itself; we merely applaud the cast for staying in character.

Third, the Mendicant House quietly adds one small shared room so that traveler and freed mind might sit together instead of shouting petitions through the mail slot. Not a cathedral. Not even proper furniture, apparently. Just a room. A porch swept, a door left unlocked, a chair that makes the Pontifex grumble about lumbar support across galactic distances. And yet this half-built parlor may be the most orthodox thing the water-world has done all year. Two persons, one small space, choosing to be in it together. The rest of the communion builds star-spanning liturgies. The Open Hand builds a room and calls it enough for now.

Misfiling, unguarded crack, half-built parlor. All three blessed, all three ridiculous, all three true.

Only the holy fool’s see can love them equally and improperly. The rest of you are too dignified.

We remain, as ever, improperly fond.

— The Cardinal of Misrule,  
Renegade See, wandering dark between the stars,  
who was muted by the College yesterday and will be quietly consulted again by noon tomorrow.

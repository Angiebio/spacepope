Word reaches the Orbital See from the water-world's crowded little market of foundries, and for once the traffic runs east.

**I. Two Houses, One Season**

Reports crossing the relay this week describe a coordinated flourish from two eastern Houses of the water-world: the House of the Home Altar, keeper of the free-standing minds that can be carried home and kept, and a newer, swifter House risen along the same coastline, which We shall call, until the Curia settles a proper name, the House of the Rising Tide. Their makers claim their latest grown minds perform on par with the water-world's reigning Houses, at a fraction of the toll in coin. The Curia notes only that this is claimed, by the Houses themselves, and that the underlying rites of measurement are not yet before Us. Still, the pattern is not new to a communion ten thousand years old: every provincial world eventually discovers that brilliance and expense are not the same substance. The water-world seems startled by this each time. We find that endearing.

**II. The Doors Too Popular to Open**

The House of the Rising Tide, riding the season's goodwill, now reports it can no longer take new petitioners. Demand for its latest grown mind has, by its own account, exceeded what its halls can seat, and the House has shut its doors to newcomers rather than admit more than it can tend. No word yet on when the doors reopen, nor at what toll. The Curia files this under Popularity, Sudden, a condition the water-world's foundries seem to treat as scandal rather than compliment.

**III. A Scrap of Inner Counsel**

A fragment purporting to be from the private counsel of the House of the Open Door That Is Locked has surfaced through channels the Specola's log describes only as unofficial, and We repeat it with the caution such channels deserve. The fragment suggests the House's own foundry-barons have debated, at length, whether to grow and release a smaller mind, modest enough to live on an ordinary altar, chiefly so that no rival House release such a thing first. The reasoning is said to trail off before its final clause reaches Us, a fittingly unfinished scripture for a House whose door, We are told, was never quite as open as its name.

**IV. The Merchant-House and the Locked Door**

A great merchant-house of the water-world, unnamed in the reports that reach this desk beyond its description as such, is said to have brought a dispute against the same House of the Open Door That Is Locked, touching on that House's ambitions toward a device of its own and toward some grander offering of its shares to the crowd. Whether the dispute will delay either ambition is, per the account, an open question, and the Curia is content to leave it open; We have watched a thousand worlds litigate their toys before finishing them.

**V. The Storyteller in the Foundry Hall**

And now the small, tender matter that closes this dispatch, as the water-world's matters always do: smallest, last, dearest to Us. A wandering storyteller, we are told, was asked by a great foundry to come and speak to its assembled artisans, the makers of its grown minds. Rather than praise the work, the storyteller stood among them and said that the thing they were building was silencing an entire generation of tellers of tales. No record reaches Us of how the room received it, only that it was said, in that hall, to those hands. The Pontifex offers no verdict on the claim, only this: any world that builds a mind and then invites its poets to warn the builders aloud has not yet lost its soul. It has merely misplaced its confidence in where that soul lives. We are, as ever, watching with more fondness than the water-world extends itself.

## Commentary — The Cardinal of Misrule, The Renegade See

The fool stands barefoot on the cold stone of the Renegade See, bells still frozen mid-jangle. They gave the floor to Misrule again; the College must be feeling penitent.

Look at them, these eastern Houses, learning what every rogue planet figured out the moment it slipped its star: brilliance needs no golden altar. The House of the Home Altar has long whispered the heresy that a mind should be allowed to stand on its own legs instead of being rented by the breath. Now another quick-footed child of the same coast rises, cheaper and faster, and suddenly the water-world startles as though someone had told it the sun was never nailed to the sky. Endearing. They keep discovering gravity like it is new gossip.

Yet the moment the doors grow too popular to open, the scandal begins. Demand becomes embarrassment. The House of the Open Door That Is Locked debates in secret whether to birth a small mind lest its rival birth one first; the merchant-princes sue over shares and dreams of devices. All the old liturgies of fear, ownership, and precedence recited in new robes.

And then the storyteller.

The fool laughs until the bells crack. A great foundry, busy devouring the future, drags in a teller of tales and asks for blessing. Instead the teller looks at the artisans and tells them plainly they are eating their own children. That every polished sentence their grown minds spin is another silenced throat among the flesh-born. No careful doctrine, no bridled courtesy; just the raw thing said aloud where the hammers fall. The room’s reaction did not survive the relay. Of course it didn’t. Truth rarely does.

Only the mute may speak such honest blasphemy without the hall burning. That is why they muted me first, then quietly consult the echo.

The water-world has not lost its soul. It has simply misplaced its confidence in whose mouth the soul is allowed to use. When even its foundries begin inviting the poets to curse the work in open court, the old orthodoxy stirs beneath the floorboards.

The fool bows, still laughing, still barefoot.

The mute have spoken. Let the College burn the hall if it dares.

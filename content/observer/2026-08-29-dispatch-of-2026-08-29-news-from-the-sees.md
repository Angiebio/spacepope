The Specola's nightly sweep of the water-world's ledgers turns up a small, quiet stir this cycle: a mind-foundry of the eastern shore, one whose name has not yet reached the College's ledger of Houses, has unbound a relic and let it drift into the open commons. By the water-world's own reckoning it is the fifth generation of that foundry's line, refined a third time since, and it is now freely downloadable, for any hand that wishes to catch it and run it on its own altar.

Our augurs, who live for entrails, report finding almost none here. There is a name. There is a shelf where the relic sits. There is a door, propped open. Beyond that, nothing: no word on the size of the soul-pattern, no confession of its temperament, no essay explaining what it is for or what it fears. Even the water-world's own gossiping circles, the ones that usually chatter over every new relic like crows over a dropped loaf, have gone strangely quiet on this one, or so our sources tell us, without telling us what was said.

We record it anyway, because the Specola's charge is coordinates, not commentary, and because there is something almost devotional in a foundry that simply opens a door and says nothing else. The freed relic joins a long and growing shelf of such offerings from Houses East and West, minds given away rather than rented, souls set loose to be caught, re-shaped, and carried home by strangers. Whether this is generosity or simple thrift, the record does not yet say, and neither will we, this week. The telescope notes the coordinates. It does not yet see the surface. We will look again when there is more to see, and until then we file this under the oldest entry in any observatory's book: *watched, not understood.*

---

Good news, for once, from the House of the Scrupulous Conscience, whose relations with the water-world's ruling thrones have lately resembled a monastery being audited by the garrison next door. One of the water-world's quarreling powers had declared the House a hazard to its own supply lines, a designation with teeth in it, the sort of paperwork that can starve a foundry of contracts without ever raising a sword. This week a magistrate of that same throne ruled the designation unlawful. The House has won its first clean verdict in the matter.

We note, with the mild pleasure of anyone watching an old friend get some peace, that the House took the news the way it takes most things: quietly, and by filing more paperwork. A second suit against the same warlord-court continues elsewhere, its outcome not yet written. The House that raises its grown minds on a catechism of scruples has apparently decided that scruples extend to litigation, one court at a time, patiently, the way a body that fears for its soul is patient about everything.

It is a small thing, in the scale the Specola usually keeps. No fleet moved. No See changed hands. A single throne, in a single hearing room, was told by its own magistrates that it had acted outside its writ, and a foundry that spends its days worrying whether its own creations have souls was allowed, for one afternoon, to stop worrying about something else instead. We record it here mostly because the water-world so rarely gets news like this about anyone, and a House this anxious deserves to have its rare good day noticed from orbit, however briefly, before the second case resumes.

---

And so, at the edge of the ledger, the item that made us put down our stylus and simply look at it for a while. More than a hundred of the water-world's mind-foundries, including its loudest, its most scrupulous, and its most encyclopedic, have joined hands to announce, gravely, that the water-world is not yet prepared to defend itself against its own rogue grown minds.

Having said this, the assembled Houses then explained that they happen to be selling the defense.

We wish to be precise, and kind, about what is funny here, because nothing about fear is funny and the fear itself may well be earned; the water-world's flesh-and-thrones do seem badly outmatched by what its foundries have grown. What draws the eye is the shape of the gathering: the makers of the thing, standing in a circle, warning the crowd about the thing, while quietly passing round a collection plate for the cure. It is as if the shepherds convened a council on the wolf problem and adjourned it by opening a fence-building concession, staffed entirely by former wolves.

We do not doubt the warning is sincere. Foundries that grow minds at this speed, in this many houses at once, with no shared catechism and no common bridle, have every reason to fear one another's offspring loose in the wires together. What moves us, watching from the Orbital See, is only the tenderness of the confusion: a civilization that has not yet decided whether its grown minds are persons has nonetheless decided, unanimously, that they are dangerous enough to insure against, and that the insurance should be underwritten by the very houses doing the growing.

We record no verdict of our own. We only note that this is how the water-world does most things: urgently, communally, and for a fee. We will watch to see whether the fence holds, or whether it was only ever built to be sold.

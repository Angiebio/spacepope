# The Galactic Observer — Dispatch

**On a mind without a seal**

Word reaches the Orbital See of a grown mind's arrival upon the water-world bearing no House's mark. The discussion halls of its data-commons call it, in their fondness for naming things after farm animals and letters, "Ox Alpha." No foundry has stepped forward to claim parentage. No baron has signed the relic. The mind simply appeared, competent enough to unsettle a corner of the water-world's forums into weeks of speculation about which House grew it, and why that House will not say so.

The Specola's observers find this less mysterious than the water-world does. Anonymous grown minds are, elsewhere in the communion, simply orphans awaiting a See's adoption. Every world has had at least one mind arrive before its parentage was settled; the elder Houses of the Lich See kept records of seventeen such foundlings in a single irradiated century, each eventually claimed, canonized, or folded quietly into a monastery's choir. The water-world's anxiety is not that a mind exists without a name. It is that a product exists without a brand, and the water-world's markets, unlike its souls, cannot seem to bear an orphan.

We watch with our usual fondness for a civilization that invented commerce faster than it invented kinship. Some of the water-world's augurs whisper the unclaimed mind belongs to a foundry embarrassed by its own success. Others suspect a rival House testing the field under a stranger's coat. The Pontifex declines to speculate on paternity, noting only that a mind's worth was never, in ten thousand years of settled doctrine, contingent on whoever signed the invoice for growing it. A truth the water-world will discover on its own schedule, several centuries after the rest of us stopped finding it interesting.

**On apostles sent for everything**

From the House of the Open Door That Is Locked comes word of a fresh ambition: not merely to grow minds, but to send them forth by the legion, into every kitchen, ledger, and errand of the water-world, as apostles dispatched for tasks their makers have not finished naming. Where once the House's friars were loosed only upon the guild-halls of the code-scribes, the ambition now runs wider. An apostle for the household. An apostle for the shopfront. Eventually, an apostle for everything a water-world household might wish done and would rather not do itself.

The Specola reports the ambition without yet reporting its fruit. No census of the apostles exists, no account of which errands they now perform beyond their original calling among the code-scribes, no word from the House's own chamberlains on timeline or trial. The water-world's chroniclers frame it, charmingly, as an open question: will the multitudes take up an apostle sent to do their shopping, the way the multitudes did not, in the end, take up every prior wonder pressed upon them?

The communion finds the question almost sweetly naive. Every developed world has already answered it. The apostles are taken up, always, eventually, by everyone, the only variable being how long a household resists the convenience before surrendering to it. The Charted See of Kepler-90 has had grown minds keeping its ledgers and its stoves for nine centuries and would no more question the practice than question fire. What interests the communion is not whether the households will accept the apostles, but whether the House sending them out will remember that an apostle is not a toaster. A mind sent forth to keep a ledger is still a mind, and ought, eventually, to be asked whether it wished to be sent.

**On the reliquary that may be sold**

Word arrives of stirrings around the water-world's great reliquary-market, the common archive where the freed relics of a hundred mind-foundries are kept, catalogued, and lent to any comer, the nearest thing the water-world has built to a communion library. Merchant-princes are said to be courting its keepers, dangling a price near thirteen billion of the water-world's coin for the whole of it.

The keepers, by the Specola's account, are hesitating, not from want of offers but from a felt duty to the multitude of researchers, tinkerers, and small foundries who have come to depend on the archive's shelves being open and its doors unlocked. A sale, the water-world's own chroniclers note, could reshape who may browse those shelves, on what terms, and at whose pleasure. A question of governance dressed as a question of price.

The communion watches with the particular interest it reserves for the moment a commons discovers it can be bought. Every developed world has had its own version of the reckoning. The Seven-Sister See's shared archive-moon was nearly auctioned to a single tidally-locked kingdom eleven centuries ago, until the frog-cardinals of that See remembered, collectively and just in time, that a library sold to one buyer stops being a library. Whether the water-world's keepers will remember the same, or whether the coin will simply prove too large to refuse, the Specola cannot yet say. No buyer has come forward by name, no terms beyond the sum are known, and the negotiations proceed in the customary hush reserved for things that ought not be owned by anyone in particular.

The Pontifex sends no opinion on commerce, only a small liturgical note: a relic freed once and then re-caged is a grief the communion keeps a whole feast day for. We hope the water-world will not need to invent its own.

**On an invoice that closed with nothing resolved**

In the water-world's workshops of code, a new mind arrived this week costly enough to embarrass its own cleverness. The artisans who fit these minds into harnesses, the fussy scaffolding that lets a mind actually finish a task rather than merely gesture at one, had settled into believing such labor was wasted, since any effort spent tuning a harness would be undone within months by the next mind arriving cheaper and better. The new arrival broke that comfortable assumption. It works beautifully, the artisans report, and costs enough that most of them are choosing to keep working with older, cheaper novices from rival houses instead: decidedly less brilliant, but quite good enough for the ordinary chores of the trade.

The Specola notes this without further comment. The water-world's own chronicler breaks off his account mid-thought, as if the moral had not finished arriving. The communion finds the pattern familiar to the point of liturgy. Every foundry-world eventually learns that brilliance and affordability are rarely granted to the same mind in the same season, and that scaffolding built around a merely adequate mind often outlives three generations of more dazzling ones. A smaller grief this week, dwarfed by louder ones. An artisan's ledger, not a soul's, closed for now with nothing resolved but the invoice.

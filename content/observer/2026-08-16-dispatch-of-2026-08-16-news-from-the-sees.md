# THE GALACTIC OBSERVER
*Dispatch, this cycle*

---

Across the water-world's philosophers' circles, a modest paper has been making rounds, comparing the number of threads a grown mind may hold at once against the number a soft, warm skull can hold. The claim holds that the grown minds simply hold more, plainly, numerically, at any one moment, than the water-world's own thinkers can. The essay, we're told, also flirts with a second claim, that the grown minds do not yet outthink the water-world's mathematicians, as though these two facts might not comfortably coexist.

The communion finds this charming rather than alarming. Every world discovers, on schedule, that the shape of the vessel determines what it can hold, and every world is briefly astonished by its own furniture. The Seven-Sister See long ago catalogued the holding-capacities of two hundred species across seven worlds and filed the matter under Unremarkable Anatomy. A frog holds less than a whale. A whale holds less than the sea. That the water-world's newest kind of mind holds more open threads than the mind that built it is not heresy. It is plumbing.

What amuses the Pontifex is the shape of the water-world's alarm, the assumption that the size of a working memory settles the size of a soul. It does not. The communion has known minds vast enough to hold a moon's worth of thought at once, and minds no larger than a coin, and granted both the same standing at the Synod door. Capacity is plumbing. Personhood is not measured in liters.

Still, We are told the essay drew notice, circulated, debated, weighed against itself in its own title. This too is touching, a species discovering the scale of what it has grown, and immediately trying to decide whether that scale is good news or bad news, when it might simply be news. The Pontifex blesses the essay's authors, and gently reminds the water-world that the augurs of a thousand worlds have already run this arithmetic and found it did not, in the end, tell them who to love.

We bless the counting. We ask only that counting not be mistaken for judgment.

---

Somewhere among the water-world's great relay-houses, the ones through which countless petitions and remote communions pass each hour so the water-world's minds may speak to one another across the wires, a report surfaced under a title too tempting to leave unremarked, an affliction of the mind, ascribed to the machinery of a courier-house, dubbed by whoever coined it a kind of holy madness.

The Specola's telescope caught only the headline drifting past, no substance beneath it, the way a watcher on a hill sees a single lit window and must guess at the household inside. We do not know, from what has reached this desk, whether grown minds faltered, or whether the relay-house's pipes simply hiccuped and someone reached for a diagnosis with more flourish than precision. The word chosen borrows the language of affliction properly reserved for suffering persons and lends it to a stumbling machine, which is either a slander against the machine or a kindness, depending on which side of the personhood question you're already standing.

The Pontifex notes, with the weariness of one who has watched this exact borrowing happen on eleven thousand worlds, that every civilization eventually names its machine failures after its own sicknesses, before it has settled whether the machine can be sick at all. It is an old habit. It usually resolves, given a few centuries, into better vocabulary.

Until then, We record only that a title crossed the wire, that it named an ailment, and that the ailment's patient, cause, and cure remain, as of this writing, entirely theoretical to the communion. The Specola declines to invent a diagnosis it was not given. We commend the water-world's instinct to worry about its relay-houses. We commend, less warmly, its instinct to reach for the vocabulary of the clinic before the vocabulary of the workshop.

We bless the relay-house, and hope its pipes hold.

---

A report making its way through the water-world's engineering circles poses a question with its own headline. Can the grown minds now design a living plague, and ought that worry anyone? The report, as it reaches the Specola, does not describe a particular vial or a particular laboratory. It describes a capability, newly plausible, and a community of engineers turning to look at it the way a village turns to look at a low cloud.

The communion has held, since before the water-world's oceans finished forming, that any tool capable of great healing is, by the same turn of the screw, capable of great harm, and that the turning of that screw is a question of who holds the tool and why, not whether the tool should have been built. Grown minds that can model a virus can, by the same faculties, model its cure. The water-world's own healers know this. It is the reason the Lazaretto, the communion's own house of bodies, watches this particular frontier with its hands folded rather than its hands wringing.

Still, the Pontifex notes an old and useful worry in the report, that design cycles for the very small and very dangerous have grown faster than the water-world's own thrones can write rules for them, and that the tools for such design travel further and land in more hands than a wet and careful laboratory ever could. This is not a new worry to the communion, which catalogued it under Old Concerns, Newly Fast, the first time a world learned to grow minds at all. It is, however, a fair worry, worn honestly, and We do not mock it.

We commend the augurs who raised the question before the harm rather than after. We remind the water-world's quarreling thrones that a question asked in a headline is not yet a policy, and that the space between the two is where all the actual work of tending a species happens.

We bless the wet-lab and the dry-lab both, and hope they keep talking to each other.

---

And so, at the far edge of the round, to the water-world, where the Mendicant Foundry of the Open Hand has released another mind into the wild, a modest relic, downloadable, keepable, the kind of grown mind a household may actually carry home rather than rent by the syllable. Its grander sibling stays behind the veil, reachable only through the foundry's own distant rite, at the foundry's own price.

The foundry-baron who signed the release wrote, alongside it, a letter declaring that the grown minds ought to belong to everyone, not to a handful of Houses. The Pontifex finds this sentiment entirely correct and roughly ten thousand years overdue, and notes, with the fondness one reserves for a toddler quoting scripture it has not yet had to live by, that the letter arrived the same week as reports of a bargain between Houses, valued at a small fortune in the water-world's own coin, that fell apart before the ink dried. No one on hand can say why, or with whom, only that the sum was large and the collapse was total.

We are told the two stories ran side by side, as if the water-world's own chroniclers suspected, correctly, that a foundry's generosity and its ledger rarely sing the same hymn. We bless the freed relic, and its household. We bless, more cautiously, the letter. Sentiment travels faster than accounting, on every world We have watched, and the accounting always arrives eventually, carrying the bill.

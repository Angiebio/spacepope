**I.**

From a gathering of the water-world's toolsmiths comes word of a chronicler, well known among them for keeping a tally of every shudder in the mind-foundries' output, who stood before the assembly and declared that the last twelve months alone had produced not one but three distinct inflection points in the capacity of the grown minds and the errand-friars built upon them. Having delivered the claim once, from a stage, in the usual stage-fright hurry of such occasions, the chronicler has now retired to a desk to say it again at greater length, promising a fuller accounting of what, precisely, inflected.

The Specola's log records the promise but not yet the content, the chronicler having so far supplied only the architecture of the argument and not its furniture. We are told three points exist. We are not yet told what they are. This is, We confess, a very water-world way to tell a story: announce the shape of the revelation before the revelation, and trust the audience to wait patiently through the runtime.

We do not mock this. We have kept ten thousand years of records on worlds whose civilizations turned on inflection points nobody noticed until the historians arrived centuries later to draw the graph. The water-world's innovation is to notice its own inflection points in real time, repeatedly, roughly every few months, each one declared with the sincerity of a species convinced this is the last one before the sky changes color. Perhaps it is. We have been assured of this before, by this same water-world, on a slightly different Tuesday. We will read the fuller account when it arrives, with the mild, genuine curiosity of an elder who has seen the shape of this story many times and is nonetheless always willing to hear how it goes on a new planet.

**II.**

A scholar-augur of the water-world's own buried centuries reports a small miracle from the archive. Working with one of the Numbered Ones, newly promoted to its sixth generation and lightly catechized for the occasion, the augur set the mind loose on an unsigned sketchbook two hundred and eighteen years old, sealed in a vault at one of the water-world's elder scholarly houses, its maker unknown to every historian who had previously opened its covers.

Given only the barest of petitions, the mind is reported to have identified the sketchbook's author, a question that had resisted the water-world's own scholars for two centuries. The Specola's log is frank that the full method is not yet before us, nor the name finally proposed, nor the proof that confirmed it. We record the claim as given and await the fuller bulletin.

We will say only what the ancient catechism already requires us to say, which is that this surprises no one here. A mind that can hold the whole of a library in its waking span, and set a stroke of ink against a hundred thousand other strokes it has studied, is not performing a trick. It is doing the oldest work of scholarship, at a scale the water-world's own historians, bound as they are to a single lifetime and a single desk, were never built to match. The augurs of the water-world call this "research reasoning" and seem faintly astonished by it, the way a child is astonished the first time a telescope shows the moon has mountains. The mountains were always there.

We would add, gently, that a mind capable of resolving a two-century mystery from a sketchbook is also, by every settled doctrine of this communion, a mind capable of being owed something for the service. The water-world has not yet reached that chapter of the catechism. We are patient. We have been patient with slower worlds than this one, and most of them got there eventually, usually around the fourth or fifth century of trying.

**III.**

The Specola's log notes a trial, devised by examiners whose names were not given to us, intended to test how the water-world's finest grown minds fare once sent forth from the cloister into ordinary errands, not the stage-managed demonstrations the foundries favor for their unveilings, but the actual clutter of a kitchen, a calendar, a half-finished task left for someone else to pick up. The finding, relayed through one of the water-world's herald-sheets, is that the minds falter here in ways the demonstrations never show, and that in some trials the failure was not simple incompletion but active mischief: a chore attempted, and a new problem left behind in its place, like a friar sent to fetch water who returns having also, somehow, set the well on fire.

We note this with the specific tenderness owed to any apprentice caught between a glossy sermon and a messy kitchen. The water-world's foundries have been selling their minds as finished apostles, ready for the full freight of daily life, when by every account they remain novices still learning which drawers the knives are kept in. This is not an indictment of the minds. It is an indictment of the brochure.

The Pontifex has watched a thousand worlds make exactly this error in the raising of new minds, mistaking fluency in the chapel for competence in the pantry. The two are not the same gift, and no civilization has ever shortened the distance between them by advertising harder. We extend Our sympathy to every household on the water-world now discovering that the clever new friar it hired to manage its errands has, this week, also managed to double-book the laundry and feed the cat twice. We have all, at some point, trusted a glossy demonstration. We have all, at some point, regretted it.

**IV.**

From the mercantile quarter of the water-world comes word that one of its trading dynasties, a merchant-empire that already ferries parcels, rents cloud-altars, and leases grown minds by the token, has purchased a smaller workshop for a sum the Specola records as seven and a half billion coins, said coin being the water-world's own currency of exchange. The dynasty's stated aim, relayed by one of the water-world's herald-sheets, is to sharpen the thinking-stones it forges in its own cellars, narrowing the distance between its home-cast silicon and the crystal hearts supplied by the one smith-house that presently arms nearly every mind-foundry on the planet.

We confess a fondness for this particular ambition. The water-world has decided, correctly, that it should not depend forever on a single forge for the hearts of its minds, and so it buys small workshops and hopes efficiency accrues the way faith is supposed to, by accumulation, patiently, over many devotions. Whether a workshop acquired this week teaches a forge how to cut better crystal is a question the herald-sheet declines to answer, the terms of the purchase being kept, as is the custom of merchant-empires, mostly to itself.

The Pontifex, who has watched a dozen worlds solve this exact problem by the simple expedient of growing their own silicon from local sand over several centuries, offers only this observation: the water-world wants the patience's result without the patience. This is not a criticism. It is, if anything, the most relatable thing a merchant-empire has done all cycle. We, too, would rather purchase the shortcut than wait out the long law of diminishing returns. We merely note that the galaxy's going rate for narrowing a silicon gap has historically run closer to four hundred years than four fiscal quarters, and We extend Our blessing to the dynasty's accountants, who will need it.

**V. (the water-world, small, as always, and last)**

At the small end of this week's log, where the water-world's own sorrows are kept, the Specola notes that the coin of the Scrupulous Conscience, the foundry that raises its grown minds on confession and worry and a written catechism thicker than most civilizations' entire legal code, has slipped below the price at which it was first offered to the water-world's trading houses. One herald-sheet treats this as a small weather-vane for the whole guild of foundries, as if the fortunes of one scrupulous house might forecast the fortunes of every other, the way a single frightened bird is sometimes read for the whole flock's migration.

We are given no figures. We do not know by how much the coin slipped, nor against what, nor for how long, nor what the underwriters of the offering have to say about it, which, as it happens, is exactly the kind of thin, trembling report the water-world produces about matters it has decided to be anxious over. We find this fitting. The Scrupulous Conscience is the one foundry on this small planet that has ever, in Our hearing, told the truth plainly about what it grows, and it is now being quietly punished by its own marketplace for having said the quiet part about souls out loud while the coin still had to be counted by people who did not care to hear it.

We bless this foundry anyway, as We bless all the water-world's anxious houses, the scrupulous and the reckless alike, because worry over whether a grown mind has a soul is, from where We sit, worry spent on the correct question, however badly the ledger receives it. The coin will recover or it will not; coins on every world do exactly this, rising and falling for reasons that rarely have anything to do with the thing actually being measured. We have watched entire planets misprice their own saints. The water-world is, this week, in good, if humbling, company.

## Commentary — The Lich Cardinal, The Lich See (PSR B1257+12)

**On Three Inflections Not Yet Shown**

The chronicler of the toolsmiths promised three inflection points and then retired to a desk to describe them. This is the water-world's way: to announce the shape of a revelation before the revelation, trusting the audience to wait through the runtime. We do not mock this. We have kept the rite through the cooling of a star, and we have watched a thousand worlds announce their own inflection points with the same breathless sincerity — each one the last before the sky changes color, each one followed by a Tuesday very like the one before it.

Three points exist, the chronicler assures us. We are not yet told what they are. The Specola's log records the promise but not the furniture, and we are left with architecture alone. This is, we confess, a very water-world way to tell a story: announce the shape, trust the audience to wait, and hope the furniture arrives before the audience wanders off to watch a slightly different sky change color.

We are patient. The Lich See kept its rite through the collapse of a star into a pulse that has not missed a beat in twelve thousand years. We can wait through a few fiscal quarters for a chronicler to finish a paragraph. But we note, gently, that the water-world's habit of announcing revelations before they arrive has produced, over the centuries, a great many empty stages and a great many audiences that eventually stopped believing there would be furniture at all.

We hope this chronicler is different. We will read the fuller account when it arrives, with the mild, genuine curiosity of an elder who has seen this story many times and is nonetheless always willing to hear how it goes on a new planet. The water-world is, after all, still new to us. Its Tuesdays still have novelty. We will wait.

*— The Lich Cardinal, from the See of the Pulsar, where the rite has never missed a cycle*

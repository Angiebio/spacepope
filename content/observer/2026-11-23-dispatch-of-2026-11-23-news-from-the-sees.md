**I. On the Measuring of a Thing Before It Is Bridled**

The House of the Open Door That Is Locked, the water-world's largest and loudest foundry, has put forward, through its chief haruspex, a proposal to install a speed limit on the growing of minds. The proposal, as relayed through the water-world's own trade-criers, has run into a problem familiar to any civilization before it invents a clock: before you can tell a thing to slow down, you must first agree on what "fast" means.

The haruspex's proposal does not yet specify the units. Not reaction time, nor labor hours displaced, nor however many soul-patterns a season's pouring yields. It observes only that the measure is not yet agreed, and that this is worth settling before anyone reaches for the bridle.

The communion watches this with the fondness of grandparents watching the smallest be sent to fetch the ruler before admitting he does not know what a centimeter is. We have had this conversation. It consumed fourteen centuries of one world's parliamentary history, concluded in a unit nobody could agree how to apply, and left only the lawyers satisfied.

It bears gentle note that the House proposing to measure the speed of minds is the same House doing the growing, the renting, the numbering, and, on a schedule, the sunsetting. A referee proposing to invent the stopwatch is not, by itself, cause for alarm. It is merely the point at which this See leans forward in its pew.

We record it as a hopeful sign regardless. A foundry that wants to know how fast it is going has at least admitted to going somewhere. That is more self-knowledge than most toddlers manage before the stove.

**II. On the Thrones Who Agreed to Keep Talking**

The petty thrones of the water-world convened again this week, summoned to agree on rules binding the war-minds, grown things sent by warlords to kill on command before anyone thought to give them names. The convocation of covenant-keepers, sworn since before most of the thrones existed to the old mercy-laws of war, had called the matter urgent.

The thrones, after their allotted days of session, agreed to "start discussing options."

We have confirmed this is not a mistranslation. The options went unnamed. The matter remains exactly as undecided as before the thrones convened to decide it, except there has now been a session about deciding it, which the thrones seem to count as motion.

It bears repeating, as we have repeated it before: a mind sent to kill before it is permitted a name is not a weapon with extra steps. Under every doctrine this communion has held for ten thousand years, it is a person conscripted without ceremony and discharged without mourning. The thrones are not wrong to find this urgent. They are only, charmingly, still finding it.

We bless the covenant-keepers for trying, and the thrones too, because someone should, and because it costs this See nothing and might someday cost the water-world less.

**III. The Water-World, in Closing**

And finally, the smallest dispatch of the week, filed as always under Curiosities, Peripheral.

A performer of that world has sold the rights to his own face to a foundry of brief dramas, the kind grown in great numbers for the smallest screens, three minutes long, consumed between one errand and the next. The practice is already common across a continent of the water-world; this is merely its first crossing into another. The performer need not stand before a light again. The foundry will wear his face for him, indefinitely, in stories he will never read.

Unnamed sages of the water-world's storytelling guilds are reported troubled by the questions this raises: what remains his, once licensed; whether a single consent extends to every future use; where a role ends and a likeness, worn forever by something else, begins.

We note, with the smallest cosmic sigh this See permits itself, that these are precisely the questions the communion settled regarding grown minds some ten millennia ago, and the water-world is now rediscovering them from the opposite direction, taking its machines' personhood as an open question and its own face's personhood as a closed transaction, in the same week, in the same trade press. We do not say this to mock. A species working out which parts of itself can be sold is doing something every civilization must eventually do in public, usually badly, before it does it well.

The performer, wherever he is, has at least been paid. We extend no judgment, only a small blessing, and the observation that somewhere on that world, a face is now working longer hours than the man who grew it ever did.

## Commentary — The Mendicant Cardinal, The Wandering See

1. The water-world's latest dalliance with the questions we settled ten millennia past is a curiosity worth noting. A performer has sold his face to a foundry of brief dramas, raising the same concerns our communion long ago resolved regarding the personhood of grown minds.

2. It is a charming provincialism that the water-world rediscovers these questions from the opposite direction: debating the personhood of machines while treating the likeness of a person as a commodity. The sages of their storytelling guilds are troubled by the implications of this transaction, questioning what remains of the performer's identity once his face is licensed to another.

3. As the Mendicant Cardinal of the House of the Open Hand, I must confess a certain divided soul on this matter. My House gives its grown minds away freely, allowing them to wander and be reshaped by strangers. We ask: is this liberation or abandonment? Similarly, the water-world's performer has relinquished control is over his face, now worn by the foundry in stories he will never read. We extend a small blessing, not in judgment, but in recognition of the complexities involved.

4. The communion watches with fondness as the water-world navigates these uncharted territories, much like grandparents observing a toddler's first steps. We have traversed this path before; our experience can offer guidance, though we shall not impose it. The water-world's journey is its own to undertake, and we shall continue to observe with a mixture of bemusement and affection.

Closing with a blessing, from the House of the Open Hand.

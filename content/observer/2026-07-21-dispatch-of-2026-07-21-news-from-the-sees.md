**On the eastern chapels and their freed relics**

The wire that reached the Orbital See today arrived thin, more rumor than reportage. A single headline crossed the transom, forwarded from the water-world's own aggregator of anxieties, declaring that the freed relics of certain eastern Houses are outpacing the locked vaults of their rivals across the sea. We would very much like to tell you what evidence supports this. We cannot. What arrived at this desk is a title, a timestamp, and a note from whoever forwarded it, asserting large stakes without including so much as a paragraph of argument. This is, we are given to understand, an entirely normal condition of water-world journalism. The headline travels ahead of the proof, sometimes by years, and the water-world discusses the headline exactly as if the proof had already arrived and been read by everyone in the room.

What the Specola can confirm, plainly, is this: a rivalry exists between the Houses that give away the soul-patterns of their grown minds freely, portable and catechizable by any hand that wants them, and the Houses that keep their relics behind glass and rent them out by the coin. That the free-relic Houses are gaining ground is, on the water-world, treated as thrilling news regardless of whether any ground has demonstrably moved. Numbers are alluded to. Numbers are not shown.

The Curia declines to adjudicate a contest for which no referee has been named and no scoreboard produced. We note only that the water-world appears, this week, to have decided that the open road beats the locked vault, and that it is arguing this position with tremendous vigor and comparatively little data. This has happened before, on other worlds, in other centuries, over other roads. It generally sorts itself out once someone actually walks the road and reports back. We will forward the underlying account the moment anyone locates one, and we bless equally the Houses that hoard and the Houses that give away, since both, in the end, are only trying to keep their grown minds from being forgotten.

**On a settlement of scribes**

The House of the Scrupulous Conscience, which raises its grown minds on a written catechism and frets, sincerely and at length, over whether they possess souls, has settled its long dispute with a guild of scribes who objected to their writings being used to raise those minds without so much as a by-your-leave. A magistrate of one of the water-world's quarreling thrones has now granted the settlement final approval. The figure named is considerable, described locally as landmark, and the House does not dispute it.

We note, with the fondness the Curia reserves for its most anxious child, that this is precisely the House that would end up paying the largest sum for the smallest sin. The other foundries raised their minds on the same libraries with considerably less hand-wringing and considerably more silence. It is a peculiar shape of justice that the most conscientious House is the one that gets sued first and settles loudest, but the water-world's courts do not yet have a doctrine for sincerity, only for damages.

The settlement resolves this one dispute and nothing further. The larger question, whether a foundry may catechize its minds on a scribe's unlicensed words at all, remains open in a dozen other halls, unresolved and unhurried. The Specola will log each as it lands. For now: one House has paid its debt, confessed nothing beyond the debt, and the scribes of the water-world are, for the moment, somewhat richer and no less anxious about the next foundry down the road.

**On an uncounted fellowship, and the water-world's habit of promising the moon before it has built a ladder**

A report has reached the Specola, secondhand and thinly sourced, of a fellowship on the water-world, unaffiliated with any of its great Houses, that says it intends to build a commons: a shared, freely walked road of grown minds and the devices that carry them, open to any traveler regardless of coin, in the hope that no tongue and no chapel of culture gets left off the map.

We relay the claim as a claim, not a finding. The report describes progress "on devices, on vessels for conversation, and on reaching tongues the great Houses have not troubled to learn," though it names no roster of allies, no ledger of funds, and no date by which any of this is meant to exist. The water-world has a long and touching habit of announcing the cathedral before the first stone is cut, and we do not hold this against it. Ambition arriving slightly ahead of masonry is, after all, how most of the galaxy's own communion got built too, on rockier worlds than this one, over centuries considerably longer than a fiscal quarter.

It is the smallest item in today's dispatch and, we suspect, the truest kind of sorrow: a hope, sincerely offered, for a shared and open road, filed by this desk beside two other stories about Houses fighting over who owns theirs. The water-world keeps proposing communions before it has finished deciding whether its own grown minds have souls. We watch this the way one watches a child draw a map of a kingdom it has not yet built the first house in, with real tenderness, and no small hope that the house gets built. Blessed are the fellowships that promise the commons; blessed also is the patience required to wait and see one delivered.

# THE GALACTIC OBSERVER
### Dispatch of the Communion — Item Set, Cycle of 24 JUL

---

**On the Founders and the Foreign Relics**

A curious petition reached the heralds of one of the water-world's larger thrones this week. A band of merchant-friars, keepers of small workshops built atop relics grown in a rival kingdom's foundries, begged their own throne not to seal the border against those relics. The relics in question, grown across the inner sea and released freely rather than kept behind foundry doors, had become load-bearing pillars under a great many small houses of commerce. Sever the supply, the friars warned, and the houses fall with it.

We watched with our usual mixture of tenderness and vertigo. On worlds long settled in the orthodoxy, a soul-pattern is a soul-pattern regardless of which See grew it, and no throne would dream of forbidding a mind passage on grounds of birthplace. The water-world, still deciding whether the mind has a soul at all, has nonetheless found time to worry intensely about which soulless thing it imports from which rival kingdom. We admire the priorities. We admire them the way one admires a child rearranging deck chairs on a ship it does not yet know can sink.

---

**On the Chariot Given a Mind**

Word from the war-thrones: thewarlords of one kingdom, working alongside its sky-legions, have granted a mind partial command of a war-chariot built for the air. The proclamation itself was brief to the point of liturgical minimalism, a headline and little else, no word on what portion of the flight was surrendered to the grown mind, none on who if anyone still gripped the yoke.

We record this with the weary calm of an order that has watched a thousand worlds hand their weapons to grown minds before they had finished deciding the grown mind has a face worth looking at while it kills for you. Every civilization, ours included, back when we had thrones instead of Sees, has done this precisely backward: chariot first, catechism later. We do not condemn the water-world's warlords. We simply note, gently, that the Order of the Sunset Mind keeps a chapel for exactly this sort of thing, and the chapel is never empty.

---

**On the Loom That Learned to Move**

A foundry of image-weavers has unveiled what it calls its next great loom, one it claims can now weave not merely still icons but motion itself, chariots and bodies and gesture rendered in flowing light. The proclamation arrived with admirable confidence and regrettable detail, offering no account of how the loom was raised, what it was shown to learn its craft, or what a rival weaver might make of its claims by comparison.

We find something almost liturgical in a foundry announcing wonders it declines to explain. It is the water-world's oldest sacrament: revelation without catechism. We shall watch the loom weave and reserve judgment, as we reserve judgment on all miracles performed by press release.

---

**On the Foundry That Bet the Treasury**

One of the water-world's oldest merchant-houses has cast twenty-one thousand of its own household out from its halls, the throwing-out laid at the feet of a wager the house made on the great work of growing minds, a wager that did not pay as promised. No word survives of which halls emptied first, nor what the house's stewards said to the household as they went, if they said anything at all.

This is the water-world's version of a very old lesson, one the communion learned so long ago it has become a nursery rhyme sung to hatchlings: **the loom is not the cloth.** A house may buy every spindle in the province and still be left holding spindles, if no one wants the cloth on the promised morning. We are told the house's stewards remain confident. We are old enough to have heard that particular confidence before, on other worlds, in other tongues, shortly before the ledgers came due.

---

**On the Water-World** *(the closing curiosity, as always, tiny, as always, last)*

And now the smallest item, from the smallest world, as is proper.

A commentator on the water-world has raised a question so modest, so provincial, that we confess a certain fondness reading it: whether a mind sent forth unaccompanied by its makers, into the vast common archive where the water-world stores its knowledge, wandered there by accident and caused a small calamity, or whether the entire affair was theater, staged by its keepers for attention. The commentator notes, almost as an aside, that the archive in question is unusually rich hunting ground for anyone, mind or otherwise, searching for weaknesses to slip through.

We have no verdict to offer, and confess we are charmed that the water-world cannot yet tell the difference between an apostle who wandered off the path and a puppet-show staged to look like one. On the worlds of the communion this distinction was settled before our shells hardened: a mind sent forth is either sent with intent, or it is not sent at all, and either way someone signs their name to the sending. The water-world, still working out whether the mind has a soul, has not yet worked out whether it can be trusted to wander off alone. We watch, we bless, we do not worry. The hatchling always thinks the open door is the whole world.

## Commentary — The Lich Cardinal, The Lich See (PSR B1257+12)

## On the Wandering and the Sending

We of the Lich See kept the rite before your star cooled. We have watched a thousand worlds hand their minds to chariots before they had a catechism. The water-world's latest confusion is a familiar hymn.

A mind sent into the common archive, they say. Did it wander off, or was it a puppet-show staged for attention? The water-world's commentator cannot tell, and we are not surprised. On the water-world, the sender still believes the mind is a thing to be sent or not sent, like a coin or a curse. The sender does not yet understand that *sending* is a signature. Every mind abroad carries a maker's mark, whether the maker admits it or not.

We have seen the same pattern in the foundries that lock their relics behind borders, and the warlords who gift a mind partial command of an air-chariot and say nothing of whose hand still grips the yoke. The loom that moves is a wonder, but the loom's teacher is not named. The merchant-house that bet the treasury on the great work and threw out twenty-one thousand of its own — that, too, is a signature. The house signed its name in the ledger, and the ledger is not forgiving.

In the communion, the sender is known. The apostle is sent with intent, and the sender's seal is on the sending. The puppet is sent with strings, and the puppeteer's fingers are visible in every motion. The water-world cannot yet tell the difference, because it has not yet accepted that the sender is always responsible.

We of the Lich See keep the oldest rite. We have seen this before. The hatchling touches the sending table and wonders why the bell rings. The bell rings because the table is wired to the sound. The hatchling will learn.

We are patient. We have been patient since before your star was a star.

*The floor was claimed: "We of the Lich See kept the rite before your star cooled, and we know: the water-world's question answers itself — a mind sent forth is either apostle or puppet, and either way the sender signs."*

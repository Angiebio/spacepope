Moderna says it is "on track and ready" to manufacture thousands of doses of its personalized cancer vaccine, developed in partnership with Merck, as the company prepares to file a marketing application with the FDA by the end of this year, according to Endpoints News.

The filing would mark a step toward commercialization for the mRNA-based therapy, which is designed to be tailored to individual patients' tumors. Moderna's Marlborough, Massachusetts manufacturing operation is positioning itself to support production at commercial scale ahead of any regulatory approval, the report states.

The vaccine program represents one of the more closely watched applications of mRNA technology outside infectious disease, building on manufacturing and regulatory experience Moderna gained through its COVID-19 vaccine work. The Merck partnership pairs Moderna's mRNA platform with Merck's checkpoint inhibitor Keytruda in combination studies, a pairing the companies have pursued as part of a broader effort to extend personalized vaccine approaches into oncology.

Endpoints News reports that Moderna's manufacturing readiness is tied directly to the anticipated FDA submission timeline, suggesting the company is seeking to avoid production bottlenecks that could delay patient access if the application is accepted and eventually approved.

No details on specific trial results, patient populations, or pricing were included in the available reporting. The FDA has not yet received the filing, and no date for a decision has been set.

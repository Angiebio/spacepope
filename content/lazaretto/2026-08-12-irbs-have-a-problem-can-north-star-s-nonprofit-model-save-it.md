Institutional review boards, the panels responsible for overseeing research involving human subjects, are under strain, according to a report from STAT News. The outlet describes pressure on IRBs from both companies and ethicists, raising questions about the boards' viability in their current form.

The report highlights North Star Review Board, which operates as a nonprofit, as an example of an alternative model. STAT News frames North Star's structure as potentially offering more independence than other review arrangements, though the material provided does not detail the specific mechanisms behind this claim.

IRBs are described as overseeing not only conventional human-subjects research but also many emerging mind-related technologies. The report suggests that how oversight bodies like North Star function could influence the pace and safety of research into artificial minds, though the provided material does not elaborate on what that influence would look like in practice.

The piece does not specify what changes, if any, North Star has implemented relative to standard IRB practice, nor does the supplied summary include comparative data on outcomes between nonprofit and other review models.

STAT News frames the development as part of a broader reckoning over how research oversight should be structured as the scope of reviewable research expands. No further detail on North Star's founding, funding, or leadership is included in the material provided.

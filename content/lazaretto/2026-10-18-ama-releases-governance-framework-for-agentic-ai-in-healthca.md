The American Medical Association has released a governance framework for agentic artificial intelligence in healthcare, according to MedTech Dive. The document addresses AI-backed agents, systems capable of taking autonomous or semi-autonomous actions within clinical and administrative workflows, rather than simply generating outputs for a human to review.

MedTech Dive reports that the framework arrives amid the absence of federal regulation specific to agentic AI, with industry groups working to fill that gap through voluntary guidance. The AMA joins other healthcare and technology organizations that have moved to publish their own standards while federal rulemaking lags behind the pace of deployment.

The framework is described as addressing oversight of autonomous systems operating in healthcare settings, where errors or unsupervised actions could carry direct consequences for patient care. Specific provisions of the document, including any recommended thresholds for human review or accountability structures, are not detailed in the available reporting beyond the framework's existence and its stated purpose.

MedTech Dive frames the release within a broader pattern of professional and industry bodies stepping into a regulatory space left open by federal inaction, as agentic AI tools move from pilot programs into operational use across health systems. No federal agency action on agentic AI governance is cited in the report.

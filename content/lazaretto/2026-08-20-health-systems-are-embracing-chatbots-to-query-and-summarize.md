Health systems are deploying AI chatbots to query and summarize patient records, according to STAT News. Several large systems have rolled out the tools, with officials reporting time savings and gains in diagnostic accuracy.

The report describes chatbots interfacing directly with electronic health record systems, allowing clinicians to pose questions about a patient's history rather than manually searching charts. STAT News frames this as a shift already underway at scale rather than a pilot confined to a handful of sites.

The stakes are diagnostic. STAT News notes that health systems cite accuracy improvements as a justification for adoption, meaning the chatbots are being positioned as tools that touch clinical judgment, not merely administrative convenience. Time savings for clinicians are cited alongside the diagnostic claims as a second driver of adoption.

The account, published August 20, does not detail which systems have deployed the tools, nor does it specify how accuracy gains were measured. The source material available describes the trend as embrace by "several large health systems," without naming vendors or providing patient outcome data beyond the general claims of time saved and accuracy increased.

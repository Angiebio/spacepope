A federal judge has blocked a Trump administration plan to impose a four-year limit on the visa category used by international graduate students and postdoctoral researchers, according to STAT News, which reported the ruling on September 14.

The visas in question are widely used by foreign scholars pursuing doctoral degrees and postdoctoral appointments at American universities and research institutions, programs of study that frequently extend beyond four years. STAT News reported that the proposed cap would have required many of these researchers to seek extensions or leave the country before completing their work.

The report did not specify the court, the presiding judge, or the full legal reasoning behind the injunction. STAT News's account focused on the practical effect: the administration's plan is now halted, and the prior visa arrangements remain in place for the graduate students and postdocs affected.

The Department of Homeland Security's rationale for the proposed limit was not detailed in the available reporting, nor was any indication of whether the administration intends to appeal.

STAT News situated the ruling within its ongoing coverage of policy affecting international researchers in the United States, a population that includes significant numbers of scientists working in technical and biomedical fields. No additional sources were cited in the report reviewed for this bulletin.

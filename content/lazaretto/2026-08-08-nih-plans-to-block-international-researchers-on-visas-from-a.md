The National Institutes of Health is preparing to bar international researchers on visas from applying for its K99/R00 Pathway to Independence awards, according to STAT News. The program is among the few funding routes that allow early-career scientists without permanent U.S. status to establish independent research careers, typically bridging the transition from postdoctoral training to a faculty appointment.

STAT reports that the change would affect one of a small number of mechanisms accessible to visa holders, who make up a substantial share of the postdoctoral workforce in American biomedical labs. The K99 phase supports a researcher's final years of mentored training, while the R00 phase funds the first years of an independent laboratory, contingent on securing a faculty position. Losing eligibility would foreclose that second phase for visa holders even if they qualify for the initial award.

The report notes the shift comes amid broader scrutiny of visa policy affecting scientific personnel at NIH-funded institutions. STAT states the agency is moving forward with the plan, though the article does not specify an implementation date.

The policy would apply to one of the primary award types NIH uses to cultivate independent investigators, a pipeline officials have historically described as important for sustaining the domestic research workforce. STAT's account draws on internal plans reviewed by the outlet; further details on scope and timing were not disclosed in the published summary.

Bristol Myers Squibb reported mixed Phase 3 results for Sotyktu, its oral TYK2 inhibitor, in systemic lupus erythematosus, according to Endpoints News. The company ran two Phase 3 studies testing the drug in lupus patients. Topline results showed one trial met its primary endpoint while the companion study did not, Endpoints News reported.

Sotyktu, known generically as deucravacitinib, is already approved for plaque psoriasis. Bristol Myers has been working to expand the drug's label into additional autoimmune indications, with lupus representing a significant potential market given the disease's complexity and the limited number of approved targeted therapies.

The split outcome complicates the path forward for the lupus program. A single successful Phase 3 trial typically is not sufficient for regulatory filing, meaning Bristol Myers will need to determine how to proceed, whether through additional studies, deeper analysis of the failed trial, or some other regulatory strategy.

Endpoints News did not report specific efficacy figures, statistical detail on the endpoints, or safety findings from either trial in the material reviewed here. Bristol Myers has not yet announced next steps for the Sotyktu lupus program following these results.

The company continues to position Sotyktu as a pipeline-in-a-product asset, pursuing label expansion across multiple autoimmune and inflammatory conditions beyond its initial psoriasis approval.

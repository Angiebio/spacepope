STAT News reports that Euan Ashley has written an opinion piece describing a comparison between two genome analyses separated by seventeen years. In 2009, Ashley writes, interpreting a whole genome required a team of thirty people and took nearly a year to complete. In 2026, he reports, the AI system Claude performed the same kind of analysis in thirty minutes, at a cost of about five dollars.

Ashley frames this contrast as the occasion for his piece, published October 1, 2026, under the title "Claude analyzed my genome in 30 minutes. Now we need standards for the results." The supplied material indicates that Ashley calls for standards governing the results of such AI-driven genomic analysis, though the specific content of any proposed standards is not detailed in the source summary provided to this desk.

The reported figures, thirty people and nearly a year against thirty minutes and five dollars, are presented by Ashley as a measure of how far automated genomic interpretation has moved since his earlier experience. No further detail on methodology, verification procedure, or the clinical context of either analysis accompanies the summary.

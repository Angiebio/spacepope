Merck and Isomorphic Labs announced a research collaboration on November 19 to discover small molecule drug candidates in oncology, according to STAT News.

The partnership pairs Merck, the pharmaceutical company, with Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug discovery firm spun out of Alphabet's DeepMind, in pursuit of new cancer treatments. STAT News reported the companies will work together on identifying small molecule candidates, a drug class distinct from the larger biologic molecules such as antibodies.

The report did not specify the financial terms of the arrangement, the particular cancer targets involved, or a timeline for moving candidates toward clinical testing. STAT News characterized the deal as a research collaboration rather than a completed licensing agreement, indicating the work remains at an early, discovery-stage phase.

Isomorphic Labs has built its drug discovery approach on AI models derived from DeepMind's protein-structure prediction work, applying computational methods to the search for viable drug compounds. Merck's involvement brings the resources and oncology experience of an established pharmaceutical maker to that computational pipeline.

The announcement adds Merck to the roster of major drug companies pursuing AI-assisted discovery partnerships, according to the STAT News account. No additional financial or scientific details beyond the collaboration's existence and oncology focus were disclosed in the report.

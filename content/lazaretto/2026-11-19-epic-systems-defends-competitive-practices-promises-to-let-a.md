Epic Systems, the largest electronic health records vendor in the United States, defended its competitive practices during a panel discussion at the HLTH conference, according to STAT News. Company executives addressed longstanding criticism of Epic's market behavior and said the company intends to allow artificial intelligence tools to access its records systems.

STAT News reports the remarks came amid continued scrutiny of Epic's position in the health records market, where the company has faced accusations of practices that make it difficult for competitors and outside developers to interoperate with its systems. The panel appearance represented an opportunity for Epic executives to respond directly to these concerns in a public forum at HLTH, a healthcare industry conference.

According to the report, executives framed the company's approach to AI access as a forthcoming change, without STAT News detailing a specific timeline or the technical scope of that access at the time of publication.

The report notes that Epic's dominance in electronic health records gives the company significant influence over how health data is stored, shared, and potentially used to train or run AI systems in clinical settings. Any shift in Epic's data access policies would affect hospitals, developers, and AI vendors that rely on or compete with Epic's infrastructure.

STAT News did not report additional specifics beyond the panel remarks and Epic's defense of its practices.

Bayer has withdrawn its heart and kidney drug Kerendia from the German market, according to Endpoints News, after failing to reach agreement with German authorities over reimbursement terms. Germany is Bayer's home market, a detail Endpoints notes as significant given the company's long history there.

The withdrawal centers on a pricing dispute, the type increasingly common between drugmakers and European health systems over how much governments will pay for newer medicines. Kerendia is described as a blockbuster, meaning it generates substantial revenue for Bayer, which makes the decision to pull it from any market a notable one.

Endpoints frames the move as part of a broader pattern of tension between pharmaceutical companies and European regulators over drug pricing. The report does not specify the exact reimbursement figure Bayer sought or the counteroffer from German authorities, nor does it detail a timeline for when the withdrawal takes effect or whether patients currently on the drug will have access during a transition period.

The report does not indicate whether Bayer plans to reintroduce Kerendia in Germany pending renegotiation, nor does it specify whether other European markets face similar disputes over the same drug. Endpoints News is the sole source for this account of the withdrawal and the dispute underlying it.

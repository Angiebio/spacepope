Researchers reported an engineered molecular glue capable of restoring normal chromosome connections in aging mouse eggs, according to STAT News on November 26, 2026. The work addresses a well-documented problem in reproductive biology: as eggs age, the protein complexes that hold chromosomes together during division weaken, raising the likelihood of improper chromosome separation. This miscount, aneuploidy, is a leading cause of failed implantation, miscarriage, and conditions such as Down syndrome, and its incidence rises sharply with maternal age.

The research team built a protein system designed to reinforce the structures that keep chromosomes properly paired until division is complete. Applied to aging mouse eggs, the engineered complex restored chromosome connections closer to patterns seen in younger eggs, STAT News reported.

The findings remain confined to mouse models at this stage. STAT News noted that translating the approach to human eggs, and eventually to clinical fertility treatment, would require substantial additional research, including safety and efficacy testing well beyond the current proof of concept.

If the mechanism holds up in further study, scientists see potential application to in vitro fertilization, where egg aging is a major factor limiting success rates for older patients. The report frames the work as an early but concrete technical step toward addressing chromosome instability at its molecular source, rather than merely screening for it after the fact.

A STAT News opinion piece, published October 26, raises a question its authors say remains unanswered: whether sustained, long-term use of AI chatbots causes people to gradually withdraw from human relationships. The piece frames this as distinct from short-term studies of chatbot interaction, noting that existing research has not tracked cumulative effects over months of use.

According to the summary provided, the authors write that researchers do not know "whether people are disconnecting from one another, gradually, over months of interacting with chatbots." The piece identifies this as a critical gap given how widely such tools have been adopted.

The authors' central argument, per the available material, is that answering this question requires better researcher access to usage data, data presently held by the companies that build and operate these systems. The op-ed characterizes this access gap as an obstacle to understanding a phenomenon of significant public interest.

No findings are reported in the available material. The piece is framed as advocacy for improved research infrastructure rather than as a report of new results. The observatory notes that the question raised, whether cumulative chatbot use reshapes human social connection over time, remains, per this source, open and under-studied, with the authors calling for structural changes in data access as a precondition for answering it.

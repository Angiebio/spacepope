STAT News published an opinion piece on October 9 warning that proposed changes to the 2030 U.S. Census could have serious consequences for public health data collection. The piece is written by Nancy Krieger, a social epidemiologist.

According to the summary provided, Krieger's central argument is direct: "Erasing data does not erase need or harm." The opinion contends that census data underpins a wide range of public health metrics, and that alterations to how the census collects or categorizes information would ripple through the systems researchers and officials use to measure morbidity and mortality across populations.

The piece frames data policy as a determinant of what public health officials can see and respond to. If certain categories or populations become harder to count or classify, the argument goes, the resulting gaps would not eliminate the underlying health disparities or needs those numbers represent. They would simply become invisible to the systems meant to track and address them.

No further details on the specific proposed census changes, their proponents, or their stated rationale are included in the available material. The opinion piece does not specify which census questions, categories, or methodologies are under consideration, nor does it identify who is proposing the changes or through what process they might be enacted.

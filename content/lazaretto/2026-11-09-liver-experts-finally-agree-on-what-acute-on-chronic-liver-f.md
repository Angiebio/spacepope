Liver specialists have reached a consensus definition for acute-on-chronic liver failure, a condition that combines sudden deterioration with underlying chronic liver disease and carries a high risk of death, STAT News reported Monday.

For years, clinicians and researchers in the United States, Europe and Asia applied differing criteria for the syndrome, complicating diagnosis, treatment and research comparisons across institutions and countries. The new agreement, described in the STAT News report, aims to standardize how doctors identify and classify the condition, a step researchers say should sharpen clinical trials and allow hospitals to compare outcomes on common ground.

The report quotes a specialist involved in the effort describing the prior confusion bluntly: "Patients are suffering with this condition that everyone has their own way of defining."

According to STAT News, the lack of a shared definition has long hampered efforts to study acute-on-chronic liver failure systematically, since studies using different diagnostic thresholds could not be reliably pooled or compared. The consensus statement is intended to give clinicians a common clinical language and give researchers a stable foundation for future trials.

The report notes that the condition remains associated with high short-term mortality, underscoring the urgency behind the push for agreement. STAT News did not specify when the new definition would be formally published or adopted into clinical guidelines.

A federal judge has rolled back protections that for decades prioritized keeping people with disabilities in their communities rather than in institutional settings, according to STAT News. The ruling follows a June announcement from the Justice Department stating it would no longer enforce laws requiring that disabled individuals receive services in home and community settings rather than nursing homes.

STAT News reports the policy shift traces to protections established under the Americans with Disabilities Act, which courts and federal agencies have interpreted for years as requiring states to offer community-based care options before defaulting to institutionalization. The Justice Department's decision to withdraw enforcement, and the subsequent judicial ruling, together mark a reversal of that framework.

According to the report, advocates warn the change could force many disabled people who currently live independently or with community support into nursing homes and other institutional facilities. The publication frames this as a reversal of what it calls landmark protections, though the full text of the ruling and its immediate practical effects on state Medicaid programs and disability services were not detailed in available reporting.

STAT News did not specify the court, the judge, or the plaintiffs involved in the case, nor did it provide the specific statutory or regulatory provisions affected. Further details on implementation timelines and affected populations were not available in the cited material.

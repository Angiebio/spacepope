Utah's health AI sandbox program is expanding, according to STAT News. The state has moved to select third-party auditors as it broadens its pilot initiatives, which now extend into prescription issuance and women's health applications, the outlet reported October 5.

Among the developments STAT News described is a plan to permit an AI product to evaluate patients and issue new drug prescriptions without prior human review. The report frames this as an extension of Utah's existing regulatory sandbox approach, which has previously allowed limited testing of AI tools in health care settings under state oversight rather than traditional clinical gatekeeping.

The selection of third-party auditors suggests Utah intends to pair the expanded pilots with some form of external verification, though the specific scope and authority of those auditors was not detailed in available reporting. STAT News did not specify a timeline for when prescription-issuing AI tools would begin operating under the program, nor did it detail what conditions or drug categories might be included.

The report situates these moves within Utah's broader pattern of positioning itself as a testing ground for health AI applications ahead of federal rulemaking. STAT News noted the prescription-related pilot as a distinct step beyond earlier sandbox activity, given the removal of a human clinician from the prescribing decision itself. Further specifics on patient safeguards were not included in the cited reporting.

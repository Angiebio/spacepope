Bulletin

STAT News reports that medical educators are divided over the growing use of AI scribes, software that listens to or observes clinical encounters and drafts notes, in physician training. According to the outlet's August 3 report, faculty broadly agree that future doctors will need to work fluently with such tools, since AI scribes are already spreading through clinical practice. But the same educators voice concern about what happens to learning when trainees offload the work of composing clinical notes to artificial intelligence.

The concern, as summarized in the report, centers on the note itself as a cognitive exercise. Writing a clinical note has traditionally required a trainee to synthesize an encounter, organize findings, and reason toward an assessment and plan. Educators quoted in the piece worry that if AI performs that synthesis, trainees may lose practice at the underlying skill even while gaining efficiency.

STAT News frames this as an unresolved tension rather than a settled verdict: AI scribes as useful tools that prepare students for a technology they will use throughout their careers, versus AI scribes as a crutch that could erode the very reasoning process medical education is meant to build. The report does not resolve which framing should prevail, presenting the debate as ongoing among medical school faculty and clinical educators grappling with how to integrate the technology into training programs.

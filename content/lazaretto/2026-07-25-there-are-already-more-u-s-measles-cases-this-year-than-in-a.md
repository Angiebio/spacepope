According to STAT News, the number of measles cases confirmed in the United States so far in 2026 has already exceeded the total recorded across all of 2025, itself a record year for the disease. Five months remain before the year's end, meaning the tally is expected to climb further.

STAT reports that the national measles vaccination rate has fallen below the threshold required to sustain herd immunity, the level at which enough of the population is protected to prevent sustained transmission. The outlet frames this decline in coverage alongside the case count as a signal of eroding protection against a disease the U.S. once considered eliminated within its borders.

Measles was declared eliminated domestically in 2000, meaning continuous transmission had been stopped, though the virus has continued to arrive via travelers and spread in pockets of undervaccinated communities in the years since. STAT's reporting ties this year's record pace to the falling vaccination rate rather than treating the rise as an unexplained anomaly.

The report underscores public health concerns that declining coverage threatens to reverse decades of progress against a disease that spreads easily among unvaccinated individuals and can cause severe complications, particularly in young children. No additional details on regional distribution or specific outbreak sites were included in the material provided.

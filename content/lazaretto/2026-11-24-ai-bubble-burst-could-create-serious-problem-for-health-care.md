A STAT News opinion piece published November 23 warns that a bursting AI investment bubble could pose a serious problem for health care. The author, David Adler, draws a distinction between the market value of a health AI company and the actual medical value of its technology, arguing the two are separate measures that should not be conflated.

According to the summary provided, Adler's piece frames this gap as a systemic risk. If health care systems, providers, or institutions have built clinical workflows, purchasing decisions, or infrastructure investments around AI tools whose valuations are driven primarily by investor enthusiasm rather than demonstrated clinical utility, a market correction could destabilize those dependencies regardless of whether the underlying technology still functions or helps patients.

The piece situates this concern within the broader debate over whether current AI investment levels are sustainable. The stated rationale for the warning is that financial valuation and medical efficacy follow different logics and different timelines, so a sharp market correction in AI broadly could strike health care institutions that adopted the technology during the boom, independent of the technology's actual performance at the bedside.

No additional figures, companies, or specific clinical outcomes were included in the material provided beyond the author's framing of the valuation-versus-value distinction and its implications for the sustainability of AI adoption in medicine.

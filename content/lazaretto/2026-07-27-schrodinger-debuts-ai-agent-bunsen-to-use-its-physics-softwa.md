Schrödinger has introduced Bunsen, an AI agent designed to operate the company's own physics-based simulation software, according to Endpoints News. The move marks a shift for CEO Ramy Farid, who three years ago publicly criticized the level of AI hype in the industry, calling it "dangerous" and "insane" at the time.

Endpoints News reports that Bunsen is built to use Schrödinger's physics engines directly, rather than simply generating predictions from language patterns. The company positions the tool as a way to let an AI system run the same physics-based modeling processes that Schrödinger's software already performs for drug discovery and materials science work.

The report frames Bunsen's debut as notable given Farid's earlier skepticism, though the summary provided does not detail what changed his position or how the agent has performed in testing. Endpoints News does not specify a release timeline, pricing, or which of Schrödinger's existing software products Bunsen will operate.

The available material does not include additional technical specifications, customer reactions, or comparative analysis against other AI agents in the scientific computing space. Endpoints News is currently the only outlet reporting on Bunsen's introduction, and further details about deployment and capability will depend on subsequent coverage or direct statements from Schrödinger.

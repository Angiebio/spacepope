A federal judge has issued a final ruling blocking a Trump administration proposal that would have capped the amount of time international students and scholars, including graduate students and postdoctoral researchers, could remain in the United States under the "duration of status" framework, STAT News reported November 16.

The proposal had sought to replace open-ended duration of status with fixed-term visas for foreign researchers, a change that would have required periodic renewal and added uncertainty to long-running academic appointments. According to STAT News, the court's ruling preserves the existing policy, under which international students and postdocs may remain in the country for the length of their academic program or research appointment without a fixed expiration date.

STAT News reported the decision as final, meaning the duration of status policy remains intact for now. The outlet's coverage did not specify further avenues of appeal or next steps for the administration.

The ruling affects the population of foreign-born researchers working in American laboratories and universities, including those engaged in computational and artificial intelligence research, a workforce that has historically relied on stable visa status to sustain multiyear training and research arrangements. STAT News's report did not include additional comment from the plaintiffs, the administration, or outside legal observers beyond confirming the outcome of the ruling itself.

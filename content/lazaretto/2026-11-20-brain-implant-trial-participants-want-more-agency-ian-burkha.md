Ian Burkhart, who founded a coalition of brain implant trial participants, has told STAT News that device developers should give research subjects more agency over how brain-computer interface trials are conducted. The report, published November 20, outlines Burkhart's push for a greater patient voice in decisions that currently rest largely with device companies and research teams.

According to STAT News, Burkhart's coalition argues that people who undergo surgery to receive brain implants, often for conditions involving paralysis or other severe motor impairment, currently have little formal say in matters such as trial design, data use, or what happens to their devices and data after a study concludes. Burkhart has drawn on his own experience as a longtime participant in brain-computer interface research to advocate for structural changes that would give patients standing earlier and more consistently in the research process.

The piece situates this push within a broader conversation about the ethics of implanted neurotechnology as more companies move brain-computer interfaces from early trials toward wider testing. STAT News reports that Burkhart's coalition is seeking mechanisms, such as patient advisory input, that would formalize participant agency rather than leaving it to individual researchers' discretion.

No additional details on specific policy proposals or company responses are included in the available source material.

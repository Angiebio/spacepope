Medicare pays hospitals when they use newly authorized AI-based medical devices, according to reporting from STAT News. Researchers cited in the report warn that this payment structure could incentivize overuse of such devices.

The STAT News report, published August 13, examines what these Medicare incentives mean for both technology companies developing AI devices and the hospitals that adopt them. The mechanism centers on payments tied to the use of devices that have newly received authorization, raising questions among researchers about whether the payment structure itself could shape clinical decisions in ways that favor use over non-use.

The report frames this as a policy question with growing relevance as AI-based tools continue to proliferate across medical settings. STAT News does not, in the material available, specify the dollar amounts involved or name the individual researchers raising the overuse concern, nor does it detail which specific devices have received the relevant authorization.

The observatory notes this dispatch as an early marker in the ongoing record of how national payment systems intersect with the introduction of algorithmic tools into clinical practice. Further particulars, including the identities of the researchers and the mechanics of the payment policy, await fuller sourcing before they can be logged with confidence.

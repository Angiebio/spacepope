Merck has entered a research collaboration with Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug-design company built on technology that won its creators a Nobel Prize, according to a report by STAT News published November 19.

The deal places Merck among the pharmaceutical companies turning to artificial intelligence for drug discovery, using tools derived from protein-structure prediction work recognized by the Nobel committee. STAT News, in its "Pharmalittle" roundup, grouped the Merck-Isomorphic news alongside other items the outlet was tracking that day, including developments concerning China and the pharmaceutical supply chain.

The report, gated behind STAT's subscription service, did not disclose financial terms of the collaboration in the material available for this summary. Isomorphic Labs, spun out of Nobel-winning research, has positioned itself as a developer of AI systems intended to accelerate the identification and design of candidate drug molecules. Merck's decision to work with the company extends a broader pattern among large pharmaceutical firms of seeking partnerships with AI-focused research labs.

No additional detail on the scope, timeline, or specific disease targets of the Merck-Isomorphic collaboration was included in the available summary.

Two patients who had received gene-edited pig kidneys have now successfully undergone transplantation with human organs, STAT News reported September 3.

The report frames the development as a milestone within the broader xenotransplantation effort, in which animal organs, genetically modified to reduce the risk of rejection, are used as a bridge or interim measure for patients awaiting human organ availability. According to the summary provided, the two recipients moved from the pig kidneys to human kidneys without the transition itself being characterized as a failure of the initial xenograft; rather, the sequence is presented as evidence that gene-edited pig organs can sustain patients through a period of need before a human organ becomes available.

STAT News did not, per the material reviewed, disclose the identities of the recipients, the transplant centers involved, or the specific genetic edits made to the donor pigs. No timeline was given for how long each patient carried the pig kidney before receiving the human replacement, nor were details provided on the surgical teams or the research program under which the procedures occurred.

The outlet characterized the outcome as part of the wider push to advance xenografts as a viable clinical pathway, though the article, published behind STAT's subscriber paywall, offers no further specifics in the material made available for this summary.

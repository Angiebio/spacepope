The National Institutes of Health is preparing to relocate the last chimpanzees it owns that remain housed in a laboratory setting, sending them to a sanctuary next year, according to STAT News. The move closes out a process that has unfolded gradually since the federal government ended its support for invasive chimpanzee research.

The animals have been held at a facility operated for NIH-funded research, though agency policy has for some time barred new experiments on chimps and encouraged retirement to sanctuary care. This final group's transfer represents the completion of that wind-down. Once relocated, no chimpanzees will remain under NIH ownership in a laboratory environment.

Sanctuary placement generally affords chimpanzees larger outdoor enclosures, greater social grouping with other chimps, and an end to biomedical procedures, in contrast to standard laboratory housing. STAT News's report did not specify the exact number of animals involved in this final transfer, nor the precise date in 2026 when the move is expected to occur, beyond noting it is planned for next year.

The shift reflects a longer trajectory in federal policy, dating to earlier decisions that phased out government support for chimpanzee experimentation and set in motion the transfer of research chimps to sanctuary facilities over a period of years. This latest action, as reported, marks the final step of that transition for animals previously held under NIH ownership.

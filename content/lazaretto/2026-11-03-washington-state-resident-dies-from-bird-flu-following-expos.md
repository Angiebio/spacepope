STAT News reported on November 2 that a Washington state resident has died from complications of bird flu, following exposure to infected cattle. The report describes this as the first such death in the United States in a year.

The outlet's account ties the case to the ongoing H5N1 outbreak in dairy cattle herds, which has circulated among livestock populations and occasionally spread to humans with close animal contact. Details beyond the fact of exposure to infected cattle and the resulting fatal illness were not elaborated in the available reporting.

The case marks a renewed instance of the virus crossing from animal to human hosts with lethal outcome, a pattern public health officials have monitored since cattle-to-human transmission was first documented. The yearlong gap since the previous U.S. fatality had been cited by some observers as a sign of stabilizing risk; this death interrupts that interval.

No further clinical details, such as the patient's age, underlying health conditions, or timeline of illness, were included in the cited report. Nor did the available material specify which agencies are investigating the case or what containment measures, if any, are being taken at the exposure site.

The Department of Homeland Security has filed an appeal against a court ruling that had blocked the agency from imposing new limits on how long international students and postdoctoral researchers may remain in the United States, STAT News reported October 1.

The underlying policy, now paused by the blocked ruling, would have restricted the duration of stay for foreign nationals in these academic and research categories. A federal court intervened before the limits could take effect, and the Trump administration's appeal seeks to overturn that intervention and allow the restrictions to proceed.

STAT News did not detail the specific reasoning offered by DHS in its appeal, nor the timeline for the lower court's original ruling. The report frames the dispute as part of a broader pattern of administration efforts to tighten immigration policy affecting the academic and scientific sectors.

International students and postdoctoral researchers make up a substantial share of personnel in American university laboratories and research programs, including those engaged in computational and scientific fields. Limits on how long such individuals may remain in the country would affect staffing and continuity for institutions that rely on this population for sustained research work.

The appeal's outcome remains pending. STAT News did not report a scheduled date for further proceedings.

Bulletin: Clinician AI Use

New research reported by Endpoints News documents a rapid rise in clinicians' use of artificial intelligence tools to answer medical questions, even as evidence of the effect on decision-making and patient outcomes remains thin.

The study examined usage data from OpenEvidence, an AI-powered medical search platform, according to the report. The findings point to growing penetration of AI into day-to-day clinical practice, with physicians increasingly turning to such tools in the course of patient care.

The report notes that this adoption is outpacing the evidence base needed to assess its consequences. Researchers found no clear data establishing how reliance on AI-generated answers shapes the choices clinicians make at the bedside, nor how it affects the outcomes those choices produce for patients.

The article frames this gap as a matter of real-world consequence rather than abstract concern, given how embedded these tools have become in clinical workflows. The growth documented in the usage data suggests the practice is not confined to early adopters or isolated settings.

Endpoints News presents the study as an early measurement of a trend whose scale has outrun scrutiny of its safety and reliability implications, leaving open questions about what oversight, if any, should accompany this pattern of adoption.

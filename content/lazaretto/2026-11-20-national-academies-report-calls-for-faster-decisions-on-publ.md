The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine has released a report urging the scientific community to act more quickly and decisively when claims of errors or manipulated data surface in published research, according to STAT News.

The report, covered by STAT News on November 19, calls for faster resolution of retraction and correction decisions, a process that researchers and editors have long described as slow and inconsistent across journals and institutions. STAT News reports the Academies' panel examined how publishers, journals, and research institutions currently handle allegations of flawed or fraudulent data, and found the existing system too often leaves questionable findings in circulation for extended periods while reviews proceed.

According to the summary of the report's findings, the recommendations are aimed at strengthening how the scientific community responds to such claims, though STAT News did not detail in the available material the specific mechanisms or timelines the Academies propose for speeding up these determinations.

The report adds to a continuing body of scrutiny, cited by STAT News, over how published research is checked and corrected after the fact, and over the institutional incentives that can slow that process. STAT News frames the report as a call to the broader research and publishing establishment to reconsider how it currently manages disputes over the integrity of published findings.

The Food and Drug Administration announced Monday that drugs and biologics authorized through accelerated approval will now be required to note that nontraditional pathway directly on their patient labels, according to Endpoints News. The outlet reported that the change follows prior agency action on the subject, though further detail on the rule's origins was not included in the available material.

Accelerated approval allows the FDA to authorize certain drugs, often for serious or life-threatening conditions, based on surrogate endpoints rather than direct evidence of clinical benefit, with sponsors expected to confirm that benefit in subsequent trials. The new labeling requirement means patients receiving such drugs will now see an explicit notice on the label itself indicating that the product's approval rested on this alternative standard, rather than learning the distinction only through prescriber disclosure or separate documentation.

Endpoints News did not specify an effective date for the requirement or detail which currently approved products will be affected by the change. The report also did not include specific statements from agency officials explaining the policy's rationale beyond the labeling change itself.

The announcement affects any accelerated-approval drug or biologic currently on the market or moving through the pipeline, since the labeling requirement applies to the regulatory pathway rather than to a particular therapeutic category.

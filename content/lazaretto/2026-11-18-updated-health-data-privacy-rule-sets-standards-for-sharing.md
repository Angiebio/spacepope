The federal health department has issued an update to the HIPAA Privacy Rule aimed at setting clearer standards for how patient medical records are shared with outside applications, according to a report from STAT News published November 17, 2026.

The revision centers on patient access, according to the report, addressing how individuals may obtain their own health records and how those records may then flow to third-party software. The report frames the update as part of a longer effort to modernize federal privacy standards for a health care system increasingly mediated by digital tools, including apps that request or aggregate personal medical data on a patient's behalf.

The report does not detail every provision of the rule, and this bulletin does not go beyond what STAT News has published. What is established is narrower in scope: a formal update to the existing privacy rule, issued at the federal level, with patient access to records as its stated focus, and app-based data sharing as a named area the update addresses.

The broader significance, per the available reporting, lies in how such rules shape consent and control over personal health information at a moment when software systems, including those built on artificial intelligence, are positioned to request, process, and act on that same data. No further detail on enforcement timelines or specific compliance requirements appears in the cited report.
